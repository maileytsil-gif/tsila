# Conflict Resolution

When specialist skills disagree, resolve the conflict by identifying the **musical invariant** and the **resource collision**.

## Priority order

1. explicit user constraint
2. identity / hook
3. groove
4. low-end ownership
5. intelligibility
6. sound-design complexity
7. processing preference
8. loudness

## Typical conflicts

### Long 808 kick vs sub bass
If `BASS OWNS SUB`, shorten or filter the kick tail, use alternating ownership, or redesign the note pattern. Do not solve a temporal collision only with EQ.

### Wide bass vs mono compatibility
Keep low fundamentals stable/mono and move width to upper harmonics, parallel layers or higher bands.

### Big reverb vs punch
Separate dry attack from wet tail, duck the return, or reduce send around the transient.

### Complex harmony vs strong bass hook
Keep the harmonic skeleton simple if the bass is already carrying melodic information.

### Producer reference vs originality
Keep the abstract vector (e.g. negative space) and change notes, rhythm, timbre, phrase lengths and form so the result is not a recognizable derivative.

### Master loudness vs transient quality
Protect the musical transient first; reduce upstream density/clipper drive before forcing more limiter gain.
