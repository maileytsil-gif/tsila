# Voicing engine — Pop & Electronic

[DOC] Berklee's contemporary voice-leading guidance emphasizes keeping common tones and moving remaining voices economically. Use this as a default, not a law.

## Archetypes

### 1. Close triad
Three chord tones within one octave. Best for keys, plucks and compact stabs.

### 2. Independent bass + close upper triad
Bass owns the root/register; upper voices can use inversion. Excellent for Pop, Future House and Dance Pop because the chord does not need to duplicate low root energy.

### 3. Open fifth / no third
Root + fifth or spread fifths. Use when the sound itself is harmonically dense or when major/minor identity should remain ambiguous.

### 4. Add9 / sus2 color
Add a nearby scale tone when it supports the melody. [HEUR] In dense drops, omit an unnecessary fifth before adding more notes.

### 5. Pedal voicing
Keep one note fixed while upper/lower voices change. Useful for tension and continuity.

### 6. House stab inversion
Keep the top note or one/two common tones stable while roots change underneath. Short articulation makes inversion choice audible as *shape*, not classical harmony.

## Decision rule
Choose the voicing that best satisfies, in order:
1. melody/top-note intention;
2. smoothness or deliberate jump;
3. bass ownership;
4. register separation;
5. sound-design density.

Do not widen chords merely to make them 'big'. If a wide voicing masks the vocal/lead, reduce it.
