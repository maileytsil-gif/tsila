# Electronic mastering engine

## Goal
Produce translation, impact and controlled loudness; do not chase a genre LUFS number blindly.

## Pre-master gate
Do not master around unresolved kick/bass conflict, harsh resonances, clipping on individual channels or unstable stereo low-end. Fix the mix first where practical.

## Candidate chain (modular, not mandatory)
1. Pro-Q4/F6: very small tonal correction if needed.
2. soothe3: only if moving global harshness remains.
3. Optional color/glue: Waves/Analog Obsession/API/SSL/J37 etc.
4. Peak shaving: RazorClip or L4 clipping stage if it improves punch.
5. Waves L4: final limiting / true peak / loudness.
6. WLM/PAZ and reference checks.

A strong mix may need only L4, or Pro-Q4 -> RazorClip -> L4.

## Waves L4 role
Compare modes at equal loudness rather than assigning a mode solely from genre. Starting hypotheses: Modern = balanced/punchy; Aggressive = dense/transient-forward; Smooth = rounder; Safe = conservative; L2 mode = classic behavior. Treat these as audition points, not rules.

## Club vs streaming
- Club/DJ: protect kick/sub physical impact and mixability. Loudness that destroys crest/transients can sound smaller on a big system.
- Streaming: normalization does not require mastering to a fixed -14 LUFS. Choose loudness where the master still sounds better at equal playback level. Conservative true-peak margin may reduce codec overs.

## QC
Check integrated and short-term loudness, true peak, crest/dynamics, low-end spectrum, correlation/mono, section-to-section consistency, fades, head/tail, sample rate/bit depth and export format. Measurements support decisions; they do not replace listening.
