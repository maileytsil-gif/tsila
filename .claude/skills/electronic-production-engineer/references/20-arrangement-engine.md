# Arrangement engine: breaks, bridges, builds and drops

## Energy model
Represent each section by 5 axes from 0-5: `low_end`, `drum_density`, `harmonic_density`, `space`, `tension`. A useful transition changes at least one axis clearly while preserving continuity in another.

## Section functions
- **Breakdown**: remove physical energy to expose identity, emotion or anticipation. Decide what pulse cue remains.
- **Bridge**: connect two energy states; often 1-8 bars. Use fills, bass cuts, filter moves, vocal throws, alternate percussion or a deliberate void.
- **Build**: increase expectation through density, rate, pitch, filter, register, reverb, automation or rhythmic subdivision. Addition is optional; substitution can be cleaner.
- **Pre-drop gap**: 1/4 beat to 1 bar of reduced information. Use only when genre-appropriate.
- **Drop**: restore or transform the core promise of the track. The type of payoff is genre-dependent.

## Genre-specific drop payoffs
- House: return of groove.
- Tech House: kick+bass + hook after tension/gap.
- Minimal: familiar groove perceived as fresh after deprivation.
- Techno: return of full physical/timbral density.
- Bass House: contrast + call/response sound-design event.
- Melodic: emotional motif plus rhythmic foundation/climax.
- Afro House: full polyrhythmic network + low-end return.

## 4/8/16/32-bar discipline
Use phrase lengths as an organizing grid, not a prison. Ask every 4-8 bars whether expectation changes. Major section changes commonly live on 16/32-bar boundaries, but asymmetry is allowed when it improves momentum.

## Second-drop rule
Do not merely duplicate. Change one or two high-value dimensions: bass response, percussion layer, octave/register, vocal response, rhythm, fill language, modulation depth, spatial size or transient density.

## DJ-friendly design
When intended for DJs, preserve mixable intro/outro regions and avoid hiding the first clear downbeat. Keep enough stable rhythm for beatmatching before major thematic content arrives.

## Verified structures (Harmonix Set) [DOC]
`chorus` = sung chorus, `inst` = instrumental drop.
- Guetta pop-EDM formula, Titanium (126 BPM): intro 4, 2 × (verse 16, chorus 16, inst 8), bridge 8, chorus 8, inst 8 — sung chorus 16 (last one 8) → instrumental drop 8, three times.
- Radio → extended keeps inner 16-bar blocks: Take Over Control 84 → 200 bars (intro 4 → 16, outro 8 → 48); Memories extended adds a 20-bar intro and a 16-bar post-chorus per chorus.
- Club: Sofi Needs a Ladder (128), intro 17, ten 16-bar sections, outro 15.

## Block-of-8 rules [COMM]
- Boundaries on multiples of 8 from bar 1; change something every 8, something big every 16/32 (techno: 16/32 only).
- Subtraction test: every 8-bar block drops one earlier element.
- Drop: 2 bars call, 2 response, escalate in bars 13–16; end bar 16/32 on a fill or cut.
- Build 8, or 16 before a main festival/bass-house drop; tech house breakdown + build ≈ 8.
- Gap: 1 beat standard, 2 dramatic, 1 bar very dramatic; two true silences per track at most (kick removal 4–8 bars early is not a gap).
- Breakdown 6–10 LU under the drop; peak at 60–75 % of duration.

## Radio vs extended
Radio 2:30–3:30: intro 4–8, no initial build, first drop by 0:45–1:00 [DOC; HEUR]; at 128 BPM, intro 4 + theme 8 + build 8 = 20 bars = 37.5 s [CALC]. Extended: DJ intro 16–32, outro 16–48, no full-spectrum content [DOC; COMM].
