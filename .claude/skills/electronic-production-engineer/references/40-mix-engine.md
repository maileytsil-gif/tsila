# Mix engine

## Mix sequence
1. static balance/gain staging,
2. kick/bass relationship,
3. drum hierarchy and transients,
4. masking/tonal balance,
5. spatial/spectral plan and depth/sends,
6. stereo/mono stability,
7. buses/color,
8. automation,
9. reference comparison,
10. pre-master readiness.

## EQ
Use Pro-Q4/F6 for precision/dynamics. Static EQ handles stable problems; dynamic tools handle level-dependent conflicts. Avoid carving every track simply to make analyzer plots look separated.

## soothe3
Best for moving resonances, harshness and spectral sidechain. Use Delta/removed-signal listening to confirm it is not eating musical identity. On master, be especially conservative.

## Compression
Choose by goal: level control, transient shape, groove/pumping, glue or color. API 2500/SSL/TheBus are bus options; dbx 160/CLA-76 can be punch/color choices; Pro-C3/Renaissance can be more controlled. If the compressor makes the kick smaller at equal loudness, question whether it belongs.

## Saturation / clipping
Saturation adds harmonics/color; clipping limits peaks and may also color. They are related but not interchangeable. Use Roar/Waves/Analog Obsession for color; RazorClip for deliberate peak shaving; then re-check aliasing/harshness and transient quality.

## Stereo and spatial planning
Read `41-spatial-spectrum-stereo-engine.md` for element-by-element placement. Keep center authority. Distinguish panning from width, and depth from reverb amount. Spread supporting percussion, textures, reverbs and upper harmonics as appropriate. Mid/Side is a diagnostic/processing domain, not a mandatory widening trick. Protect low-frequency mono stability and verify phase/correlation after widening.

## Space
Use shared returns for coherent depth. High-pass/shape reverb low end as needed. Hybrid Reverb/Valhalla/Waves can cover rooms, plates, long atmospheres and creative delays.
