# Sources v11 — progression, melody, voicing & Ableton MIDI generation

## Ableton — documentation officielle [DOC]

- Ableton Live 12 Manual — MIDI Tools: https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/midi-tools/
  - Transformations operate on existing MIDI notes; Generators create new material.
  - Pitch-related parameters become scale-degree aware when a clip scale is active.
  - Native tools include Arpeggiate, Connect, Quantize, Recombine, Span, Strum, Time Warp; Generators include Rhythm, Seed, Shape and Stacks.
- Ableton Live Concepts — Scale Awareness: https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/live-concepts/
- Ableton Clip View: https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/clip-view/
- Ableton Making Music — Catalog of Attributes: https://makingmusic.ableton.com/catalog-of-attributes

## Berklee — théorie/songwriting [DOC]

- Voice Leading Paradigms: https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/voice-leading-paradigms-for-harmony-in-music-composition/
  - Common tones, stepwise movement and inversions can reduce unnecessary jumps.
- Harmonic Progressions for Songwriters: https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/harmonic-progressions-for-songwriters/
  - Simple progressions can support different sections; harmonic rhythm and one changed chord can create contrast.
- Writing Melodies: 3 Simple Tools: https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/simple-tools-for-better-melodies/
  - Motif, repetition and contrast are controllable melody-design variables.
- How to Write Killer Song Hooks: https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/how-to-write-songs-with-killer-hooks/
  - Hooks benefit from coherent melody/harmony/message, contrast, position and repetition.

## Status of the library

The exact progressions, voicings, motifs and MIDI clips in v11 are **original teaching templates** generated for this skill. They are [HEUR], not transcriptions of commercial songs. Chord symbols themselves are general musical vocabulary. Use them as seeds, then change rhythm, register, voicing, melody, sound and form.
