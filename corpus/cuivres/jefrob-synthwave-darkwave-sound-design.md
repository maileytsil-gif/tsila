# Sound Design Palette

## Synthwave

### Signature Sounds

#### Arpeggio Synth (Primary Identity)
- **Character**: Bright, pulsing, 16th-note cascading patterns — the genre's defining sound
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw wave with slight detuning (2–3 voices), fast-attack amplitude envelope, short release
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 4 kHz (covers mid to upper-mid)
- **Processing**: Chorus (BBD-style), short delay (16th or dotted 8th), sidechain to kick for pump
- **Role in mix**: Central rhythmic and harmonic element — drives the track forward
- **Classic sources**: Juno-106, Jupiter-8, OB-Xa, modern: TAL-U-NO-LX, Diva, Repro

#### Lead Synth
- **Character**: Bright, slightly detuned, singing quality — like an 80s electric guitar solo
- **Synthesis approach**: 2–3 saw oscillators detuned, low-pass filter with moderate resonance, portamento/glide
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 6 kHz
- **Processing**: Chorus, medium reverb (plate or hall), delay (dotted 8th)
- **Role in mix**: Melodic focal point in choruses and solo sections
- **Classic sources**: Minimoog, Prophet-5, CS-80, modern: Arturia Mini V, Omnisphere

#### Pads
- **Character**: Lush, warm, evolving — pillowy bed of harmony
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw/pulse waves, slow attack, long release, chorus/ensemble effect
- **Frequency range**: 150 Hz – 3 kHz (wide, warm)
- **Processing**: Heavy chorus (Juno-style), long reverb (4–6s), subtle filter movement
- **Role in mix**: Background harmonic bed — fills space without drawing attention
- **Classic sources**: Roland Juno-60/106 (chorus patches), JP-8000, modern: TAL-U-NO-LX

#### Bass
- **Character**: Punchy, mono, defined — octave-jumping patterns
- **Synthesis approach**: Single saw or square oscillator, low-pass filter, short decay envelope
- **Frequency range**: 40–200 Hz fundamental, character up to 500 Hz
- **Processing**: Light saturation, sidechain to kick, mono below 120 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Low-end foundation, often plays octave patterns
- **Classic sources**: Minimoog, SH-101, Moog Sub 37

#### Drums (Gated Reverb Snare)
- **Character**: Big, explosive, 80s — Phil Collins/Peter Gabriel era
- **Synthesis approach**: Acoustic snare sample + gated reverb (short gate time, ~200ms)
- **Frequency range**: 150 Hz – 8 kHz (wide, present)
- **Processing**: Gated reverb (essential), compression, slight EQ boost at 3–5 kHz
- **Role in mix**: Defining rhythmic character — the "80s" sound
- **Classic sources**: LinnDrum, Simmons SDS-V, Oberheim DMX

#### Electric Guitar (Optional)
- **Character**: Clean chorus or 80s power-chord crunch
- **Processing**: Chorus (CE-1/CE-2 style), spring or hall reverb, light overdrive
- **Role in mix**: Solo sections, chorus reinforcement, textural layer
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 6 kHz

## Retrowave

### Signature Sounds

#### Saxophone
- **Character**: Smooth, breathy, expressive — Kenny G meets Top Gun soundtrack
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based (real sax recordings) or physical modeling
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 4 kHz
- **Processing**: Reverb (plate), slight compression, presence EQ at 2–4 kHz
- **Role in mix**: Solo instrument, melodic hook, defines the "retro" quality
- **Classic sources**: Real saxophone recordings, Sample Modeling, SWAM

#### Warm Pads
- **Character**: Lush, wide, 80s — warmer and brighter than darkwave pads
- **Synthesis approach**: Detuned saws with slow LFO on filter, long attack/release
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 4 kHz
- **Processing**: Heavy chorus, long reverb, stereo widening
- **Role in mix**: Emotional bed — supports vocals and melody
- **Classic sources**: Juno-106 "pad" patches, DX7 electric piano, OB-Xa

#### Vocals
- **Character**: Smooth, 80s-production-style, reverb-drenched
- **Processing**: Plate reverb, slapback delay, light chorus, de-essing, compression (4–6 dB GR)
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 8 kHz
- **Role in mix**: Primary focal element — front and center
- **Techniques**: Double-tracking, whispered layers, backing harmonies (3rds and 5ths)

#### Bass
- **Character**: Round, warm, slightly funky — supports but doesn't dominate
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw/square with low-pass filter, moderate decay
- **Frequency range**: 40–300 Hz
- **Processing**: Light saturation, compression, sidechain to kick
- **Role in mix**: Groove foundation — locks with kick drum

#### Drums
- **Character**: Less extreme gated reverb than synthwave — more natural 80s pop
- **Synthesis approach**: LinnDrum/DMX samples, lighter gated reverb
- **Processing**: Room reverb (shorter than synthwave), moderate compression
- **Role in mix**: Supportive groove — less prominent than in synthwave instrumentals
- **Classic sources**: LinnDrum, DMX, acoustic kit with 80s processing

## Outrun

### Signature Sounds

#### Aggressive Arpeggio
- **Character**: Fast, driving, distorted — heavier than synthwave arps
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw waves (2–3 detuned), aggressive filter envelope, fast 16ths
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 5 kHz
- **Processing**: Overdrive/saturation, short delay, compression, stereo spread
- **Role in mix**: Primary driving force — relentless energy
- **Classic sources**: Prophet-5, Moog, modern: Serum, Diva (with drive)

#### Power Lead
- **Character**: Screaming, distorted, guitar-like — hero synth
- **Synthesis approach**: Multiple detuned saws/squares, resonant filter, portamento
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 8 kHz
- **Processing**: Distortion, long reverb, delay, compression
- **Role in mix**: Lead melody in peak sections, solo instrument
- **Classic sources**: Minimoog, CS-80, modern: Monark, Diva

#### Sub Bass
- **Character**: Heavy, distorted, driving — more aggressive than synthwave bass
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw or square with heavy low-pass, sub-oscillator
- **Frequency range**: 30–150 Hz fundamental, distortion harmonics up to 500 Hz
- **Processing**: Distortion/saturation, sidechain to kick, mono
- **Role in mix**: Low-end power — heavier than standard synthwave

#### Drums
- **Character**: Electronic + rock hybrid, punchy and driving
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered electronic + processed acoustic samples
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum
- **Processing**: Parallel compression, saturation, tight gated reverb
- **Role in mix**: Hard-hitting, aggressive, driving the tempo forward

#### Impact FX
- **Character**: Metallic crashes, engine sounds, tire screeches — cinematic
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered noise + metallic synthesis + reverb
- **Processing**: Long reverb, distortion, dramatic EQ
- **Role in mix**: Transition accents, section markers, thematic elements

## Darkwave

### Signature Sounds

#### Driving Bass (Primary)
- **Character**: Dark, melodic, mid-register — often the lead instrument (post-punk heritage)
- **Synthesis approach**: Single oscillator (saw or square), low-pass filter, short decay, mono
- **Frequency range**: 60–400 Hz (higher register than typical bass — melodic role)
- **Processing**: Chorus (subtle), compression, slight overdrive for presence
- **Role in mix**: THE leading element — provides melody, rhythm, and bass simultaneously
- **Classic sources**: SH-101, Moog Rogue, MS-20, real bass guitar with chorus

#### Cold Pads
- **Character**: Thin, cold, atmospheric — not lush (unlike synthwave pads)
- **Synthesis approach**: Single oscillator (saw or pulse), slow attack, minimal detuning
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 3 kHz
- **Processing**: Long reverb (cavernous), subtle filter modulation
- **Role in mix**: Atmospheric bed — cold, distant, not warm
- **Classic sources**: Korg MS-20, Roland SH-2, Juno with minimal chorus

#### Drum Machine
- **Character**: Dry, mechanical, thin — unprocessed drum machine aesthetics
- **Synthesis approach**: Classic drum machines (808, LinnDrum, Oberheim DMX)
- **Frequency range**: Narrow — thin kick, crisp snare, simple hat
- **Processing**: MINIMAL — dryness is part of the aesthetic; slight compression at most
- **Role in mix**: Timekeeping — background element, not focal
- **Classic sources**: TR-808, LinnDrum, Oberheim DMX, Korg KR-55

#### Dark Guitar
- **Character**: Chorus-drenched, reverb-soaked, jangly or angular
- **Synthesis approach**: Real electric guitar (not synthesized)
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 4 kHz
- **Processing**: Heavy chorus (CE-2, Small Clone), long reverb, flanger
- **Role in mix**: Textural mid-range — arpeggiated chords or single-note lines

#### Vocals
- **Character**: Deep baritone, melancholic, slightly distant
- **Processing**: Room/hall reverb, subtle delay, minimal compression
- **Frequency range**: 100–3 kHz (deeper male vocals typical)
- **Role in mix**: Emotional center — present but not dominating the mix

## Darksynth

### Signature Sounds

#### Distorted Lead Synth
- **Character**: Aggressive, screaming, horror-movie — heavily distorted saw leads
- **Synthesis approach**: Stacked saws (5+), heavy waveshaping/distortion, resonant filter
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 8 kHz (wide, aggressive)
- **Processing**: Heavy distortion (tube/tape/digital), compression, short delay
- **Role in mix**: Primary assault — the "riff" instrument, guitar-replacement

#### Heavy Bass
- **Character**: Massive, distorted, sub + mid presence — HEAVY
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw + sub sine, heavy low-pass with drive, FM for grit
- **Frequency range**: 30–300 Hz (wide bass, not just sub)
- **Processing**: Distortion, multiband compression, sidechain to kick
- **Role in mix**: Low-end weight — aggressive and prominent

#### Horror Stingers
- **Character**: Short, dissonant, shocking — jump-scare sound design
- **Synthesis approach**: Detuned stacks hitting diminished/chromatic intervals, short envelope
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 6 kHz (midrange shock)
- **Processing**: Reverb (short, dark), distortion, fast reverb tail
- **Role in mix**: Transitional accents, dramatic punctuation

#### Drums (Metal-Electronic Hybrid)
- **Character**: Rock/metal kick and snare layered with electronic samples
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered acoustic metal samples + 808/909 electronic
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum — heavy sub kick, bright cymbals
- **Processing**: Parallel compression, saturation, room reverb on snare
- **Role in mix**: Hard-hitting, driving — bridge between electronic and metal

#### Atmospheric Drones
- **Character**: Dark, oppressive, evolving — horror film atmosphere
- **Synthesis approach**: Granular, wavetable, or long evolving samples
- **Frequency range**: 80 Hz – 2 kHz (low-mid darkness)
- **Processing**: Long reverb, saturation, slow modulation, volume automation
- **Role in mix**: Background atmosphere — creates dread and tension

## Coldwave

### Signature Sounds

#### Minimal Bass Synth
- **Character**: Cold, thin, mono — melodically active but sonically restrained
- **Synthesis approach**: Single oscillator (pulse or saw), minimal filter, short decay
- **Frequency range**: 60–300 Hz
- **Processing**: Almost none — dryness is the aesthetic
- **Role in mix**: Melodic lead AND bass simultaneously (post-punk heritage)
- **Classic sources**: SH-101, Moog Prodigy, MS-20

#### Thin Synth Lines
- **Character**: Cold, single-oscillator, piercing — minimal warmth
- **Synthesis approach**: Single square or narrow pulse wave, no detuning, simple envelope
- **Frequency range**: 400 Hz – 4 kHz
- **Processing**: Minimal — perhaps subtle chorus or short delay
- **Role in mix**: Counter-melody or textural line — sparse, not layered

#### Drum Machine (Dry)
- **Character**: Minimal, dry, cold — unprocessed drum machine directly into the mix
- **Synthesis approach**: Cheap drum machines or basic 808/606 sounds
- **Frequency range**: Limited — intentionally thin and lo-fi
- **Processing**: None or minimal — rawness is authentic
- **Role in mix**: Basic timekeeping — deliberately unsexy
- **Classic sources**: Roland TR-606, Boss DR-55, Korg KR-55

#### Angular Guitar
- **Character**: Dry, angular, post-punk — chorus-less (unlike darkwave guitar)
- **Synthesis approach**: Real electric guitar, clean amp, angular playing
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 4 kHz
- **Processing**: Minimal — dry or very short reverb; flanger occasionally
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic texture, counter-rhythm to bass

#### Vocals
- **Character**: Detached, cold, spoken-sung, monotone
- **Processing**: Minimal reverb, no effects — dry and present
- **Frequency range**: 150 Hz – 3 kHz
- **Role in mix**: Emotionally detached — present but deliberately flat in delivery

## Post-Punk

### Signature Sounds

#### Bass Guitar (Primary)
- **Character**: Driving, melodic, high-register — THE defining instrument
- **Synthesis approach**: Real bass guitar (Rickenbacker, Fender P-Bass, Gibson EB)
- **Frequency range**: 80–800 Hz (plays high — melodic register)
- **Processing**: Chorus (optional), compression, slight overdrive, DI + amp blend
- **Role in mix**: Lead melodic instrument AND rhythmic driver simultaneously
- **Classic players**: Peter Hook (Joy Division), Simon Gallup (The Cure), Paul Simonon

#### Angular Guitar
- **Character**: Jangly, angular, sharp — rhythmic and textural
- **Synthesis approach**: Real electric guitar — telecaster/jazzmaster type
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 6 kHz (high-passed, leaving room for bass)
- **Processing**: Chorus (CE-2), flanger (Electric Mistress), reverb, short delay
- **Role in mix**: Upper-mid texture — rhythmic counterpoint to bass
- **Playing style**: Arpeggiated chords, angular single-note lines, funk-influenced scratching

#### Drums
- **Character**: Tight, dry, urgent, tom-heavy fills
- **Synthesis approach**: Real acoustic drum kit (minimal)
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum but not over-produced
- **Processing**: Minimal — room mics, light compression, tight and immediate
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic backbone — urgent and driving
- **Style**: Tom-heavy fills, minimal cymbals, tight kick and snare

#### Vocals
- **Character**: Urgent, spoken-sung, theatrical, intellectual
- **Processing**: Room reverb (short), slapback delay, compression
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 6 kHz
- **Role in mix**: Upfront, present, commanding — emotional center
- **Style**: Ian Curtis (baritone, deep), Mark E. Smith (spoken), Robert Smith (ethereal)

#### Synths (When Present)
- **Character**: Textural, minimal, atmospheric — secondary role
- **Synthesis approach**: Simple mono synth lines or basic pads
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 3 kHz
- **Processing**: Reverb, delay — atmospheric treatment
- **Role in mix**: Background texture — NEVER dominates over guitar/bass

## EBM (Electronic Body Music)

### Signature Sounds

#### Sequencer Bass (The Definitive Sound)
- **Character**: Acidic, driving, repetitive — 16th-note sequences that ARE the music
- **Synthesis approach**: Single saw/square oscillator through resonant low-pass filter, filter envelope
- **Frequency range**: 60–500 Hz (sub + character)
- **Processing**: Distortion/saturation, filter automation, sidechain to kick
- **Role in mix**: THE primary element — everything else supports this
- **Classic sources**: TB-303, SH-101, MS-20, Novation Bass Station

#### Hard Kick
- **Character**: Punchy, electronic, club-weight — driving the dancefloor
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine wave with pitch envelope (909/808 synthesis)
- **Frequency range**: 40–100 Hz fundamental, click at 2–4 kHz
- **Processing**: Compression, saturation, EQ for click
- **Role in mix**: Foundation — locks with bass sequence
- **Classic sources**: TR-909, TR-808, LinnDrum

#### Electronic Clap
- **Character**: Sharp, aggressive, dry — snappy
- **Synthesis approach**: Noise burst with band-pass filter, short decay
- **Frequency range**: 800 Hz – 4 kHz
- **Processing**: Minimal — sharp and dry
- **Role in mix**: Backbeat marker — punchy and present

#### Synth Stabs
- **Character**: Short, aggressive, industrial-tinged — punctuation
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw wave chord, very short envelope (10–50ms)
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 3 kHz
- **Processing**: Distortion, slight reverb
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic accents, counter-rhythm to bass sequence

#### Vocals
- **Character**: Commanding, shouted, aggressive — militant delivery
- **Processing**: Compression (heavy), distortion (light), short delay
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 6 kHz
- **Role in mix**: Commanding presence — authoritative and upfront
- **Style**: Spoken/shouted commands, call-and-response, minimal melody

## Industrial (Electronic)

### Signature Sounds

#### Metal Percussion
- **Character**: Literally metal being struck — pipes, plates, springs, chains
- **Synthesis approach**: Contact mic recordings of metal objects, sampled and sequenced
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 10 kHz (metallic resonances)
- **Processing**: Compression, EQ to shape resonances, reverb (industrial spaces)
- **Role in mix**: Percussion replacement or layer — raw and physical

#### Distorted Kick
- **Character**: Heavily distorted, almost noise — functional but destroyed
- **Synthesis approach**: 909/808 kick driven through heavy distortion chains
- **Frequency range**: 30–300 Hz (distortion adds wide harmonic content)
- **Processing**: Heavy distortion, compression, limiting
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic anchor — felt as much as heard

#### Noise Textures
- **Character**: Harsh, evolving, confrontational — white/pink noise manipulation
- **Synthesis approach**: Noise generators, modular feedback, tape manipulation
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum (noise is broadband)
- **Processing**: Filtering, gating, modulation, ring modulation
- **Role in mix**: Atmospheric aggression — fills space with tension

#### Found Sound Samples
- **Character**: Machine recordings, speech fragments, environmental sounds
- **Synthesis approach**: Field recording + processing
- **Frequency range**: Varies (source-dependent)
- **Processing**: Time-stretching, pitch-shifting, granular processing, reverb
- **Role in mix**: Textural interest, thematic content, rhythmic material

#### Power Electronics Vocals
- **Character**: Screamed, processed, feedback-laden
- **Processing**: Extreme distortion, feedback, delay, reverb
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 8 kHz (midrange aggression)
- **Role in mix**: Confrontational presence — may dominate or be buried in noise

## Witch House

### Signature Sounds

#### Pitched-Down Vocals
- **Character**: Slowed, screwed, ghostly — words become textures
- **Synthesis approach**: Real vocals pitch-shifted down 3–8 semitones, time-stretched
- **Frequency range**: 80–1 kHz (pitched low, dark)
- **Processing**: Pitch-shift, reverb (long, dark), saturation, chopping
- **Role in mix**: Atmospheric lead — textural rather than lyrical

#### 808 Sub Bass
- **Character**: Deep, heavy, short — trap-influenced but slowed
- **Synthesis approach**: Classic 808 synthesis (sine with pitch envelope)
- **Frequency range**: 30–80 Hz
- **Processing**: Distortion for harmonics, sidechain, mono
- **Role in mix**: Low-end weight — sparse, impactful hits

#### Chopped Samples
- **Character**: Fragments of pop/R&B slowed and screwed — uncanny valley
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample manipulation — time-stretch, pitch-shift, reverse, chop
- **Frequency range**: Varies (full spectrum content degraded)
- **Processing**: Pitch-down, reverb, saturation, bit-crushing, vinyl noise
- **Role in mix**: Primary harmonic/melodic content (repurposed samples)

#### Dark Pads / Drones
- **Character**: Occult, dark, evolving — ritual atmosphere
- **Synthesis approach**: Granular synthesis, reversed reverb tails, slowed textures
- **Frequency range**: 80 Hz – 2 kHz (dark, low-mid focus)
- **Processing**: Reverb (massive), saturation, slow modulation
- **Role in mix**: Atmospheric foundation — creates the occult space

#### Trap Hi-Hats (Slowed)
- **Character**: Triplet rolls pitched down and slowed — druggy and heavy
- **Synthesis approach**: Trap hat samples slowed 20–40%
- **Frequency range**: 4–10 kHz (lower than typical trap hats due to slowdown)
- **Processing**: Saturation, pitch-down, reverb
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic texture — sparse, slow, heavy

## Vaporwave / Mallsoft

### Signature Sounds

#### Slowed Samples (Primary)
- **Character**: Commercial music from the 80s/90s pitched down and slowed — surreal, melancholic
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample existing music, slow to 70–80% speed, pitch-shift down
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum (content dependent on source)
- **Processing**: Pitch/time manipulation, vinyl crackle, tape warble, reverb
- **Role in mix**: THE primary content — everything else serves this

#### Vinyl Noise / Tape Hiss
- **Character**: Lo-fi degradation — crackle, hiss, warble — simulated aging
- **Synthesis approach**: Vinyl noise samples, tape machine recordings, iZotope Vinyl plugin
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum (subtle background)
- **Processing**: Layered under main content, volume automation
- **Role in mix**: Atmosphere — creates sense of time, decay, nostalgia

#### Reverb / Space Simulation
- **Character**: Large empty spaces — malls, lobbies, atriums
- **Synthesis approach**: Long reverb (8–15 seconds), convolution of real spaces
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum (spatial information)
- **Processing**: Long decay, pre-delay, diffusion
- **Role in mix**: Creates the "space" — especially critical for mallsoft (simulating physical environments)

#### Glitch / Degradation
- **Character**: Digital artifacts, CD skipping, buffer glitches
- **Synthesis approach**: Bit-crushing, sample-rate reduction, buffer manipulation, stutter edits
- **Frequency range**: Varies (artifacts can be any frequency)
- **Processing**: Deliberate "errors" — the medium breaking down
- **Role in mix**: Transitional elements, textural interest, commentary on digital decay

#### Ambient Textures (Mallsoft)
- **Character**: Fountain sounds, distant conversation, HVAC hum — environmental
- **Synthesis approach**: Field recordings of commercial spaces, layered and processed
- **Frequency range**: Broadband (environmental ambience)
- **Processing**: Reverb, EQ to shape "space," volume modulation
- **Role in mix**: Creates the mallsoft "world" — music plays WITHIN this environment
