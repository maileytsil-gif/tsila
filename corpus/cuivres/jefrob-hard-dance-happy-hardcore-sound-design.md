# Sound Design Palette

## Hard Trance

### Signature Sounds

#### Hard Trance Kick
- **Character**: Hard, punchy, short-tailed — aggressive but not distorted like gabber
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered sine wave pitch envelope (fast sweep 200→50 Hz) + click transient sample
- **Frequency range**: Fundamental 50–70 Hz, body 100–200 Hz, click 3–5 kHz
- **Processing**: Transient shaping (enhance attack), light saturation, tight compression, HPF at 30 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Anchor — drives the four-on-the-floor without dominating like hardstyle kicks

#### TB-303 Acid Line
- **Character**: Squelchy, resonant, evolving — the soul of hard trance
- **Synthesis approach**: Monophonic sawtooth/square oscillator with resonant low-pass filter, accent, and slide controls
- **Frequency range**: Fundamental 80–300 Hz, filter harmonics up to 8 kHz when resonance peaks
- **Processing**: Distortion/overdrive, delay (16th note sync), filter automation throughout arrangement
- **Role in mix**: Primary melodic/rhythmic element — THE driving force of the genre
- **Key technique**: Filter cutoff automation over 16–32 bars creates the "opening" build

#### Trance Pads (Breakdowns)
- **Character**: Lush, atmospheric, warm — emotional contrast to the hard main sections
- **Synthesis approach**: Detuned sawtooth stack (5–7 voices), slow filter movement, chorus
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 6 kHz (wide, filling)
- **Processing**: Long reverb (3–5 seconds), slow chorus, subtle stereo widening
- **Role in mix**: Carries breakdowns emotionally — removed when kick returns

#### Snare/Clap
- **Character**: Hard, bright, cutting — more aggressive than standard trance snare
- **Synthesis approach**: Noise burst (white noise) + resonant body, tight envelope
- **Frequency range**: Body 200–400 Hz, snap 2–5 kHz, air 8–12 kHz
- **Processing**: Tight compression, high-pass at 150 Hz, short reverb for space
- **Role in mix**: Backbeat driver, transition tool (rolls)

#### Acid Lead (Peak Sections)
- **Character**: Screaming, distorted 303 at full resonance — raw, aggressive
- **Synthesis approach**: 303 emulation with resonance at 90%+, multiple distortion stages
- **Frequency range**: 1–8 kHz (cuts through everything when resonance peaks)
- **Processing**: Heavy distortion, delay throws, occasional reverb for space
- **Role in mix**: Peak energy element — appears only at climax moments

#### Reverse Crash/Riser
- **Character**: Building tension — sweeping noise moving toward impact point
- **Synthesis approach**: White noise with band-pass filter sweep (low→high over 4–8 bars)
- **Frequency range**: Sweeps from 200 Hz to 12 kHz
- **Processing**: Reverb, volume automation (crescendo), sometimes layered with pitch-rising sine
- **Role in mix**: Transition tool — signals upcoming section change

## Hard NRG

### Signature Sounds

#### NRG Kick
- **Character**: Relentless, hard, punchy — bigger and harder than hard trance, simpler than hardstyle
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine pitch envelope + layered transient, minimal harmonics
- **Frequency range**: Fundamental 45–65 Hz, punch 100–200 Hz, click 3–5 kHz
- **Processing**: Heavy compression for consistency, transient enhancement, HPF at 30 Hz
- **Role in mix**: THE dominant element — everything else serves the kick

#### Offbeat Bass
- **Character**: Simple, driving, often following kick pitch — functional
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine/triangle with light saturation, tight envelope
- **Frequency range**: 40–120 Hz
- **Processing**: Sidechain to kick, mono, compression for evenness
- **Role in mix**: Low-end weight between kick hits — creates relentless forward motion

#### Stab Synth
- **Character**: Short, sharp, bright — percussive more than melodic
- **Synthesis approach**: Detuned saw stack, fast attack/decay envelope, no sustain
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 6 kHz
- **Processing**: Tight compression, short reverb, possible chorus
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic accent — adds energy without melodic complexity

#### Hi-Hat (Driving)
- **Character**: Bright, consistent, metronomic — the machine-gun 16ths
- **Synthesis approach**: Filtered noise burst, very short decay
- **Frequency range**: 6–14 kHz
- **Processing**: Minimal — just EQ for brightness, maybe slight compression
- **Role in mix**: Creates continuous forward motion alongside the kick

#### Filter Sweep (Main Loop)
- **Character**: Rising/falling filtered noise or synth — tension tool
- **Synthesis approach**: White noise or saw through resonant filter with automation
- **Frequency range**: Sweeps through 200 Hz – 10 kHz
- **Processing**: Resonant filter automation, reverb on the tail
- **Role in mix**: Transition element — only functional tool, not emotional

## Happy Hardcore

### Signature Sounds

#### Piano Stabs (THE Defining Sound)
- **Character**: Bright, punchy, euphoric — acoustic piano quality, rhythmic delivery
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled piano (bright grand or FM piano), short note lengths
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 5 kHz (warm midrange presence)
- **Processing**: Light compression, short room reverb, EQ scoop at 200 Hz to avoid mud
- **Role in mix**: PRIMARY element — the piano IS happy hardcore
- **Key technique**: Chromatic ascending runs for builds, offbeat stab patterns for groove

#### Happy HC Kick
- **Character**: Bouncy, punchy, clean — NOT distorted (distinguish from gabber)
- **Synthesis approach**: Short sine pitch envelope (quick 150→55 Hz), minimal harmonics
- **Frequency range**: Fundamental 50–65 Hz, body 100–150 Hz, light click 2–3 kHz
- **Processing**: Light compression, HPF at 35 Hz, no distortion or saturation
- **Role in mix**: Anchor — provides pulse without dominating the piano

#### Pitched-Up Vocals
- **Character**: Female vocal pitched up 2–4 semitones — bright, euphoric, rave nostalgia
- **Synthesis approach**: Recorded vocal pitch-shifted (formant-preserving or raw pitch shift for classic sound)
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 8 kHz (presence range)
- **Processing**: Compression, de-essing, plate reverb, delay throws on phrases
- **Role in mix**: Emotional hook — carries the song structure (verse/chorus)
- **Classic technique**: Pitch-shifting without formant correction gives the "chipmunk" character

#### Supersaw Lead
- **Character**: Wide, bright, euphoric — massive unison detuned saws
- **Synthesis approach**: 5–7 detuned sawtooth oscillators, layered in octaves
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 10 kHz (wide spectral presence)
- **Processing**: Chorus, short delay, sidechain to kick, stereo widening
- **Role in mix**: Leads melodic sections, fills drops with energy

#### Rave Stab (Hoover/Mentasm)
- **Character**: Classic 90s rave sound — gritty, filtered, nostalgic
- **Synthesis approach**: PWM sawtooth through resonant filter, detuned voices (Roland Alpha Juno origin)
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 4 kHz
- **Processing**: Distortion, chorus, filter modulation, reverb
- **Role in mix**: Accent element — punctuation between piano stabs, nostalgia trigger

#### Snare Roll
- **Character**: Rapid-fire 32nd-note rolls at 170+ BPM — extreme speed, building tension
- **Synthesis approach**: Tight snare sample programmed as 32nd notes with rising velocity
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 8 kHz
- **Processing**: Compression for evenness, reverb on final hit only, pitch-rise optional
- **Role in mix**: Build tool — creates anticipation before drops

## UK Hardcore

### Signature Sounds

#### Modern Supersaw Stack
- **Character**: Wide, polished, full-spectrum — more produced than classic happy HC saws
- **Synthesis approach**: 7+ detuned sawtooth oscillators, multi-band processing, layered octaves
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 12 kHz (controlled, sculpted presence)
- **Processing**: Multi-band compression, stereo imaging, sidechain pumping, precise EQ
- **Role in mix**: Primary tonal element in drops — replaces piano as the main sound

#### Pitched Female Vocal
- **Character**: Modern, emotional, R&B-influenced — pitched up 1–3 semitones with formant preservation
- **Synthesis approach**: Professional vocal recording, pitch-shifted with modern tools (Waves, Melodyne)
- **Frequency range**: 400 Hz – 10 kHz
- **Processing**: Detailed compression (multi-stage), de-essing, tape saturation, tuning, reverb sends
- **Role in mix**: Emotional core — breakdowns feature full vocal production

#### UK HC Kick
- **Character**: Clean, tight, punchy — modern production, precise frequency control
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered: sub sine (pitch envelope) + mid punch sample + transient click
- **Frequency range**: Sub 40–60 Hz, punch 80–150 Hz, click 2–4 kHz
- **Processing**: Transient design, multi-band compression, precision EQ, sidechain source
- **Role in mix**: Foundation — clean enough to coexist with dedicated sub bass

#### Sub Bass Layer
- **Character**: Deep, sustained sub — separate from kick (modern low-end approach)
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine wave or triangle, sustained envelope
- **Frequency range**: 30–80 Hz
- **Processing**: Sidechain to kick (heavy ducking), mono, limiter, HPF at 25 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Low-end weight between kicks — adds fullness without muddiness

#### White Noise Riser
- **Character**: Building filtered noise sweep — modern transition tool
- **Synthesis approach**: White noise through high-pass filter with rising cutoff
- **Frequency range**: Sweeps from 200 Hz to full spectrum
- **Processing**: Volume automation, filter automation, reverb pre-impact
- **Role in mix**: Transition/build element — signals energy increase

#### Impact Hit
- **Character**: Massive, layered hit on beat 1 of new sections
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered: sub drop (pitch 200→30 Hz) + noise burst + cymbal crash + reverb tail
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum (30 Hz – 16 kHz)
- **Processing**: Limiting, tight stereo (mono sub), wide top end
- **Role in mix**: Marks section transitions — drop arrivals, breakdown entries

## Freeform

### Signature Sounds

#### Trance-Style Pad Stack
- **Character**: Rich, evolving, atmospheric — extended sustain for long breakdowns
- **Synthesis approach**: Wavetable or granular synthesis, multiple oscillators with slow modulation
- **Frequency range**: 150 Hz – 8 kHz (wide, immersive)
- **Processing**: Long reverb (5+ seconds), slow chorus, filter LFO, stereo spread
- **Role in mix**: Backbone of extended breakdowns — can sustain for 32+ bars

#### Freeform Kick (Variable)
- **Character**: Adaptable — harder for peak sections, softer for melodic passages
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered sine + distortion amount varies per section
- **Frequency range**: 40–200 Hz depending on character
- **Processing**: Varies: clean compression for melodic sections, saturation for hard sections
- **Role in mix**: Provides tempo anchor while adapting to the track's emotional arc

#### Arpeggiated Lead
- **Character**: Fast 16th-note arpeggios outlining complex chords — trance influence at HC speed
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw or square wave through arpeggiator, filter modulation
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 8 kHz
- **Processing**: Delay (sync'd), filter automation, stereo ping-pong
- **Role in mix**: Climax energy — appears during peak sections for maximum intensity

#### Experimental Textures
- **Character**: Granular, time-stretched, abstract — pushing sonic boundaries
- **Synthesis approach**: Granular synthesis, spectral processing, time-stretch artifacts
- **Frequency range**: Variable (full spectrum potential)
- **Processing**: Heavy reverb, delay, pitch shifting, granular clouds
- **Role in mix**: Atmospheric color — distinguishes freeform from other HC subgenres

#### Extended Breakdown Melody
- **Character**: Lyrical, emotional, wide-ranging — trance-length phrases at HC tempo
- **Synthesis approach**: Analog-style lead (saw with filter), or FM synthesis for bell-like quality
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 6 kHz
- **Processing**: Delay (dotted 8th), reverb, subtle chorus, volume expression
- **Role in mix**: Emotional centerpiece during 32+ bar breakdowns

## Trancecore

### Signature Sounds

#### Supersaw Pad/Lead (Trance DNA)
- **Character**: Classic trance supersaw — massive, wide, emotional
- **Synthesis approach**: 7+ unison detuned sawtooths (JP-8000/Sylenth1 style)
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 10 kHz
- **Processing**: Chorus, sidechain, stereo imaging, long reverb tail
- **Role in mix**: Carries both breakdowns (pads) and climaxes (leads) — genre-defining sound

#### Trancecore Kick
- **Character**: Punchy, clean, trance-weight — not overly hard or distorted
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine pitch envelope with moderate attack transient
- **Frequency range**: Sub 45–60 Hz, body 100–180 Hz, click 2–4 kHz
- **Processing**: Light compression, EQ sculpting, sidechain source for other elements
- **Role in mix**: Tempo anchor — removed during extended breakdowns (trance convention)

#### Pluck/Arpeggio Synth
- **Character**: Short, bright, rhythmic — 16th-note patterns for energy
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw/square with fast filter envelope (pluck shape), arpeggiator
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 8 kHz
- **Processing**: Delay (dotted 8th or triplet), filter modulation, light reverb
- **Role in mix**: Adds movement during builds and transitions — classic trance device

#### Breakdown Vocal/Choir
- **Character**: Ethereal, emotional — choir pads or processed vocal for breakdown atmosphere
- **Synthesis approach**: Choir samples or vocal processing (pitch shifting, granular)
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 6 kHz
- **Processing**: Long reverb, delay, filter automation, gentle compression
- **Role in mix**: Emotional layer during extended breakdowns — enhances the trance feeling

#### Trance Bass (Driving Sections)
- **Character**: Rolling, filtered saw bass — trance-style but faster
- **Synthesis approach**: Sawtooth with rhythmic filter gate (16th-note pattern)
- **Frequency range**: 60–300 Hz
- **Processing**: Sidechain to kick, distortion for harmonics, mono below 150 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Drives main sections with harmonic energy above the sub

## Powerstomp

### Signature Sounds

#### Stomping Kick (THE Signature)
- **Character**: Bouncy, mid-heavy stomp — the physical "stomp" response in listeners
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered: punchy mid-body (100–200 Hz emphasis) + sub layer + stomp transient
- **Frequency range**: Sub 50–70 Hz, stomp body 100–250 Hz, attack 2–4 kHz
- **Processing**: Parallel compression for sustain, saturation for warmth, EQ boost at 150 Hz
- **Role in mix**: DOMINANT — the stomp IS the genre, everything else supports it

#### Offbeat Bass Bounce
- **Character**: Simple, rounded, bouncy — offbeat hits creating the bounce feel
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine/triangle wave, short-medium envelope
- **Frequency range**: 50–150 Hz
- **Processing**: Sidechain to kick, mono, compression for consistency
- **Role in mix**: Creates bounce between kick stomps — essential groove partner

#### Rave Stab (Classic)
- **Character**: 90s rave stab — hoover/mentasm sound, gritty, nostalgic
- **Synthesis approach**: PWM saw, resonant filter, detuned (Alpha Juno style)
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 5 kHz
- **Processing**: Distortion, chorus, filter modulation, short reverb
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic accent and energy — punctuates the stomp pattern

#### MC Vocal Chants
- **Character**: Crowd-participatory vocal phrases — "everybody stomp", call-and-response
- **Synthesis approach**: Recorded vocals, sometimes pitched/chopped
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 6 kHz
- **Processing**: Compression, short delay, telephone EQ for effect, crowd reverb
- **Role in mix**: Engages the crowd — participatory element, not emotional like UK HC vocals

#### Noise Stab/Hit
- **Character**: Short burst of filtered noise — percussive accent
- **Synthesis approach**: White noise with band-pass filter, fast decay envelope
- **Frequency range**: 1–8 kHz depending on filter setting
- **Processing**: Saturation, tight gate, slight reverb
- **Role in mix**: Fills rhythmic gaps, adds energy without harmonic content
