# How to Make a Hardstyle Screech in Xfer Serum

 In this tutorial, learn how to design a powerful hardstyle screech sound in Xfer Serum . Using a mix of detuned oscillators, peak filters, and dynamic modulation, this sound gives your drops and intros that signature high-energy edge found in hardstyle and rawstyle tracks.

 If you love this sound design style, check out our curated Hardstyle Serum Preset Packs and the full collection of Serum Presets available now on ADSR.

## 1. Start with the Oscillator Setup

 Begin by initializing a new patch and loading the Basic CJW wavetable from the Analog folder. This waveform sits between a sine, saw, and triangle — ideal for a screech foundation. Copy Oscillator A to Oscillator B and set both to 7 unison voices . Increase detune for a wide, aggressive tone.

 This heavy detuning creates that thick, noisy texture common in hardstyle screeches. Slightly lower the blend value to keep the sound centered and powerful.

## 2. Add Vibrato with LFO Modulation

 To introduce motion, drag LFO 1 to the Coarse Pitch of both oscillators. Disable BPM sync and set the rate around 20 Hz for a fast vibrato effect. This adds an intense, metallic shimmer to the screech — a key characteristic of hardstyle leads.

 Next, assign Macro 1 to control vibrato depth in the Matrix tab. This lets you automate the vibrato amount in your DAW, making it easy to build transitions or automate pitch energy through your track.

## 3. Add Sub Layer for Depth

 Enable the Sub Oscillator and select a saw wave. Instead of using it as a low-end sub, tune it slightly higher to thicken the overall sound. Blend it in around 25% — this subtle harmonic reinforcement fills the sound’s lower midrange without overpowering the mix.

## 4. Shape the Sound with Filters

 Now move to the Filter section and select Peak 12 . Increase the Resonance and automate the Cutoff using LFO 2 . This creates the movement and tonal sweep typical of hardstyle screeches. Slowly increase Drive and Fat to add body and distortion.

 The trick is to use LFO 2 in Envelope Mode — so it triggers once per note rather than looping. This creates a sharp, evolving tone that responds dynamically to your playing.

## 5. Add Effects for Punch and Character

 Now it’s time to polish the sound inside Serum’s FX rack :

 Hyper/Dimension: Adds subtle stereo width and presence.

 Compressor (Multiband): Brings punch and volume consistency.

 EQ: Boost highs for presence and clarity.

 Reverb: Use a short, bright reverb for space. High-cut the reverb to prevent masking your hi-hats or lead elements.

 Keep in mind that too much reverb in the high range can clutter your mix — always filter out highs and lows in the reverb to keep your mix clean and punchy.

## 6. Add Pitch Glide

 Finally, create that signature “screech glide” by assigning LFO 2 to Master Tune . Use Envelope mode again and adjust the rate for a fast pitch ramp-up. This creates the classic pitching screech effect you hear in tracks by artists like Headhunterz and Sub Zero Project.

## 7. Final Touches

 Once your sound is complete, tweak macro controls for vibrato and filter cutoff. This gives you live control over intensity and movement during drops or build-ups. Try automating Macro 1 for energy sweeps across your arrangement.
