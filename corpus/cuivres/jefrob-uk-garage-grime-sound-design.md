# Sound Design Palette

## 2-Step / UK Garage

### Signature Sounds

#### Kick Drum (Skipped Pattern)
- **Character**: Deep, rounded, subby — NOT clicky or punchy like techno
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based (classic UK garage kicks are specific samples passed between producers)
- **Frequency range**: 40–80 Hz fundamental, light body at 100–150 Hz, minimal click above 3 kHz
- **Processing**: Minimal — slight saturation for warmth, HPF at 30 Hz
- **Length**: Medium (80–120ms) — enough sub tail to fill the groove
- **Role**: Rhythmic anchor, but NOT dominant — its absence (the skip) is as important as its presence

#### Hi-Hats (Shuffled)
- **Character**: Crisp, bright, swung — organic feel despite electronic source
- **Source**: Roland TR-909 closed/open hat or similar bright electronic hats
- **Frequency range**: 6–14 kHz
- **Processing**: Light reverb (short room), no compression, pan slightly off-center
- **Open hat**: Longer decay, placed on offbeats for rhythmic lift
- **Critical**: MUST be swung — straight hats break the entire genre feel

#### Bass (Deep Sub)
- **Character**: Deep, warm, funky — melodic with sub weight
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine/triangle sub with slight saturation, sometimes layered with higher harmonic for definition
- **Frequency range**: 35–80 Hz fundamental, harmonics to 200 Hz for definition
- **Processing**: Sidechain to kick (subtle, 3–4 dB), mono below 120 Hz, light compression
- **Articulation**: Short-medium notes, syncopated — funky patterns
- **Role**: Melodic foundation AND rhythmic driver — one of the groove's primary voices

#### Vocal Chops
- **Character**: R&B-style vocals, chopped, timestretched, pitched — used as rhythmic/melodic hooks
- **Source**: Sampled from R&B/soul records, then sliced and rearranged
- **Processing**: Time-stretching (signature grainy quality), pitch correction, reverb, delay throws
- **Technique**: Todd Edwards-style micro-chop (tiny vocal syllables rearranged into new melodies)
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 5 kHz
- **Role**: Melodic hook and rhythmic element simultaneously

#### Pads (Lush Chords)
- **Character**: Warm, jazzy, Rhodes/string-like — sustained atmosphere
- **Voicings**: 7ths, 9ths — jazz-influenced shell voicings
- **Synthesis approach**: Analog-modeled pads, Rhodes/Wurlitzer patches, string machines
- **Processing**: Chorus, medium plate reverb, slight high-pass at 200 Hz
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 4 kHz
- **Role**: Harmonic bed — supports vocals without dominating

#### Percussion (Rimshots / Shakers / Tambourine)
- **Character**: Organic, swung, light — complements the groove without heaviness
- **Source**: Acoustic samples (tambourine, shaker, rimshot) or CR-78/LinnDrum-style
- **Processing**: Minimal — maybe light room reverb, HPF to keep them airy
- **Swing**: MUST match the hi-hat shuffle grid
- **Role**: Texture layer, fills rhythmic space around the skipped kick

## Speed Garage

### Signature Sounds

#### Kick Drum (Heavy 4x4)
- **Character**: Heavy, sub-weighted, dark — more aggressive than standard house kick
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based with layered sub sine for extra weight
- **Frequency range**: 35–60 Hz sub, body at 80–150 Hz, minimal top above 4 kHz
- **Processing**: Slight saturation, transient enhancement, HPF at 25 Hz
- **Length**: Medium-long (100–150ms) — sub tail carries weight
- **Role**: Driving foundation — relentless four-on-the-floor momentum

#### Warped Bass (THE Signature Sound)
- **Character**: Morphing, filtered, time-stretched quality — like being pulled through a wormhole
- **Synthesis approach**: Resampled bass with heavy filter modulation, or analog synth with resonant LPF sweep
- **Frequency range**: 40–200 Hz fundamental, filter harmonics up to 2 kHz
- **Processing**: Resonant low-pass filter with LFO/envelope modulation, distortion/saturation, sidechain to kick
- **Modulation**: Filter cutoff automated throughout the bar — the movement IS the sound
- **Role**: Star of the track — everything else supports the bass

#### Time-Stretched Vocal Stabs
- **Character**: Grainy, stretched, warped — recognizable word pulled to rhythmic length
- **Source**: Vocal samples (often "yeah", one-word exclamations) time-stretched dramatically
- **Processing**: Extreme time-stretch (Akai S-series or software equivalent), pitch adjustment, reverb
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 4 kHz
- **Effect**: Grainy artifacts from early time-stretch algorithms ARE the sound — don't clean them up
- **Role**: Genre identifier — signals "this is speed garage"

#### Hi-Hats
- **Character**: Tight, closed, driving — less swung than 2-step
- **Source**: TR-909 or similar electronic hat
- **Frequency range**: 7–14 kHz
- **Processing**: Minimal, tight, dry
- **Pattern**: Offbeat 8ths primarily, occasional 16th fills
- **Role**: Timekeeping — steady against the warped bass movement

#### Organ Stabs
- **Character**: US garage influence — percussive organ hits
- **Source**: Hammond/gospel organ patches, short staccato
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 3 kHz
- **Processing**: Light overdrive, short reverb, percussive envelope
- **Role**: Rhythmic harmonic punctuation — bridges US garage heritage

## Bassline / 4x4 Garage

### Signature Sounds

#### Kick Drum (Punchy 4x4)
- **Character**: Punchy, mid-focused, relentless — cuts through on club systems
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based, layered for punch (click layer + sub layer)
- **Frequency range**: 50–70 Hz sub, strong body at 100–200 Hz, click at 3–5 kHz
- **Processing**: Transient shaping (enhanced attack), compression, slight saturation
- **Length**: Short-medium (60–100ms) — tight to avoid clashing with rolling bass
- **Role**: Driving anchor — never stops, always present

#### Rolling Bass (THE Defining Element)
- **Character**: Heavy, rolling, LFO-modulated — continuous rhythmic sub energy
- **Synthesis approach**: Square/saw sub with resonant low-pass filter + LFO modulation on cutoff
- **Frequency range**: 40–150 Hz fundamental, filter harmonics up to 500 Hz when filter opens
- **Processing**: Sidechain to kick (tight, fast release), saturation for harmonics, mono below 120 Hz
- **LFO**: Synced to tempo, often 1/8 or 1/16 rate — creates the "rolling" quality
- **Role**: Energy engine — provides continuous forward momentum

#### Hi-Hats (Fast Offbeat)
- **Character**: Sharp, bright, mechanical — fast and relentless
- **Source**: TR-909 or hard electronic hat
- **Frequency range**: 8–14 kHz
- **Processing**: Minimal — tight and dry, maybe slight HPF at 7 kHz
- **Pattern**: Offbeat 16ths — constant motion
- **Role**: Urgency — adds to the relentless energy

#### Synth Stabs (Short & Sharp)
- **Character**: Percussive, short, rhythmic — hoover-influenced or simple saw/square stabs
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw/square wave with short amplitude envelope (fast attack, short decay)
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 3 kHz
- **Processing**: Compression, short reverb, maybe chorus for width
- **Role**: Rhythmic accent — fills gaps in the kick/hat pattern

#### Vocal Chops (Hooks)
- **Character**: Short chopped vocal phrases, repeated — hook-driven
- **Source**: R&B vocals sliced into short phrases, MC shouts
- **Processing**: Pitch correction, compression, presence EQ boost, short delay
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 5 kHz
- **Role**: Earworm hook — the memorable element that names the track

#### Snare / Clap
- **Character**: Hard, punchy, layered — cuts through the dense mix
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered clap + snare samples for impact
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz body, 1–5 kHz crack, 8+ kHz tail
- **Processing**: Compression, transient shaping, short reverb
- **Role**: Backbeat anchor on 2 and 4

## Grime

### Signature Sounds

#### Square Wave Bass (THE Grime Bass)
- **Character**: Cold, hard, digital — raw square wave with sub weight
- **Synthesis approach**: Pure square wave oscillator, detuned slightly, minimal processing
- **Frequency range**: 40–80 Hz fundamental, strong harmonics from square wave up to 500 Hz
- **Processing**: Minimal — the rawness IS the sound; maybe slight saturation
- **Articulation**: Sustained or rhythmic — single notes held for bars
- **Wiley's eski variant**: Slightly higher pitch, more rhythmic, ice-cold
- **Role**: Foundation — carries the entire low end with minimal support

#### Eski Clicks (Wiley Signature)
- **Character**: Ice-cold, metallic, crystalline — like frozen water droplets
- **Synthesis approach**: Short burst of high-frequency square/pulse wave, pitched high, very short envelope
- **Frequency range**: 2–8 kHz (sits in upper mid/presence range)
- **Processing**: No reverb, no delay — bone dry
- **Articulation**: Extremely short (10–30ms), rapid-fire patterns
- **Role**: Genre identifier — the sound of grime, textural and rhythmic

#### Kick Drum (Sparse)
- **Character**: Simple, short, often absent — not the focus
- **Synthesis approach**: Basic 808/electronic kick sample, short decay
- **Frequency range**: 50–80 Hz sub, minimal body above 150 Hz
- **Processing**: Almost none — raw and utilitarian
- **Length**: Short (40–80ms)
- **Role**: Punctuation — marks key rhythmic moments, often sparse

#### Snare / Clap
- **Character**: Sharp, clipped, dry — no reverb tail
- **Source**: TR-808 snare, layered electronic clap
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz body, 2–5 kHz crack
- **Processing**: Minimal — short, dry, direct
- **Length**: Very short (30–50ms)
- **Role**: Rhythmic marker — precise and functional

#### Lead Synths (Eski Melodies)
- **Character**: Cold, thin, digital — simple waveforms with no warmth
- **Synthesis approach**: Square wave or pulse wave, minimal modulation, monophonic
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 4 kHz (midrange presence)
- **Processing**: Bone dry — no reverb, no delay, no chorus
- **Articulation**: Short, staccato notes in repetitive 2–4 note patterns
- **Role**: Melodic identity — sparse but memorable motifs

#### Strings / Pads (Rare)
- **Character**: When present: cold, synthetic, cinematic — not warm
- **Synthesis approach**: Digital string patches, minimal modulation
- **Processing**: Light reverb only, no heavy spatial effects
- **Role**: Rare atmospheric element — most grime has NO pads
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 3 kHz

## UK Funky

### Signature Sounds

#### Congas / Bongos (Primary Groove Driver)
- **Character**: Warm, organic, multiple articulations — the heart of UK funky
- **Source**: Acoustic conga/bongo samples (Afro-Caribbean percussion libraries)
- **Articulations**: Open tone, muted, slap, ghost note (velocity layers essential)
- **Frequency range**: 200–800 Hz (body), 2–5 kHz (slap attack)
- **Processing**: Light room reverb for cohesion, pan for width, bus compression
- **Role**: THE primary groove element — UK funky is built on this

#### Kick Drum (Syncopated)
- **Character**: Deep, rounded, punchy — but placed syncopatedly, not 4x4
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based, layered sub + mid punch
- **Frequency range**: 40–70 Hz sub, 100–200 Hz body, light click
- **Processing**: Slight saturation, sidechain to bass
- **Placement**: Syncopated — follows clave-like rhythmic patterns
- **Role**: Groove foundation, interacts with conga patterns

#### Bass (Deep, Percussive Attack)
- **Character**: Deep sub with percussive front-end — each note is a rhythmic event
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine sub with added pluck/attack transient layer
- **Frequency range**: 40–100 Hz fundamental, attack transient at 1–3 kHz
- **Processing**: Sidechain to kick, compression, slight saturation for definition
- **Articulation**: Short, punchy notes — not sustained
- **Role**: Low-end anchor AND rhythmic contributor

#### Clave / Cowbell
- **Character**: Metallic, short, bright — rhythmic anchor for the whole groove
- **Source**: Classic cowbell/clave samples (TR-808 cowbell or wood block)
- **Frequency range**: 800 Hz – 4 kHz
- **Processing**: Dry, minimal — maybe slight room reverb
- **Pattern**: Steady repeating clave pattern — does not change
- **Role**: Timekeeping anchor — the reference point all syncopation plays against

#### Horn / Synth Stabs
- **Character**: Short, punchy, bright — accenting syncopated beats
- **Source**: Horn samples (trumpet stabs) or bright synth patches
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 5 kHz
- **Processing**: Short reverb, compression for punch, EQ for brightness
- **Articulation**: Staccato — very short, percussive
- **Role**: Accent layer — adds energy and harmonic colour at rhythmic peaks

#### Shakers / Tambourine
- **Character**: Constant, airy, groove-glue — multiple shaker types layered
- **Source**: Acoustic shaker samples, tambourine, egg shaker
- **Frequency range**: 6–14 kHz
- **Processing**: HPF at 5 kHz, light reverb, velocity variation
- **Role**: Continuous groove thread — never stops, ties percussion layers together

## Breakstep

### Signature Sounds

#### Sub-Bass (Dubstep-Weight)
- **Character**: Heavy, sustained, chest-rattling — maximum low-end pressure
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine wave sub with slight saturation, pitch bend automation
- **Frequency range**: 30–60 Hz fundamental (extreme low end)
- **Processing**: Sidechain to kick (heavy, 6–10 dB duck), mono, limiter on sub channel
- **Portamento**: Slides between notes (characteristic "wobbly" movement)
- **Role**: Weight — provides the dubstep-level physical impact

#### Glassy Synths (Night Slugs Aesthetic)
- **Character**: Crystalline, angular, processed — futuristic and cold
- **Synthesis approach**: FM synthesis, metallic timbres, short envelopes with high resonance
- **Frequency range**: 2–8 kHz (presence/brilliance range)
- **Processing**: Bitcrushing, granular processing, stereo widening, short reverb
- **Articulation**: Short, percussive hits or rapid arpeggios
- **Role**: Textural identity — the "glassy" quality defines breakstep's upper register

#### Kick Drum (Heavy, Broken)
- **Character**: Deep, weighty, not constant — every hit is an event
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered: sub sine layer + mid punch layer + transient click
- **Frequency range**: 30–60 Hz sub, 80–150 Hz body, 3–5 kHz click
- **Processing**: Saturation, transient design, careful EQ sculpting
- **Length**: Long (150–250ms in half-time sections) — fills space in sparse patterns
- **Role**: Rhythmic anchor with physical weight — not timekeeping but impact

#### Processed Percussion
- **Character**: Metallic, synthetic, angular — heavily processed beyond recognition
- **Source**: Acoustic percussion samples run through effects chains
- **Processing**: Bitcrushing, pitch-shifting, granular, reverse reverb, flanger
- **Frequency range**: 1–8 kHz (wide midrange)
- **Articulation**: Short bursts, irregular placement
- **Role**: Textural rhythm — adds unpredictability and angular energy

#### Snare (Half-Time)
- **Character**: Heavy, cracking, reverb-tailed — dubstep influence
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered noise + body + transient
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz body, 1–3 kHz crack, 5–8 kHz air
- **Processing**: Reverb (medium plate), compression, parallel distortion
- **Placement**: Beat 3 in half-time sections (signature placement)
- **Role**: Structural marker — defines the half-time feel

#### Atmospheric Textures
- **Character**: Dark, evolving, granular — background tension
- **Synthesis approach**: Granular synthesis, processed field recordings, long reverb tails
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 4 kHz (wide, diffuse)
- **Processing**: Long reverb (5+ seconds), modulation, volume automation
- **Role**: Mood and space — fills the gaps between sparse rhythmic hits
