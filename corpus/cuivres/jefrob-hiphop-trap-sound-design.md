# Sound Design Palette

## Boom Bap

### Signature Sounds

#### Sampled Drum Break
- **Character**: Gritty, warm, compressed — vinyl-sourced drum hits with natural room tone
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled from vinyl records; chopped via MPC/SP-404; layered with programmed hits
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum — kick 60–100 Hz, snare 200–500 Hz body, cymbals 8–16 kHz
- **Processing**: Bit reduction, vinyl saturation, MPC compression, high-pass rumble removal
- **Role in mix**: Backbone — drives the entire groove; often the loudest element

#### Chopped Soul/Jazz Sample
- **Character**: Warm, nostalgic, melodically complex — fragments of older recordings
- **Synthesis approach**: Vinyl rip → chop in MPC/Ableton → rearrange; pitch-shift ±2–5 semitones
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 8 kHz (mid-focused; rolled-off extremes for warmth)
- **Processing**: Low-pass filter (8–12 kHz), vinyl saturation, light compression, pitch manipulation
- **Role in mix**: Melodic/harmonic foundation — provides chords, melody, and vibe simultaneously

#### Boom Bap Kick
- **Character**: Punchy, mid-forward, warm — NOT sub-heavy; more "boom" than "sub"
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled from breakbeats or layered: sub sine + mid punch sample
- **Frequency range**: Fundamental 60–100 Hz, punch 100–200 Hz, click 2–4 kHz
- **Processing**: Light compression, EQ boost at 80–100 Hz, high-pass at 30 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Foundation — works WITH bass sample rather than replacing it

#### Scratch Textures
- **Character**: Rhythmic, percussive, expressive — DJ turntablism as production element
- **Synthesis approach**: Real turntable scratches recorded; or sample manipulation mimicking scratching
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 10 kHz (mid-heavy, cuts through mix)
- **Processing**: Light compression, sometimes distortion for edge, reverb for space
- **Role in mix**: Transitional/accent element — fills between vocal phrases, marks section changes

#### Vinyl Crackle/Noise Bed
- **Character**: Warm, continuous, nostalgic — the sound of a record playing
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled from vinyl lead-in groove; or generated (iZotope Vinyl, RC-20)
- **Frequency range**: Broadband with emphasis 2–8 kHz (where crackle lives)
- **Processing**: High-pass at 500 Hz, volume automation, sidechain to avoid masking
- **Role in mix**: Textural bed — provides warmth, cohesion, and vintage character

## Trap

### Signature Sounds

#### 808 Bass
- **Character**: Deep, sustained, earth-shaking sine-wave sub with harmonic distortion for speaker presence
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine wave with pitch envelope (fast attack, long decay); or 808 drum machine sample pitched down
- **Frequency range**: Fundamental 30–60 Hz, harmonics via distortion up to 500 Hz
- **Processing**: Saturation/distortion for harmonics, soft clipper, sidechain to kick, glide/portamento
- **Role in mix**: Dominant bass element — fills low end; IS the bass instrument

#### Trap Hi-Hats
- **Character**: Crisp, metallic, rapid — machine-gun rolls with velocity variation
- **Synthesis approach**: 808/909 hi-hat samples; or short noise burst with bandpass filter
- **Frequency range**: 6–16 kHz (bright, cutting)
- **Processing**: Minimal — slight EQ, velocity/volume automation for rolls, panning automation
- **Role in mix**: Primary rhythmic interest — hi-hat patterns define the groove's energy level

#### Dark Piano/Bell Melody
- **Character**: Dark, minor key, crystalline — simple melodies that cut through 808 sub
- **Synthesis approach**: Kontakt piano libraries (dark patch), FM synthesis for bells, Omnisphere
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 6 kHz (clear mid-range, no sub interference)
- **Processing**: Reverb (medium plate), delay (dotted 8th), high-pass at 300 Hz, slight detuning
- **Role in mix**: Melodic hook — provides the memorable element over the 808 foundation

#### Trap Snare/Clap
- **Character**: Hard-hitting, layered, cracking — multiple samples stacked for impact
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered: 808 snare + clap + white noise burst; tuned and phase-aligned
- **Frequency range**: Body 200–400 Hz, crack 2–5 kHz, air 8–12 kHz
- **Processing**: Transient shaping, parallel compression, slight reverb, EQ scoop at 400 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic anchor — the single snare hit on beat 3 defines the half-time feel

#### Atmospheric Pads
- **Character**: Dark, evolving, textural — background atmosphere providing mood
- **Synthesis approach**: Wavetable (Serum), granular, or filtered noise with slow modulation
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 4 kHz (diffuse, wide, not prominent)
- **Processing**: Long reverb (hall/plate), stereo widening, low-pass automation, volume automation
- **Role in mix**: Background atmosphere — 10–15 dB below lead elements; felt more than heard

#### Vocal Chop
- **Character**: Processed, fragmented, rhythmic — human voice as melodic texture
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample vocal phrase → chop into single syllables → pitch/rearrange
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 6 kHz (mid-range focus)
- **Processing**: Auto-Tune/pitch correction, reverb, delay, formant shifting, stutter effects
- **Role in mix**: Secondary melodic element — adds human quality to dark production

## Lo-Fi Hip-Hop

### Signature Sounds

#### Vinyl Crackle Bed
- **Character**: Warm, continuous, enveloping — constant textural bed of vinyl surface noise
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled vinyl noise (needle on record); or plugins (RC-20, iZotope Vinyl, Goodhertz Lossy)
- **Frequency range**: Broadband, emphasis 3–8 kHz
- **Processing**: High-pass at 200 Hz, volume modulated subtly, sidechain ducks slightly on hits
- **Role in mix**: Foundational texture — defines the "lo-fi" aesthetic; always present

#### Rhodes/Wurlitzer Chords
- **Character**: Warm, electric, jazzy — the quintessential lo-fi harmonic instrument
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled (Keyscape, Scarbee) or FM synth emulation; jazz chord voicings
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 4 kHz (warm midrange)
- **Processing**: Chorus, tremolo, tape saturation, bit reduction, pitch wobble (tape speed variance)
- **Role in mix**: Harmonic foundation — provides chords, warmth, and nostalgia

#### Lo-Fi Drum Kit
- **Character**: Soft, muted, imperfect — drums that sound like they're playing through a broken speaker
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled breaks processed through tape emulation, bit reduction, EQ roll-off
- **Frequency range**: Reduced — no content above 12 kHz or below 50 Hz (rolled off both ends)
- **Processing**: Tape saturation, bit crusher (12-bit), low-pass filter at 10–12 kHz, compression
- **Role in mix**: Gentle rhythm — background groove, never aggressive or prominent

#### Jazz Guitar Sample
- **Character**: Warm, nylon or hollow-body tone — gentle, fingerpicked phrases
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled from jazz records; or live-recorded through tape emulation chain
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 5 kHz (no brightness)
- **Processing**: Tape warmth, slight chorus, reverb (room), rolled-off highs at 5–6 kHz
- **Role in mix**: Melodic color — provides gentle melody or rhythmic stabs

#### Ambient Texture (Rain/Nature)
- **Character**: Continuous, calming, atmospheric — nature field recordings as production element
- **Synthesis approach**: Field recordings (rain, birds, wind, café noise); or synthesized with filtered noise
- **Frequency range**: Broadband but low-level
- **Processing**: Volume at -15 to -20 dB, gentle filtering, subtle spatial processing
- **Role in mix**: Background atmosphere — creates sense of place/mood without musical content

## Phonk

### Signature Sounds

#### Memphis Vocal Sample
- **Character**: Dark, aggressive, lo-fi — chopped vocal phrases from 1990s Memphis rap tapes
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled from Three 6 Mafia, Tommy Wright III, DJ Zirk tapes; chopped and looped
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 5 kHz (lo-fi bandwidth; rolled-off extremes)
- **Processing**: Bit reduction, tape saturation, pitch down 1–3 semitones, reverb (dark hall), lo-pass
- **Role in mix**: Melodic hook / vocal texture — the "vocalist" of instrumental phonk

#### Cowbell
- **Character**: Bright, metallic, driving — relentless rhythmic driver at 8th or 16th notes
- **Synthesis approach**: 808 cowbell sample; or synthesized (short metallic hit with pitch envelope)
- **Frequency range**: 600 Hz – 4 kHz fundamental, harmonics to 8 kHz
- **Processing**: Minimal — slight EQ, volume consistency; sometimes pitch variation between hits
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic driver — replaces hi-hats as the primary high-frequency rhythm element

#### Distorted 808
- **Character**: Aggressive, punchy, saturated — 808 sub pushed into distortion for edge
- **Synthesis approach**: Standard 808 sine wave → heavy saturation/distortion chain
- **Frequency range**: Sub 30–60 Hz maintained; distortion adds harmonics 100–500 Hz
- **Processing**: Camel Crusher/Decapitator saturation, soft clipper, short-medium decay (punchier than trap)
- **Role in mix**: Bass foundation — aggressive and punchy rather than smooth and sustained

#### Reverse Vocal Hit
- **Character**: Eerie, dark, transitional — reversed vocal or instrument creating build effect
- **Synthesis approach**: Take any sample → reverse → fade in; or reverse reverb technique
- **Frequency range**: Varies (usually mid-focused 300 Hz – 4 kHz)
- **Processing**: Reverb before reversing, pitch manipulation, lo-fi processing
- **Role in mix**: Transition/accent — marks section changes, adds horror atmosphere

#### Lo-Fi Texture Bed
- **Character**: Dirty, degraded, cassette-quality — tape hiss and degradation as aesthetic
- **Synthesis approach**: Tape emulation plugins; actual cassette recording/re-recording; noise generators
- **Frequency range**: Broadband with reduced high-end (tape roll-off above 10–12 kHz)
- **Processing**: Wow and flutter modulation, dropout simulation, noise layering
- **Role in mix**: Textural foundation — creates the lo-fi "Memphis tape" aesthetic throughout

## UK Drill

### Signature Sounds

#### Sliding 808 Bass
- **Character**: Deep, chromatic, sliding — THE signature UK drill sound; bass that glides between pitches
- **Synthesis approach**: 808 sine with portamento/glide enabled; automated pitch slides (semitone/tone steps)
- **Frequency range**: Sub fundamental 30–60 Hz, distortion harmonics 100–300 Hz
- **Processing**: Saturation for harmonics, sidechain to kick, mono below 100 Hz, glide time 50–150ms
- **Role in mix**: Dominant element — carries both bass AND melodic roles simultaneously

#### Dark Atmospheric Pad
- **Character**: Eerie, sustained, minor key — dark synth pads providing harmonic backdrop
- **Synthesis approach**: Wavetable (Serum, Vital) — dark presets; or filtered saw waves with slow LFO
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 3 kHz (mid-range, diffuse)
- **Processing**: Long reverb (hall), stereo widening, low-pass filter at 3–4 kHz, chorus
- **Role in mix**: Harmonic foundation — sustained pads provide chord information and dark atmosphere

#### UK Drill Hi-Hat
- **Character**: Crisp, complex, triplet-based — rapid pattern changes with velocity expression
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered: closed hat + open hat + ride; sampled or synthesized noise bursts
- **Frequency range**: 5–16 kHz (bright, cutting)
- **Processing**: Velocity automation, panning (slight L/R movement on rolls), EQ boost at 10 kHz
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic complexity — triplet patterns and rolls define UK drill's rhythmic identity

#### Piano Counter-Melody
- **Character**: Dark, eerie, minor — piano melodies that weave between vocal phrases
- **Synthesis approach**: Kontakt piano (dark/felt patches); or Keyscape with heavy EQ/processing
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 4 kHz (avoiding sub range where 808 lives)
- **Processing**: Reverb (medium hall), slight delay, EQ cut below 200 Hz, gentle compression
- **Role in mix**: Melodic interest — provides counter-melody during vocal gaps; eerie atmosphere

#### Drill Snare Stack
- **Character**: Hard, layered, cracking — multiple snare/clap/rim samples stacked for impact
- **Synthesis approach**: Layer: acoustic snare + clap + rimshot + noise transient; tune and phase-align
- **Frequency range**: Body 150–300 Hz, crack 2–6 kHz, top 8–12 kHz
- **Processing**: Transient enhancement, parallel compression, tight reverb (room), EQ at 200 Hz dip
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic anchor — the snare hit defines the groove's bounce pattern

## NY Drill

### Signature Sounds

#### Aggressive Sliding 808
- **Character**: Gritty, distorted, wide-sliding — UK drill 808 with more aggression and wider slides
- **Synthesis approach**: Same as UK drill but more saturation, wider pitch slides (up to octave jumps)
- **Frequency range**: Sub 30–60 Hz; heavy distortion harmonics up to 500 Hz
- **Processing**: More distortion than UK (Camel Crusher, SoundToys Decapitator), faster glide times
- **Role in mix**: Dominant — even more prominent than UK drill; bass IS the track

#### Pop Smoke-Style Vocal Processing
- **Character**: Deep, booming, processed — low-pitched vocal with room presence
- **Synthesis approach**: N/A (vocal processing chain, not synthesis)
- **Frequency range**: 100 Hz – 4 kHz (deep vocal range emphasized)
- **Processing**: Pitch down subtle, heavy compression, plate reverb, de-ess, low-mid boost at 200 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Lead vocal treatment — deep, authoritative, room-filling

#### Dark Piano/Keys
- **Character**: Sparse, eerie, minimal — single piano notes or simple minor chord stabs
- **Synthesis approach**: Kontakt piano libraries; minimal voicings (single notes or dyads)
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz – 3 kHz
- **Processing**: Reverb, slight delay, dark EQ (roll off highs at 4 kHz), compression
- **Role in mix**: Sparse melodic element — less melodically active than UK drill

#### Sample Chop (R&B/Soul)
- **Character**: Chopped, pitched, rhythmic — R&B/soul vocal or instrument fragment over drill drums
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample R&B track → chop 1–4 beat sections → pitch-shift → layer over drill beat
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 8 kHz (retains sample's original spectrum)
- **Processing**: Pitch shift, reverb, vinyl saturation, chop editing, sidechain
- **Role in mix**: Primary melodic hook — the sample IS the melody (sample drill subgenre)

#### Gunshot/Impact SFX
- **Character**: Dark, aggressive, transitional — sound effects as percussive/dramatic elements
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled (movie sound libraries, foley); or layered noise + sub impact
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum impact (20 Hz – 10 kHz)
- **Processing**: Reverb (large space), compression, EQ boost at sub and 2–4 kHz crack
- **Role in mix**: Accent/transition — marks section beginnings, fills between phrases

## Chicago Drill

### Signature Sounds

#### Minimal Dark Piano
- **Character**: Sparse, cold, menacing — single piano notes in the bass register; no chords
- **Synthesis approach**: Kontakt (dark/muted piano patches); few notes, widely spaced
- **Frequency range**: 100–500 Hz (low piano range; dark, rumbling)
- **Processing**: Minimal reverb (dry and immediate), slight saturation, low-pass at 3 kHz
- **Role in mix**: Primary melodic element — 2–4 notes repeating; minimalism equals menace

#### Heavy 808 Sub
- **Character**: Deep, booming, sustained — maximum sub-bass weight with long decay
- **Synthesis approach**: Pure sine or triangle wave with long decay envelope (800ms+)
- **Frequency range**: 30–60 Hz fundamental; very few upper harmonics
- **Processing**: Minimal distortion (cleaner than modern trap); long sustain, mono, sidechain to kick
- **Role in mix**: Foundation — fills all low-end space; felt in chest/body

#### Raw Snare
- **Character**: Hard, dry, immediate — no frills, maximum impact
- **Synthesis approach**: Single snare sample (acoustic or 808); no layering (contrast to UK drill)
- **Frequency range**: Body 150–300 Hz, crack 2–4 kHz
- **Processing**: Almost none — dry, loud, hard; maybe slight compression
- **Role in mix**: The hit — stark, isolated, maximum impact through simplicity

#### String/Synth Stab
- **Character**: Dark, short, orchestral — string section stab or dark synth hit
- **Synthesis approach**: Orchestral sample library (short articulation); or saw wave synth with fast decay
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 2 kHz (dark, not bright)
- **Processing**: Short reverb, slight compression, low-pass filter
- **Role in mix**: Accent — sparse stabs that punctuate the minimal arrangement

#### Silence/Space
- **Character**: Intentional absence — silence as a compositional element
- **Synthesis approach**: N/A — the deliberate removal of sound
- **Frequency range**: N/A
- **Processing**: N/A — mastered to maintain dynamic range through silent sections
- **Role in mix**: Contrast — makes 808 hits land harder; silence creates tension before impact

## Cloud Rap

### Signature Sounds

#### Reverb Wash Pad
- **Character**: Ethereal, massive, enveloping — synth pad drenched in reverb creating sonic clouds
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw/square pad → massive hall reverb (100% wet) → the reverb IS the sound
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 6 kHz (wide, diffuse, filling the stereo field)
- **Processing**: Hall reverb (5–10s decay), shimmer, chorus, stereo widening, volume swells
- **Role in mix**: Foundation — creates the "cloud" in cloud rap; everything sits within this wash

#### Pitched Vocal Texture
- **Character**: Ethereal, processed, otherworldly — human voice transformed into ambient texture
- **Synthesis approach**: Vocal sample → pitch shift (up octave or down), heavy reverb, granular stretch
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 8 kHz (mid-high, floating)
- **Processing**: Pitch shift, granular, massive reverb, delay (long feedback), formant shift
- **Role in mix**: Textural hook — provides human quality without being a traditional "vocal"

#### Soft Sub Bass
- **Character**: Gentle, warm, pillowy — sub bass that supports without dominating
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine wave with gentle envelope (slow attack, long release)
- **Frequency range**: 40–80 Hz (pure sub, no harmonics)
- **Processing**: Minimal — slight compression, sidechain to kick (gentle), mono
- **Role in mix**: Low-end warmth — present but not aggressive; supportive foundation

#### Ambient Texture/Field Recording
- **Character**: Spacious, atmospheric, natural — rain, wind, distant sounds creating environment
- **Synthesis approach**: Field recordings or granular synthesis of natural sounds
- **Frequency range**: Broadband (unfocused, environmental)
- **Processing**: Reverb, stereo placement, volume automation, gentle filtering
- **Role in mix**: Atmosphere — creates sense of space and dreaminess

#### Delay-Processed Melody
- **Character**: Ethereal, cascading, hypnotic — simple melody multiplied by long delay
- **Synthesis approach**: Simple synth melody (3–5 notes) → long delay (dotted 8th/triplet, high feedback)
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 4 kHz (mid-range)
- **Processing**: Delay (70–80% feedback), reverb, filter on delay returns (darker each repeat)
- **Role in mix**: Melodic element — delay creates complexity from simplicity

## Memphis / Horrorcore

### Signature Sounds

#### Horror Movie Sample
- **Character**: Dark, cinematic, unsettling — dialogue or music from horror films
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled from horror film soundtracks, dialogue, screams; chopped and looped
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 6 kHz (mid-focused, lo-fi)
- **Processing**: Lo-fi degradation, pitch down, reverb (dark hall), tape saturation, loop
- **Role in mix**: Atmospheric/melodic hook — sets the dark, occult mood

#### Triple-Time Hi-Hat
- **Character**: Machine-gun, relentless, hypnotic — rapid triplet patterns defining Memphis rhythm
- **Synthesis approach**: 808/909 hi-hat sample sequenced in triplet 16ths; or drum machine programming
- **Frequency range**: 6–14 kHz (bright, cutting)
- **Processing**: Minimal — volume consistency, slight lo-fi degradation for tape feel
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic signature — THE defining element of Memphis production

#### Lo-Fi 808 Kick
- **Character**: Boomy, warm, tape-saturated — 808 kick processed through cassette medium
- **Synthesis approach**: Standard 808 kick → recorded to cassette → re-sampled; or tape emulation
- **Frequency range**: 40–100 Hz (sub reduced by tape; more mid-bass warmth)
- **Processing**: Tape saturation (wow/flutter), reduced sub (cassette can't reproduce), compression
- **Role in mix**: Bass foundation — boomy and warm rather than clean and sub-heavy

#### Dark Organ/Keys
- **Character**: Church organ or horror keyboard — dark, sustained, ominous tones
- **Synthesis approach**: Hammond organ samples (dark registration); or analog synth with organ waveform
- **Frequency range**: 100 Hz – 3 kHz (full-bodied, dark)
- **Processing**: Distortion, dark reverb, tremolo, lo-fi degradation
- **Role in mix**: Harmonic/atmospheric element — provides dark sustained chords

#### Vocal Ad-lib/Scream
- **Character**: Dark, aggressive, percussive — short vocal shouts, screams, or ad-libs used rhythmically
- **Synthesis approach**: Recorded vocals (MC screams, ad-libs); or sampled from Memphis tapes
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz – 6 kHz
- **Processing**: Distortion, reverb (short/dark), pitch manipulation, stutter/repeat
- **Role in mix**: Percussive accent — fills gaps, marks transitions, adds aggression

## Rage / Hyperpop-Trap

### Signature Sounds

#### Distorted 808 Bass
- **Character**: Extremely distorted, clipping, aggressive — 808 pushed far beyond clean territory
- **Synthesis approach**: 808 sine → chain of distortion (Camel Crusher → Soundgoodizer → Soft Clipper)
- **Frequency range**: Sub maintained at 30–50 Hz; distortion harmonics fill 100 Hz – 2 kHz
- **Processing**: Multiple distortion stages, soft clipper, bass enhancer, extreme compression
- **Role in mix**: Dominant — fills most of the frequency spectrum; IS the track's energy

#### Chaotic Synth Lead
- **Character**: Glitchy, distorted, video-game-inspired — rapid arpeggios and unpredictable modulation
- **Synthesis approach**: Serum/Vital — detuned saws with fast LFO, arpeggiator, FM modulation
- **Frequency range**: 1–10 kHz (mid-high; cuts through distorted 808)
- **Processing**: Distortion, bitcrusher, fast tremolo/gate, stereo widening, reverb
- **Role in mix**: Energy/melodic chaos — provides the "rage" quality through unpredictability

#### Pitched/Processed Snare
- **Character**: Non-traditional, pitched, effected — snare as a designed sound rather than acoustic sample
- **Synthesis approach**: Layer: noise burst + pitched sine + transient; or heavily processed acoustic snare
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 8 kHz (wider than normal due to processing)
- **Processing**: Pitch envelope, distortion, stutter effects, reverb → distortion chain
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic anchor — maintains half-time feel despite surrounding chaos

#### Reverse/Glitch Effects
- **Character**: Digital, broken, unpredictable — glitch, stutter, and reverse effects as musical elements
- **Synthesis approach**: Real-time glitch plugins (Glitch2, Effectrix); or manual audio chopping/reversing
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum (unpredictable)
- **Processing**: Beat-repeat, stutter, reverse, pitch-shift, granular, random modulation
- **Role in mix**: Energy peaks — deployed at transitions and climactic moments for maximum chaos

#### Video Game Synth Arpeggio
- **Character**: Chiptune-influenced, rapid, bright — 8-bit/16-bit game music inspired arpeggios
- **Synthesis approach**: Square/pulse waves (Chiptune plugin or Serum with digital waveforms); fast arpeggiator
- **Frequency range**: 1–8 kHz (bright, digital)
- **Processing**: Bitcrusher (for authenticity), fast delay, minimal reverb (keeping it tight/direct)
- **Role in mix**: Constant energy layer — rapid arpeggios maintain intensity even when drums simplify

## Plugg

### Signature Sounds

#### Flute Melody
- **Character**: Bright, airy, singing — legato flute sound carrying the primary melody
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based flute (Kontakt, East West); or Serum with breathy oscillator + noise
- **Frequency range**: 800 Hz – 6 kHz (mid-high, clear, never sub-range)
- **Processing**: Reverb (medium plate), slight delay, EQ cut below 500 Hz, gentle saturation
- **Role in mix**: PRIMARY element — the flute melody IS the plugg beat; everything else supports it

#### Bell/Music Box
- **Character**: Crystalline, delicate, nostalgic — music box or bell providing secondary melody
- **Synthesis approach**: FM synthesis (bell partials); or sample-based (glockenspiel, celesta, music box)
- **Frequency range**: 2–10 kHz (high, bright, sparkly)
- **Processing**: Reverb (large space), EQ boost at 3–5 kHz for presence, gentle compression
- **Role in mix**: Secondary melody — complements flute; adds sparkle and space

#### Clean 808 Bass
- **Character**: Clean, melodic, bouncy — tuned 808 with less distortion than trap; musical bass line
- **Synthesis approach**: 808 sine with moderate sustain (shorter than trap); clear pitch definition
- **Frequency range**: 40–100 Hz fundamental; minimal harmonics (clean)
- **Processing**: Light saturation only, sidechain to kick, mono, tuned carefully to melody
- **Role in mix**: Bass melody — bouncy, playful pattern that complements the flute/bell

#### Soft Snare/Clap
- **Character**: Gentle, quiet, unobtrusive — the snare whispers rather than hits
- **Synthesis approach**: Soft acoustic snare sample or finger snap; low velocity
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz – 6 kHz (full range but low level)
- **Processing**: Minimal — maybe slight reverb, kept quiet in mix (-6 to -10 dB below lead)
- **Role in mix**: Gentle timekeeping — marks half-time without dominating; background role

#### Ambient Reverb Tail
- **Character**: Spacious, decaying, dreamy — long reverb tails on flute/bell creating atmosphere
- **Synthesis approach**: Not a discrete sound — result of processing chain on melodies (100% wet bus)
- **Frequency range**: Full spectrum (inherits from source, with high-end decay)
- **Processing**: Long reverb (3–5s decay), low-pass filter on return, modulation (chorus on reverb)
- **Role in mix**: Space/atmosphere — fills gaps between melody notes; creates the "spacious" plugg feel
