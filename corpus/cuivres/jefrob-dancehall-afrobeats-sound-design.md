# Sound Design Palette

## Dancehall

### Signature Sounds

#### Riddim Bass (808/Synth Bass)
- **Character**: Deep, round, syncopated — the low-end foundation of every riddim
- **Synthesis approach**: 808 sub-bass (modern) or Casio-derived synth bass (classic digital era)
- **Frequency range**: 40–120 Hz fundamental, harmonic content up to 300 Hz
- **Processing**: Light saturation for warmth, sidechain to kick, mono below 150 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Carries the entire low-end — often the most prominent element after vocals
- **Variations**: Short punchy (classic) vs. sustained slide (modern trap-influenced)

#### Riddim Keys / Organ
- **Character**: Warm, muted organ or electric piano — plays the offbeat "skank"
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based organ (Farfisa, Hammond) or EP (Rhodes, Wurlitzer)
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz–2 kHz (midrange only — no sub content)
- **Processing**: Band-pass filter, light chorus, short reverb (room)
- **Role in mix**: Defines the harmonic bed and rhythmic offbeat character
- **Articulation**: Staccato, muted — hits on offbeats and releases immediately

#### Kick Drum
- **Character**: Deep, round, soft attack — NOT clicky or aggressive
- **Frequency range**: 50–80 Hz fundamental, minimal click above 3 kHz
- **Processing**: Minimal — slight compression, HPF at 30 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Anchors the one-drop or steppers pattern
- **Distinction**: Less present than in EDM — bass guitar/synth carries the low energy

#### Snare / Cross-Stick
- **Character**: Thin, woody cross-stick or rimshot — not a full snare crack
- **Frequency range**: 800 Hz–5 kHz (midrange crack, no sub weight)
- **Processing**: Dry, slight room reverb, tight compression
- **Role in mix**: Marks beat 3 (one-drop) — piercing but not dominant
- **Source**: Acoustic rimshot samples or classic drum machine (Oberheim DMX, LinnDrum)

#### Horn Stabs
- **Character**: Bright, punchy brass hits — short, accented
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based brass (recorded horn sections) or synth brass patches
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz–6 kHz (bright, cutting midrange)
- **Processing**: Compression for punch, short reverb, slight EQ presence boost
- **Role in mix**: Accent marker — signals section changes, adds energy to hooks

#### Digital Riddim Synths
- **Character**: Bright, digital, thin — 1985 Casio/Yamaha era sounds
- **Synthesis approach**: FM synthesis (DX7-style), early digital workstations
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz–8 kHz (thin, bright, cutting)
- **Processing**: Minimal — the raw digital character IS the sound
- **Role in mix**: Melodic riff or textural element — nostalgia factor in modern production
- **Reference**: The "Sleng Teng" riddim Casio MT-40 preset that started it all

## Modern Afrobeats

### Signature Sounds

#### Shaker (Primary Groove Element)
- **Character**: Bright, consistent, airy — egg shaker or cabasa
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based (single shaker hit, sequenced as 16ths)
- **Frequency range**: 6–14 kHz (entirely high-frequency, no body)
- **Processing**: High-pass at 5 kHz, minimal compression, very light reverb
- **Role in mix**: The heartbeat — provides continuous 16th-note groove
- **Velocity**: ±10–15% variation per hit for human feel

#### Log Drum / Talking Drum
- **Character**: Wooden, pitched, percussive — melodic rhythm instrument
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based (one-shot hits), pitch envelope for "talking" quality
- **Frequency range**: 80–300 Hz fundamental, harmonics up to 600 Hz
- **Processing**: Light saturation, EQ to avoid conflict with bass
- **Tuning**: Tuned to track key — this is a melodic element
- **Role in mix**: Melodic percussion — bridges rhythm and harmony

#### Nylon Guitar
- **Character**: Warm, organic, picked patterns — West African highlife heritage
- **Synthesis approach**: Live recording or high-quality sample libraries (Ample Sound, NI)
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz–6 kHz (full midrange presence)
- **Processing**: Light compression, short reverb (room/plate), gentle high-pass at 150 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Harmonic rhythm provider — fills space between vocal phrases
- **Articulation**: Fingerpicked arpeggios, muted strums, single-note riffs

#### Kick Drum
- **Character**: Light, bouncy, not aggressive — supports rather than dominates
- **Frequency range**: 50–100 Hz fundamental, soft click at 2–3 kHz
- **Processing**: Minimal processing — natural, not heavily compressed
- **Role in mix**: Background anchor — you feel it more than hear it
- **Length**: Medium decay, round character

#### Synth Pads (Warm)
- **Character**: Warm, subtle, evolving — background harmonic fill
- **Synthesis approach**: Analog-modeled soft sawtooth or string-like pads
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz–3 kHz (warm midrange, never bright)
- **Processing**: Long reverb, light chorus, low-pass filter, volume automation
- **Role in mix**: Harmonic glue — sits well below vocals and guitar, barely noticed

#### Percussion Ensemble (Congas/Bongos/Shekere)
- **Character**: Organic, warm, West African timbres
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based (multi-velocity, multiple articulations)
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz–8 kHz (depending on instrument — congas lower, shekere higher)
- **Processing**: Light room reverb for cohesion, grouped bus compression (gentle)
- **Role in mix**: Polyrhythmic texture — multiple layers interlocking

## Afroswing

### Signature Sounds

#### Bouncy Kick (2-Step Influenced)
- **Character**: Punchy but light — UK garage weight, not trap weight
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered samples: sub layer + mid punch layer
- **Frequency range**: 50–100 Hz sub, 100–200 Hz body, 2–4 kHz click
- **Processing**: Transient shaping, subtle sidechain from other elements
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic engine — the 2-step syncopation drives the bounce
- **Distinction**: More punch than Afrobeats kick, less aggressive than grime

#### Snare/Clap
- **Character**: Snappy, tight, bright — UK urban production standard
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz–8 kHz (wide-band snap)
- **Processing**: Short plate reverb, transient enhancement, layered clap + snare
- **Role in mix**: Strong backbeat on 2 and 4 — more prominent than in Afrobeats

#### Guitar Riffs (Electric/Semi-Acoustic)
- **Character**: Clean, muted, rhythmic — UK production polish on Afrobeats guitar
- **Synthesis approach**: Live recording or processed sample loops
- **Frequency range**: 250 Hz–5 kHz (focused midrange)
- **Processing**: Chorus, short delay, compression, HPF at 200 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Primary melodic/harmonic element — drives the track's identity
- **Articulation**: Palm-muted rhythmic patterns, chicken-picking, arpeggiated chords

#### Hi-Hats (UK Garage Style)
- **Character**: Crisp, swung, with open hat accents
- **Frequency range**: 6–14 kHz
- **Processing**: Light filtering, panning automation, velocity programming
- **Role in mix**: Energy and groove — open hats mark accents, closed hats fill
- **Swing**: More swung than straight Afrobeats hats

#### Synth Bass
- **Character**: Warm, round, 808-adjacent but more melodic
- **Synthesis approach**: Analog-modeled sub synth or layered 808 with synth
- **Frequency range**: 40–150 Hz fundamental, harmonics to 400 Hz
- **Processing**: Saturation for warmth, sidechain to kick, mono below 120 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Low-end foundation — more melodic movement than pure 808

#### Vocal Chops/Ad-libs
- **Character**: Chopped vocal samples as rhythmic/melodic elements
- **Synthesis approach**: Sampled vocal phrases, sliced and rearranged
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz–6 kHz
- **Processing**: Pitch correction, time-stretch, reverb throws, filter automation
- **Role in mix**: Textural hooks — fills between lead vocal phrases

## Dancehall Pop

### Signature Sounds

#### Pop-Weighted Kick
- **Character**: Clean, punchy, polished — pop production standard
- **Synthesis approach**: Layered: sub (sine) + body (sample) + click (transient)
- **Frequency range**: 40–80 Hz sub, 100–200 Hz body, 3–5 kHz click
- **Processing**: Multi-band compression, careful EQ, sidechain to bass
- **Role in mix**: Solid foundation — heard clearly on all playback systems

#### Tropical Percussion
- **Character**: Bright, playful, Caribbean flavor in pop context
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based — bongos, timbales, shakers, steel drums
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz–10 kHz (high-mid content, no sub)
- **Processing**: Short reverb, EQ for clarity, grouped and compressed lightly
- **Role in mix**: Caribbean "color" — gives the pop track its dancehall identity
- **Volume**: Subtle — felt as texture rather than driving element

#### Synth Plucks (Tropical)
- **Character**: Bright, short, marimba-like or guitar-like plucked synths
- **Synthesis approach**: Wavetable or FM synth with fast decay envelope
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz–8 kHz
- **Processing**: Delay (dotted 8th), reverb, HPF at 400 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Melodic hook element — the "tropical" signature sound
- **Reference**: Thomas Jack, Kygo influence on dancehall-pop production

#### Sub Bass
- **Character**: Clean, deep, sustained — pop-standard low end
- **Synthesis approach**: Sine wave sub or 808 with controlled decay
- **Frequency range**: 30–80 Hz (pure sub content)
- **Processing**: Compression, limiting, careful sidechain to kick
- **Role in mix**: Low-end foundation — consistent and powerful

#### Dancehall Vocal Processing
- **Character**: Clear, upfront, modern pop vocal chain with Caribbean delivery
- **Processing chain**: Compression → de-essing → EQ → saturation → delay/reverb
- **Effects**: Vocal delays (rhythmic throws), Auto-Tune (subtle), harmonies
- **Role in mix**: DOMINANT — the vocal IS the track in pop-dancehall
- **Layering**: Lead + doubles + harmonies + ad-libs (full production)

#### Offbeat Guitars/Keys
- **Character**: Muted acoustic guitar or Rhodes playing the dancehall skank
- **Synthesis approach**: Live guitar or Rhodes/EP plugin
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz–4 kHz
- **Processing**: High-pass, compression, short reverb, stereo widening
- **Role in mix**: Provides the dancehall rhythmic identity — the offbeat "bubble"

## Soca

### Signature Sounds

#### Driving Kick
- **Character**: Powerful, punchy, aggressive — hits hard at high tempo
- **Frequency range**: 50–100 Hz fundamental, strong click at 3–5 kHz
- **Processing**: Heavy compression, transient shaping (attack boost), HPF at 35 Hz
- **Role in mix**: Driving force — must cut through at 145–165 BPM
- **Length**: Short decay — tight and punchy for the fast tempo

#### Brass Section
- **Character**: Bright, bold, live-sounding brass stabs and lines
- **Synthesis approach**: Live brass recordings or high-quality sample libraries (CineBrass, NI)
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz–8 kHz (full brass spectrum)
- **Processing**: Compression for consistency, room reverb for cohesion, EQ presence at 2–4 kHz
- **Role in mix**: CENTRAL — brass defines soca's energy and carnival character
- **Articulation**: Staccato stabs, unison lines, call-and-response patterns

#### Snare (Carnival Weight)
- **Character**: Loud, aggressive, cutting — carnival marching snare quality
- **Frequency range**: 200 Hz body, 1–5 kHz crack, 6–10 kHz air
- **Processing**: Heavy compression, parallel processing for weight, short reverb
- **Role in mix**: Powerful 2 and 4 — anchor for the driving pulse
- **Layering**: Multiple snare samples layered for weight and brightness

#### Iron/Bell
- **Character**: Metallic, bright, persistent — carnival percussion essential
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based (iron, cowbell, agogô)
- **Frequency range**: 2–8 kHz (cutting, metallic midrange)
- **Processing**: Minimal — natural metallic character, maybe slight compression
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic drive — constant offbeat or pattern creating energy

#### Steel Pan (When Present)
- **Character**: Bright, melodic, Caribbean — carnival cultural marker
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample libraries or synthesized (metallic with pitch)
- **Frequency range**: 300 Hz–10 kHz (bright, full-range melodic instrument)
- **Processing**: Light reverb, EQ for presence, stereo spread
- **Role in mix**: Melodic hook or fill — cultural identity marker

#### Synth Stabs
- **Character**: Bright, punchy, energetic — reinforces brass energy electronically
- **Synthesis approach**: Saw-wave stabs with fast attack, short decay
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz–8 kHz
- **Processing**: Compression, saturation, short reverb
- **Role in mix**: Electronic energy layer — supports brass or replaces it in modern soca

## Kuduro

### Signature Sounds

#### Batida Kick
- **Character**: Hard, punchy, aggressive — hits like a fist
- **Synthesis approach**: 808-style kick with boosted transient, or sharp sample
- **Frequency range**: 50–100 Hz fundamental, aggressive click at 3–6 kHz
- **Processing**: Compression (heavy), saturation/distortion, transient shaping
- **Role in mix**: Driving force — every hit must be felt physically
- **Length**: Very short decay — punchy, not boomy

#### 808 Bass
- **Character**: Short, aggressive, punchy — not the sustained 808 of trap
- **Synthesis approach**: Classic 808 bass drum synthesis, tuned to root note
- **Frequency range**: 40–80 Hz fundamental, distortion harmonics up to 500 Hz
- **Processing**: Heavy saturation/distortion, short decay, sidechain to kick
- **Decay**: 100–250ms (SHORT — must be percussive, not sustained)
- **Role in mix**: Low-end punch — works with kick as a unit

#### Metallic Percussion (Agogô/Iron)
- **Character**: Bright, cutting, metallic — industrial quality
- **Synthesis approach**: Sample-based agogô, cowbell, or metallic found-sounds
- **Frequency range**: 2–10 kHz (all high-mid/high content)
- **Processing**: Minimal — raw is the aesthetic. Maybe slight compression.
- **Role in mix**: Rhythmic counter-pattern — the melodic-rhythmic hook of many tracks

#### Hi-Hat (Machine-Gun)
- **Character**: Constant, mechanical, thin — relentless 16th-note pattern
- **Synthesis approach**: TR-808/909 hi-hat or thin metallic sample
- **Frequency range**: 6–14 kHz
- **Processing**: High-pass at 5 kHz, minimal processing — raw and mechanical
- **Role in mix**: Constant rhythmic grid — never stops, provides machine energy
- **Feel**: STRAIGHT — no swing, grid-locked, robotic

#### Synth Hook
- **Character**: Simple, bright, repetitive — the melodic earworm
- **Synthesis approach**: Basic waveforms (saw/square), simple filter envelope
- **Frequency range**: 500 Hz–5 kHz
- **Processing**: Minimal — clarity over effects. Maybe slight delay.
- **Pattern**: 1–2 bar loop, EXTREMELY repetitive — the hook IS the track
- **Role in mix**: Melodic identity — the one thing you remember after hearing the track

#### Vocal Chants
- **Character**: Raw, rhythmic, rapid-fire — Portuguese/Kimbundu delivery
- **Processing**: Minimal — upfront, clear, natural. Light compression at most.
- **Role in mix**: Primary vocal content — rapid delivery creates its own percussion
- **Layering**: Call-and-response, multiple voices chanting in unison
- **Effect**: The vocal IS a percussion instrument in kuduro
