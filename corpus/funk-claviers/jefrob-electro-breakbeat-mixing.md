# Mixing & Mastering Benchmarks

## Classic Detroit Electro

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–60 Hz | 808 kick fundamental, bass sub | Present, controlled — not overpowering |
| 60–150 Hz | 808 kick body, bass sequence fundamental | DOMINANT — warmth and weight |
| 150–400 Hz | Bass harmonics, 808 tom resonance | Moderate — watch for mud |
| 400 Hz–2 kHz | Arpeggios, vocoder body, cowbell | Clear, dry presence |
| 2–6 kHz | Vocoder consonants, hi-hat body, clap snap | Crisp but not harsh |
| 6–12 kHz | Hi-hat air, cowbell harmonics | Present, metallic |
| 12–20 kHz | Minimal — genre doesn't emphasize ultra-highs | Subtle or absent |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: 808 kick, bass sequence, clap/snare, vocoder (center)
- **Narrow stereo**: Arpeggios (slight detune width), pads
- **Wide stereo**: Rare — classic electro is mostly mono/narrow
- **Overall width**: NARROW — mono-compatible is critical (genre emerged in mono-mixed era)
- **Mono compatibility**: Essential — many reference tracks were mixed in mono

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS integrated
- **Target LUFS (club/DJ)**: -9 to -10 LUFS
- **Dynamic range**: 8–12 dB (relatively dynamic — groove needs breathing room)
- **Bus compression**: Minimal (1–2 dB GR max) — electro should feel raw
- **Limiter**: Gentle — avoid squashing the 808 transients
- **Philosophy**: Raw and punchy — the 808 dynamics ARE the expression

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- Drexciya — "Wavejumper" (balanced 808 pattern, clear arpeggio, tight mono image)
- Cybotron — "Clear" (dry, upfront, minimal processing)
- Aux 88 — "My AUX Mind" (raw 808 balance, heavy low end, minimal reverb)

### Common Mix Mistakes
- Over-compressing the 808 kick (kills the pitch envelope character)
- Adding reverb to 808 elements (genre is DRY — reverb muddies the syncopation)
- Kick and bass fighting for the same frequencies (should interlock rhythmically, not EQ-compete)
- Making the mix too wide/modern (lose the raw, focused character of the originals)
- Over-EQing — the 808 sounds should be presented close to raw

### EQ Signature Moves
- High-pass everything except kick and bass at 100–150 Hz (keep low end clean)
- Gentle boost on 808 kick at 60–80 Hz for sub weight
- Cut muddiness in bass sequence at 200–300 Hz
- Presence boost on vocoder at 2–3 kHz for intelligibility
- Low-pass the entire mix at 14–16 kHz (genre doesn't need air/sparkle — raw aesthetic)

## Nu-Skool Breakbeat

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–50 Hz | Sub-bass fundamental | HEAVY — designed for club subs |
| 50–150 Hz | Bass harmonics, kick fundamental | Full and present |
| 150–400 Hz | Break snare body, bass upper harmonics | Controlled — avoid boxiness |
| 400 Hz–2 kHz | Break mid-character, vocal chops, synth stabs | Clear, punchy |
| 2–6 kHz | Snare crack, hi-hat body, synth presence | Bright, aggressive |
| 6–12 kHz | Hi-hat air, break shimmer, vocal air | Open, present |
| 12–20 kHz | Air, cymbal decay | Present — modern production |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: Kick, sub-bass, snare center (critical club elements)
- **Narrow stereo**: Break overheads, mid-bass harmonics
- **Wide stereo**: FX, atmosphere, delay returns, vocal chop reverb
- **Overall width**: Moderate-wide — wider than Detroit electro, club-ready stereo image
- **Mono compatibility**: Critical for sub frequencies; stereo FX can collapse acceptably

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS integrated
- **Target LUFS (club)**: -7 to -9 LUFS (LOUD — competitive with other club genres)
- **Dynamic range**: 5–8 dB (compressed for impact — drops need to hit)
- **Bus compression**: Medium-heavy (3–5 dB GR on break bus), parallel compression common
- **Limiter**: Moderate — push for club loudness while preserving break transients
- **Philosophy**: Punchy and LOUD — breaks must compete with modern bass music

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- Stanton Warriors — "Get Wild" (bass weight vs. break clarity balance)
- Plump DJs — "Gobbstopper" (loud, punchy, compressed breaks)
- Chemical Brothers — "Block Rockin' Beats" (dynamic range, stereo spread)

### Common Mix Mistakes
- Sub-bass masking the kick (need clear separation via sidechain or EQ)
- Breaks too thin/lacking body (over-high-passing, losing the 200–500 Hz warmth)
- Builds too loud relative to drops (drop should feel like a RELEASE of energy, not quieter)
- Too much reverb on breaks (breaks need to be dry and punchy — reverb on FX only)
- Synth stabs competing with vocal chops in 1–3 kHz range

### EQ Signature Moves
- High-pass breaks at 80–100 Hz (let dedicated sub-bass own the lows)
- Boost snare crack at 3–4 kHz for cut-through
- Notch bass and kick separation at 50–80 Hz (sidechain + EQ combo)
- Cut break mud at 200–300 Hz (2–3 dB, wide Q)
- Presence boost on vocal chops at 2–4 kHz
- Low-shelf boost on sub-bass at 40–50 Hz for club weight

## Electro-Funk

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–60 Hz | 808 kick sub, bass fundamental | Present but controlled |
| 60–200 Hz | Bass body (the melodic bass lives here), kick body | FULL — bass is king |
| 200–500 Hz | Bass upper harmonics, clavinet low-mids, pad warmth | Rich, warm |
| 500 Hz–2 kHz | Synth stabs, talk-box body, clavinet | Clear, funky midrange |
| 2–5 kHz | Talk-box presence, snare snap, synth brightness | Present, not harsh |
| 5–10 kHz | Hi-hat, clap air, synth shimmer | Bright but smooth |
| 10–20 kHz | Air, cymbal shimmer | Moderate — warm genre |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: Kick, bass, lead vocal/talk-box
- **Narrow stereo**: Synth stabs (slight spread), snare/clap
- **Wide stereo**: Congas/percussion (panned), pad layers, delay returns
- **Overall width**: Moderate-wide — wider than Detroit electro (funk uses stereo for groove)
- **Mono compatibility**: Important for bass/kick; wider elements add flavor

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS integrated
- **Target LUFS (club)**: -9 to -10 LUFS
- **Dynamic range**: 8–11 dB (preserve funk dynamics — groove needs push/pull)
- **Bus compression**: Light-medium (2–3 dB GR), parallel on drums
- **Limiter**: Gentle — don't squash the funk pocket (timing dynamics matter)
- **Philosophy**: Warm and dynamic — the groove breathes, dynamics are expression

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- Breakbot — "Baby I'm Yours" (warm, full bass, polished retro production)
- Chromeo — "Fancy Footwork" (balanced funk mix, talk-box forward)
- Dam-Funk — "Brookside Park" (analog warmth, bass-forward, wide stereo)

### Common Mix Mistakes
- Bass too sub-heavy/inaudible on small speakers (electro-funk bass needs midrange harmonics)
- Talk-box/vocoder buried under synth stabs (should be lead element)
- Over-compressing drums (kills the funk groove dynamics)
- Mix too bright/modern (electro-funk should sound warm, not clinical)
- Not leaving space for the bass melody to breathe (competing low-mid elements)

### EQ Signature Moves
- Boost bass synth at 100–200 Hz for body/warmth (the melodic bass range)
- Cut synth stabs at 150–250 Hz to avoid competing with bass
- Boost talk-box presence at 2–3 kHz for clarity
- Gentle high-shelf at 8 kHz on the master for warmth (roll off extreme highs)
- Mid-scoop competing elements at 400–600 Hz to prevent mud
- Boost 808 kick at 60–80 Hz, cut at 200–300 Hz for clean sub
