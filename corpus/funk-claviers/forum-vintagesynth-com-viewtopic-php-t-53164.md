### Re: How can I achieve the Zapp & Roger bass synth funk sound? 

 Quote 

 Post 
 by Solderman » Mon Dec 14, 2009 8:51 pm

 I can do this bass sound on my Moog Voyager with two oscillators: square and a triangle an octave above it. Square volume is roughly half the triangle's. Filter resonance is at a moderate level, with noticable envelope attack for that "wah" effect. Fairly wide filter envelope depth with a medium decay rate that drops pretty low. On further thought, this might actually be a layered bass, with the second having no resonance and mostly filtered down to the fundamental.

Now what I want to know is, how the h**l did Zapp and Roger do their vocoding techniques? And that one big snare they always used, I want that sample!

 Roger Troutman/Trautman used a Yamaha DX-100 through a Heil Talkbox for the "vocoder" sound. If you dig around on YouTube etc, I'm pretty sure you'll find examples of the technique.

The "massive snare" was most likely a LINNDrum cranked up in the mix with some compression to make it sizzle.

### Re: How can I achieve the Zapp & Roger bass synth funk sound? 

 Quote 

 Post 
 by nvbrkr » Tue Dec 22, 2009 9:51 am

 Here's what P-Thugg from Chromeo said about the talkbox thing:
 
With the talkbox, what do you use for the sound source?

P-Thugg: An old Yamaha DX100, which is what Roger from Zapp used. I became friends with one of the guys from his band, and he sent me the patches by email as MIDI Sys Ex files. I just dumped ’em in, and there were all his sounds. I couldn’t believe it. The whole first row of buttons is one sound that’s transposed ¬– since he had to concentrate on singing with the talkbox, he played in a comfortable key and selected the different patches for the different songs.

As far as the basses go, at least the Minneapolis end of 80s synth-y funk goes, I don't think that stuff was based around Moogs at all. I think many artists / bands just started using their polysynths for that purpose as well.
