# Music Theory & Harmony

## Deep House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Dorian mode (warm minor with raised 6th), Natural minor (Aeolian)
- **Secondary**: Mixolydian (for major-key soulful tracks), Minor pentatonic
- **Occasional**: Lydian (dreamy, floating quality), Melodic minor
- **Avoid**: Phrygian, Locrian — too dark/tense for deep house warmth

### Common Keys
- Eb minor, Ab minor, F minor, Bb minor (flat keys — comfortable for jazz voicings)
- Major: Eb major, Ab major, Db major (warm, soulful)
- C minor, G minor (standard but less characteristic)

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Minor 7th (m7) | Foundation, groove pads | Fm7, Ebm7, Cm7 |
| Major 7th (Maj7) | Resolution, brightness | AbMaj7, DbMaj7 |
| Dominant 9th (9) | Jazz tension, funk inflection | Bb9, Eb9 |
| Minor 9th (m9) | Extended warmth, lush color | Fm9, Cm9 |
| Minor 11th (m11) | Open, modern voicing | Am11, Dm11 |
| Add9 | Bright, open sound | Fmadd9, Cadd9 |
| Diminished 7th (dim7) | Passing chord, brief tension | Bdim7 |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
i - VI - III - VII        (Fm - Db - Ab - Eb — classic deep house cycle)
i - iv - VII - III        (Fm - Bbm - Eb - Ab — soulful movement)
ii - V - I                (jazz turnaround — Gm7 - C9 - FMaj7)
i - VII - VI - VII        (descending warmth — Am - G - F - G)
I - vi - ii - V           (major key, jazzy — Eb - Cm - Fm - Bb)
i - iv - i - V            (minor groove — Dm - Gm - Dm - A)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Chords change every 2–4 bars (slow harmonic rhythm)
- Stabs are rhythmic — the TIMING of the chord matters as much as the notes
- Space between chord hits is essential (don't fill every beat)
- Bass provides root movement, chords add color above
- Harmonic repetition: same 2–4 chord loop throughout (hypnotic)

### Melodic Characteristics
- Vocal melodies: pentatonic shapes, soulful intervals (minor 3rd, perfect 4th)
- Synth hooks: short motifs (2–4 notes) repeated with variation
- Bass: chromatic approach notes, passing tones between roots
- Call-and-response between vocal and instrumental phrases
- Intervals: major 2nds, minor 3rds, perfect 4ths, major 6ths

## Tech House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Natural minor (Aeolian), Minor pentatonic
- **Secondary**: Dorian (for grooviet moments), Phrygian (darker tech house)
- **Occasional**: Chromatic riffs (not scale-based)
- **Character**: Minimal harmonic content — rhythm dominates over pitch

### Common Keys
- A minor, D minor, G minor (standard DAW-friendly keys)
- E minor, B minor (darker)
- Key matters less than in deep house — many tracks have minimal melodic content

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Minor (m) | Simple stabs | Am, Dm, Em |
| Power chord (5th) | Percussive stab | A5, D5, E5 |
| Minor 7th (m7) | Occasional pad | Am7, Dm7 |
| Single notes | Most common "harmony" | Bass notes only |
| Suspended (sus2/sus4) | Tension without resolution | Asus4, Dsus2 |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
i only (single chord/bass note)    (most DJ tools — no progression)
i - iv                             (two-chord oscillation)
i - VII - VI - VII                 (driving, dark)
i - v - iv - i                     (circular, minimal)
(none — rhythm only)               (many tracks have zero harmonic content)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Often NO chord changes — single bass note/chord throughout
- When changes happen: every 8–16 bars (very slow)
- Vocal chops provide the only pitch content in many tracks
- Bass = harmony (single notes define the key)
- "Less is more" — harmonic complexity is avoided

### Melodic Characteristics
- Vocal chops: fragmented words as rhythmic texture
- Bass: single-note repetitive patterns (root + octave at most)
- Synth lines: rare, short, functional
- Melodic hooks: 1–3 notes maximum, repeated
- Rhythm IS the melody in tech house

## Progressive House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Natural minor (Aeolian), Dorian, Mixolydian
- **Secondary**: Lydian (dreamy, ethereal quality), Melodic minor (ascending)
- **Occasional**: Whole tone (for tension/builds), Harmonic minor (emotional peaks)
- **Character**: Rich, evolving harmony — more complex than standard house

### Common Keys
- A minor, C minor, F minor (emotive minor keys)
- D minor, G minor (standard)
- Major keys for uplifting progressive: C major, G major, D major

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Minor 7th (m7) | Pad foundations | Am7, Cm7, Fm7 |
| Major 7th (Maj7) | Resolution, brightness | CMaj7, FMaj7 |
| Add9 | Open, modern | Amadd9, Cadd9 |
| Suspended 4th (sus4) | Tension, no resolution | Asus4, Dsus4 |
| Minor 9th (m9) | Lush, extended | Am9, Dm9 |
| Slash chords | Smooth bass movement | Am/G, F/E |
| Diminished | Passing tension | Bdim, F#dim |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
i - VII - VI - VII        (classic progressive — Am - G - F - G)
i - III - VII - IV        (emotional arc — Am - C - G - D)
vi - IV - I - V           (anthem progression — relative key)
i - iv - VII - III        (building — Am - Dm - G - C)
I - V - vi - IV           (uplifting major progressive)
i - VII - iv - i          (circular, hypnotic loop)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Chords change every 4–8 bars in builds (slower than deep house)
- Breakdowns may hold one chord for 16+ bars
- Bass notes move more frequently than chords (creates movement)
- Slow harmonic evolution over entire track arc
- Key modulation possible in peak sections (up a semitone or whole tone)

### Melodic Characteristics
- Arpeggiated synth lines over sustained chords
- Melodies evolve gradually (new notes added over repetitions)
- Wide melodic intervals (4ths, 5ths, octaves) for epic quality
- Counter-melodies appear in later sections
- Delay-processed melodies create self-harmonization

## Funky House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Mixolydian (funky, major-but-bluesy), Dorian (minor funk)
- **Secondary**: Major pentatonic, Blues scale
- **Occasional**: Natural minor for darker funky tracks
- **Character**: Funk/disco-rooted — blues notes, chromatic bass movement

### Common Keys
- C major, F major, Bb major (funky keys)
- G minor, D minor, A minor (minor funk)
- Flat keys: Eb, Ab (comfortable for horn/disco samples)

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Dominant 7th (7) | Classic funk chord | C7, F7, G7 |
| Dominant 9th (9) | Extended funk | E9, A9, D9 |
| Minor 7th (m7) | Groove foundation | Gm7, Dm7 |
| Major 9th (Maj9) | Lush, warm | CMaj9, FMaj9 |
| Diminished 7th (dim7) | Passing chord | F#dim7 |
| 6/9 chords | Jazz/funk color | C6/9, F6/9 |
| Altered dominants | Tension (b9, #9) | G7#9 (Hendrix chord) |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
I - IV - I - V            (basic funk vamp)
i - IV (major IV) - i     (minor with borrowed chord — funky!)
I - vi - ii - V           (diatonic cycle)
I7 - IV7                  (two-chord funk vamp — dominant 7ths)
i - iv - VII - III        (minor funk cycle)
ii - V - I                (jazz turnaround — repeated)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Chords change every 1–2 bars (faster than deep house — funky movement)
- Bass provides chromatic approach notes between chord roots
- Stabs hit on syncopated rhythmic positions
- Guitar/keys comp rhythmically (scratchy, percussive)
- Harmonic repetition: 4-bar loop is standard

### Melodic Characteristics
- Funky bass riffs: octave jumps, chromatic approaches, syncopation
- Vocal hooks: pentatonic, catchy, repetitive
- Guitar licks: blues-influenced, call-and-response with vocal
- Horn stabs: short, punchy, rhythmic
- Intervals: octaves, minor 3rds, chromatic half-steps

## Afro House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Natural minor (Aeolian), Dorian, Minor pentatonic
- **Secondary**: Major pentatonic (for brighter, celebratory moments)
- **Occasional**: Phrygian dominant (Middle Eastern/North African inflection)
- **Character**: Organic, spiritual, pentatonic-heavy — reflects African melodic traditions

### Common Keys
- A minor, D minor, E minor (natural keys)
- G minor, C minor (deeper)
- Key often determined by kalimba/marimba tuning or vocal range

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Minor (m) | Foundation | Am, Dm, Em |
| Minor 7th (m7) | Warm depth | Am7, Dm7 |
| Suspended (sus2/sus4) | Open, ambiguous | Asus2, Dsus4 |
| Power chord (5th) | Percussive stab | A5, D5 |
| Major (relative) | Brightness, resolution | C, F, G |
| Single notes | Bass/kalimba — melody implied | — |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
i - VII - VI - VII        (circular minor — Am - G - F - G)
i - iv - III - VII        (spiritual build — Am - Dm - C - G)
i - v - iv - i            (dark, repetitive)
i only (drone/bass note)  (many percussion-focused tracks)
I - IV - V - IV           (celebratory, major key)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Slow — chords change every 4–8 bars (percussion is the focus)
- Many tracks: single chord/key throughout
- Kalimba/marimba provides melodic content without "chord changes"
- Vocal hooks define tonal center more than chords
- Bass notes + percussion = most of the harmonic information

### Melodic Characteristics
- Pentatonic melodies (major and minor pentatonic)
- Kalimba/marimba patterns: repetitive, interlocking
- Vocal melodies: call-and-response, narrow range
- Arpeggiated patterns: 3–5 notes, looped
- Organic instruments carry melody (not synths primarily)

## Jackin House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Natural minor, Dorian, Blues scale
- **Secondary**: Minor pentatonic, Mixolydian
- **Occasional**: Chromatic (sample-based, pitch may be incidental)
- **Character**: Harmony is secondary to groove — samples determine pitch content

### Common Keys
- A minor, D minor, G minor (whatever the sample is in)
- Key is often determined by the source material being sampled
- Basslines may be chromatic/non-diatonic

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Sampled chords | From disco/funk/soul source material | Variable |
| Minor 7th (m7) | When original chords are played | Gm7, Dm7 |
| Dominant 7th (7) | Funk influence | C7, G7 |
| Single bass notes | Most common original content | Root notes |
| Stab chords | Short, percussive | Am, Dm (dry, short) |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
(sample-determined)       (progression comes from the flipped sample)
i - iv                    (simple two-chord groove)
i - VII - iv - VII        (minor groove cycle)
I7 - IV7                  (funk vamp when not sample-based)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Determined by sampled loop (often 2–4 bar cycles)
- Bass provides root movement underneath sample
- Minimal original harmonic content
- Groove and rhythm > harmony
- Repetition: same harmonic loop throughout

### Melodic Characteristics
- Sampled melodic content (hooks lifted from records)
- Bass: simple root movement, syncopated
- Vocal hooks from samples (not original vocals usually)
- Minimal original melody composition
- "Found" melodies from sample sources

## Soulful House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Dorian (warm minor), Natural minor, Major (gospel influence)
- **Secondary**: Mixolydian (soulful major), Blues scale
- **Occasional**: Lydian (uplifting moments), Melodic minor
- **Character**: Rich, emotional, gospel/R&B-influenced harmony

### Common Keys
- Eb major, Ab major, Db major (gospel/R&B territory)
- F minor, Bb minor, Eb minor (soulful minor)
- C major, G major (standard but less characteristic)

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Minor 7th (m7) | Verse foundation | Fm7, Cm7, Bbm7 |
| Major 7th (Maj7) | Chorus resolution | EbMaj7, AbMaj7 |
| Dominant 9th (9) | Tension, funk color | Bb9, Eb9, F9 |
| Minor 9th (m9) | Lush verse pad | Fm9, Cm9 |
| Diminished 7th (dim7) | Chromatic passing | Edim7, Bdim7 |
| 6/9 chords | Bright, warm | Eb6/9, Ab6/9 |
| Augmented (aug) | Gospel tension | Cbaug, Fbaug |
| 11th / 13th | Extended richness | Cm11, Bb13 |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
I - vi - ii - V           (classic soul progression — EbMaj7 - Cm7 - Fm7 - Bb9)
i - iv - VII - III        (soulful minor — Fm7 - Bbm7 - Eb9 - AbMaj7)
ii - V - I - vi           (jazz/gospel cycle)
I - III - IV - iv         (major to minor IV — emotional)
vi - IV - I - V           (pop/soul anthem)
I - V/VII - vi - V        (dramatic descending bass)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Chords change every 1–2 bars (more movement than deep house)
- Gospel-influenced: passing chords on beats 3–4 leading to next bar
- Vocal drives harmonic rhythm (chords follow phrasing)
- Bass walks between chord roots (chromatic movement)
- Bridge sections may introduce new chords/keys

### Melodic Characteristics
- Vocal melodies: wide range, gospel-influenced runs and melisma
- Piano fills between vocal phrases (call-and-response)
- Bass: melodic, walking lines with chromatic passing tones
- String counter-melodies in chorus sections
- Rich intervallic variety: 3rds, 4ths, 6ths, 7ths

## UK Garage

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Natural minor (Aeolian), Minor pentatonic
- **Secondary**: Dorian (for warmer moments), Harmonic minor (for drama)
- **Occasional**: Major key for uplifting tracks
- **Character**: Emotional, R&B-influenced melodies over dark basslines

### Common Keys
- A minor, D minor, E minor (standard)
- G minor, C minor (deeper)
- F# minor, B minor (darker 2-step)

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Minor 7th (m7) | Foundation | Am7, Dm7, Em7 |
| Major 7th (Maj7) | Resolution, bright pads | CMaj7, FMaj7 |
| Diminished 7th (dim7) | Tension, passing | G#dim7, Bdim7 |
| Minor 9th (m9) | Lush R&B color | Am9, Dm9 |
| Suspended (sus2/sus4) | Open pads | Asus4, Gsus2 |
| Add9 | Bright, modern | Amadd9, Fadd9 |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
i - VI - III - VII        (standard UKG — Am - F - C - G)
i - iv - VII - III        (emotional UKG — Am - Dm - G - C)
i - VII - VI - V          (descending, dramatic)
vi - IV - I - V           (uplifting garage)
i - iv - i - V            (dark 2-step)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Chords change every 2–4 bars
- Pads sustain over syncopated rhythm (contrast with skippy drums)
- Bass note movement independent of chord rhythm
- Vocal determines phrasing length
- Breakdowns: sustained chord (one chord, 8+ bars)

### Melodic Characteristics
- Chopped vocal melodies (Todd Edwards technique)
- R&B-influenced vocal hooks
- Super Saw pad arpeggios
- Piano runs (ascending/descending, ornamental)
- Bass melody: sub notes with pitch bends
- Wide intervals in vocal melodies (4ths, 5ths, octaves)

## Electro House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Natural minor (Aeolian), Phrygian (dark, aggressive)
- **Secondary**: Harmonic minor (dramatic, Middle Eastern flavor)
- **Occasional**: Chromatic (non-diatonic riffs), Whole tone (builds)
- **Character**: Dark, aggressive, big — not subtle or jazzy

### Common Keys
- F minor, A minor, D minor (powerful minor keys)
- E minor, G minor (standard)
- Bb minor, Eb minor (darker, heavier)

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Minor (m) | Main stabs, drops | Am, Fm, Dm |
| Power chord (5th) | Aggressive stabs | F5, A5, D5 |
| Diminished (dim) | Dark tension | Bdim, Fdim |
| Minor added 9th | Modern sound | Amadd9, Fmadd9 |
| Suspended | Build tension | Asus4, Dsus4 |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
i - VI - III - VII        (anthem progression in minor)
i - VII - VI - V          (descending drama)
i - iv - i - V            (dark dance)
i only                    (single chord on drop — common)
i - III - VII - iv        (emotional build)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Builds: sustained chords building tension (one chord per 4–8 bars)
- Drops: short stabs (chord on beat 1 of every bar or every 2 bars)
- Breakdowns: arpeggiated or sustained pads, slow changes
- Chord rhythm aligned with sidechain pumping
- Contrast between sections is the point

### Melodic Characteristics
- Big lead melodies (supersaw, wide intervals)
- Anthemic, singable hooks
- Bass drops: pitch bends, slides, glides
- Arpeggiated builds leading to drops
- Dramatic — not subtle. Wide leaps, octave hooks

## Bass House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Natural minor (Aeolian), Phrygian (dark aggression)
- **Secondary**: Minor pentatonic, Blues scale
- **Occasional**: Chromatic (bass riffs are not always scale-based)
- **Character**: Dark, heavy, bass-driven — melody serves the bass

### Common Keys
- F minor, G minor, A minor (powerful sub frequencies)
- D minor, E minor (standard)
- Bb minor (darker, heavier)

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Minor (m) | Sparse stabs | Am, Fm, Gm |
| Power chord (5th) | Percussive, aggressive | F5, G5, A5 |
| Single notes | Bass = harmony | F, G, A (bass riffs) |
| Minor 7th (m7) | Occasional pad | Am7, Fm7 |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
i only (bass riff defines key)    (most common)
i - VII                           (two-chord if any)
i - iv - i - VII                  (dark groove loop)
(bass melody IS the progression)  (bass movement = harmonic content)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Often NO chord changes — bass riff IS the harmony
- Bass melody provides all pitch movement
- Pads (when present) hold single chord
- Harmonic content minimal in drops
- Breakdowns may introduce simple chord progression

### Melodic Characteristics
- Bass IS the melody (wobble patterns form melodic contour)
- Vocal chops: short, pitched, rhythmic
- Minimal melodic content outside of bass
- Bass riffs: chromatic movement, slides, pitch bends
- Intervals in bass: minor 2nds (chromatic), minor 3rds, 5ths

## Chicago House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Natural minor, Dorian, Major (simple, direct)
- **Secondary**: Blues scale, Minor pentatonic
- **Occasional**: Mixolydian (for gospel-influenced tracks)
- **Character**: Simple, raw — harmony serves the groove, never complex

### Common Keys
- C minor, A minor, F minor (simple, keyboard-friendly)
- G minor, D minor (standard)
- Eb major, Bb major (organ-friendly gospel keys)

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Minor (m) | Simple stabs | Am, Cm, Fm |
| Major (M) | Organ stabs, gospel | C, F, Eb |
| Minor 7th (m7) | Occasional warmth | Am7, Cm7 |
| Dominant 7th (7) | Funk/gospel | C7, G7, F7 |
| Single notes | Bass synth | Root notes only |
| Triads | Organ chords | Simple 3-note voicings |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
i only (bass note + groove)       (most raw jack-tracks)
i - iv                            (simple two-chord)
I - IV - V - IV                   (gospel influence)
i - VII - VI - VII                (cycling minor)
i - iv - VII - III                (when more harmonic content exists)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Extremely slow or non-existent (single chord tracks are standard)
- When chords change: every 4–8 bars
- Organ stabs provide harmony without "progression"
- Groove IS the content — harmony is decoration
- Vocal chants may imply pitch but aren't harmonically complex

### Melodic Characteristics
- Vocal chants: 1–3 notes, repeated (monotone is valid)
- Bass: single root note, repetitive
- Organ fills: simple ornamental riffs
- Piano: basic riff (Marshall Jefferson "Move Your Body" style)
- Minimalist — complexity is NOT the goal

## Ghetto House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Minor pentatonic, Natural minor
- **Secondary**: Chromatic (pitch isn't always intentional)
- **Occasional**: Blues scale
- **Character**: MINIMAL harmonic content — rhythm and vocals dominate entirely

### Common Keys
- A minor, E minor, D minor (808 bass tuning)
- Key is often incidental (determined by 808 tuning)
- Vocal pitch may not match any key (raw, untuned)

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Single bass note | 808 hit on root | A, D, E |
| (none) | Most tracks have zero chords | — |
| Power chord (5th) | Rare stab | A5, D5 |
| Synth stab | Occasional accent | Single note or octave |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
(none — no harmonic progression)  (MOST COMMON)
Single bass note throughout       (808 root)
i only                            (if any tonal center exists)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- NO harmonic rhythm in most tracks
- 808 bass provides the only pitched element
- Vocals are rhythmic, not melodic (chanting, not singing)
- Any pitch content is incidental, not compositional
- Genre explicitly rejects harmonic complexity

### Melodic Characteristics
- Vocal chants: monotone, rhythmic, explicit
- 808 bass: single pitch, repeated
- No melody in the traditional sense
- Pitch bends on 808 (only "melodic" variation)
- Rhythm replaces melody entirely

## UK Funky

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Natural minor, Minor pentatonic
- **Secondary**: Dorian (warmer moments), Mixolydian (soca/carnival influence)
- **Occasional**: Major pentatonic (brighter tracks)
- **Character**: Percussion-driven — harmony exists but isn't the focus

### Common Keys
- G minor, D minor, A minor (standard)
- C minor, F minor (darker)
- Bb major, Eb major (soca/carnival-influenced tracks)

### Chord Vocabulary

|-----------|-------|---------|

| Minor 7th (m7) | Pad, depth | Gm7, Dm7 |
| Major (M) | Bright stabs | Bb, Eb, F |
| Dominant 7th (7) | Soca/funk color | G7, C7 |
| Single notes | Bass hits | Root + 5th |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
i - VII - VI - VII        (cycling minor)
i - iv - VII - III        (UK funky standard)
i - v - iv - i            (dark groove)
I - IV - V - IV           (carnival-influenced, bright)

### Harmonic Rhythm

- Many tracks: no chords, only bass + percussion
- Synth stabs rhythmic (syncopated placement > harmony)
- Bass entry creates harmonic "event" mid-track
- Vocal chops provide pitch texture

### Melodic Characteristics
- Hooks are RHYTHMIC, not melodic (syncopated percussion patterns)
- R&B vocal chops: pitched, short, rhythmic
- Bass: syncopated root patterns with octave jumps
- Synth hooks: 2–3 note riffs, heavily syncopated
- Soca/Afrobeats melodic influence (major pentatonic phrases)

## Gqom

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: None (genre explicitly rejects melody)
- **Secondary**: Chromatic/atonal (when pitched elements exist)
- **Occasional**: Minor (implied by bass drone frequency)
- **Character**: NO MELODIC HOOKS BY DESIGN — percussion and atmosphere only

### Common Keys
- No key (most tracks are atonal/unpitched)
- Bass drone may sit on A, D, E (low fundamental frequency)
- "Key" is incidental, not compositional

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| (none) | Genre has no chords | — |
| Bass drone | Single sustained frequency | 40–60 Hz fundamental |
| Unpitched percussion | Main content | Not key-based |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
(none — genre explicitly rejects harmonic progression)
Single bass drone frequency sustained throughout
No resolution, no tension-release in harmonic terms
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- NO harmonic rhythm
- Bass drone: static, unchanging
- Energy comes from percussion, not harmony
- Any pitch change is rare and dramatic
- Tonal ambiguity is intentional

### Melodic Characteristics
- NONE BY DESIGN
- Bass drone provides only pitched content
- No vocal melodies, no synth hooks, no arpeggios
- Percussion patterns replace melodic interest
- The absence of melody IS the aesthetic choice

## French Touch

- **Secondary**: Natural minor, Major pentatonic
- **Occasional**: Blues scale (disco-funk DNA)
- **Character**: Disco-rooted harmony — funky, warm, sample-determined

### Common Keys
- C minor, G minor, F minor (disco sample-friendly)
- Eb major, Ab major, Bb major (disco keys)
- Key determined by source disco sample

### Chord Vocabulary

```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Determined by sampled disco loop (usually 2–4 bar cycles)
- Chord movement: every 1–2 bars (disco pace)
- Filter position obscures/reveals harmony
- When filter is closed: harmony is implied/hidden
- When filter opens: full harmonic content revealed

### Melodic Characteristics
- Sampled disco melodies (strings, horns, vocals from records)
- Bass: funky, chromatic, disco-style movement
- Vocal hooks: often from sample source
- Filter sweep reveals melodic content (structural device)
- Disco-influenced: wide intervals, string counter-melodies

## Lo-Fi House / Outsider House

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Natural minor, Dorian, Minor pentatonic
- **Secondary**: Major (warm, nostalgic), Mixolydian
- **Occasional**: Chromatic (sample-derived, may not follow strict mode)
- **Character**: Warm, nostalgic — classic house harmony through degraded processing

### Common Keys
- A minor, D minor, G minor (sample-derived)
- C major, F major (warm, nostalgic)
- Key often determined by vinyl sample source

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Minor 7th (m7) | Sampled pads, warm | Am7, Dm7, Gm7 |
| Major 7th (Maj7) | Bright, nostalgic | CMaj7, FMaj7 |
| Minor 9th (m9) | Lush, filtered | Am9, Dm9 |
| Dominant 7th (7) | Warm funk influence | G7, C7 |
| Add9 | Open, dreamy | Amadd9, Cadd9 |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
i - VI - III - VII        (classic house cycle, filtered/degraded)
i - iv - VII - III        (soulful, warm)
I - vi - IV - V           (nostalgic, major key)
i - VII - VI - V          (emotional descent)
ii - V - I                (jazz, sampled from records)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Same as classic deep house (chords every 2–4 bars)
- Filtered/degraded processing obscures exact voicings
- Simplicity: 2–4 chord loops repeated throughout
- Tape warble creates micro-pitch variation (unintentional harmony)
- Warm pad sustains → harmonic rhythm feels slower

### Melodic Characteristics
- Sampled melodies (from vinyl, processed/filtered)
- Simple synth hooks (2–4 notes, warm, fuzzy)
- Vocal samples (distant, lo-fi, fragmented)
- Bass: deep, simple, root movement
- Everything filtered/degraded — melody is "felt" not "clear"

## Future Garage

### Scales & Modes
- **Primary**: Natural minor (Aeolian), Dorian
- **Secondary**: Harmonic minor (melancholic, emotional tension)
- **Occasional**: Phrygian (dark, tense), Lydian (dreamy, floating)
- **Character**: Melancholic, emotional, atmospheric — harmony creates mood over groove

### Common Keys
- D minor, A minor, E minor (melancholic, dark)
- F minor, C minor (deeper emotion)
- B minor, F# minor (darker, more tense)

### Chord Vocabulary
| Chord Type | Usage | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Minor 7th (m7) | Foundation pads | Dm7, Am7, Em7 |
| Minor 9th (m9) | Lush, sad | Dm9, Am9, Fm9 |
| Major 7th (Maj7) | Resolution, bittersweet | FMaj7, CMaj7 |
| Suspended (sus2/sus4) | Ambiguity, tension | Dsus2, Asus4 |
| Minor 11th (m11) | Open, spacious | Am11, Dm11 |
| Add9 | Gentle brightness | Fmadd9, Amadd9 |
| Slash chords | Smooth bass movement | Am/G, Dm/C |

### Common Chord Progressions
```
i - VII - VI - V          (melancholic descent — Dm - C - Bb - A)
i - III - VII - iv        (emotional arc)
i - iv - VII - III        (bittersweet cycle)
i - VI - III - VII        (spacious, gentle movement)
vi - IV - I - V           (emotional, from garage ancestry)
i - VII - iv - VI         (dark, atmospheric)
```

### Harmonic Rhythm
- Slow — chords change every 4–8 bars (atmospheric, spacious)
- Pads sustain with long reverb tails (blurs chord boundaries)
- Vocal chops may imply additional harmony
- Bass moves independently from chords (counterpoint)
- Harmonic ambiguity: chords blend into each other via reverb

### Melodic Characteristics
- Pitched vocal chops: emotional, fragmented melodies
- Pad melodies: slow, wide intervals (4ths, 5ths, 7ths)
- Sub bass: melody through pitch bends and slow movement
- Counter-melodies from reverb/delay artifacts
- Space between notes is expressive (silence as melody)
- Intervals: perfect 4ths, minor 7ths, minor 2nds (tension)
