# Mixing & Mastering Benchmarks

## Uplifting Trance

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–60 Hz | Kick sub, bass fundamental | Present, tight |
| 60–150 Hz | Kick body, bass body | Controlled, punchy |
| 150–500 Hz | Bass upper harmonics, pad low-mids | Moderate — watch for mud |
| 500 Hz–2 kHz | Supersaw body, piano, vocal | DOMINANT in drops |
| 2–6 kHz | Supersaw brightness, vocal presence, hi-hat | Bright, energetic |
| 6–12 kHz | Ride cymbal, crash, hi-hat air | Present, sparkling |
| 12–20 kHz | Air, cymbal shimmer | Subtle presence |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: Kick, bass, lead melody (core energy)
- **Narrow stereo**: Main supersaw body, snare/clap
- **Wide stereo**: Supersaw width layer, pads, reverb returns, crash cymbals
- **Overall width**: Wide in drops, narrower in intros/outros for DJ compatibility
- **Mono compatibility**: Important — check supersaws collapse well in mono

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS integrated
- **Target LUFS (club/DJ)**: -8 to -10 LUFS
- **Dynamic range**: 8–12 dB (breakdown to climax contrast is essential)
- **Bus compression**: Light-medium (2–4 dB GR), glue character
- **Limiter**: Moderate — preserve the dynamic arc (breakdown quiet → drop loud)
- **Philosophy**: The CONTRAST between breakdown and drop IS the genre — don't squash it

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- Ferry Corsten — "Out of the Blue" (classic supersaw balance, clarity)
- Aly & Fila — "We Control the Sunlight" (modern uplifting mix, wide stereo)
- Gouryella — "Anahera" (supersaw layering, breakdown/drop contrast)

### Common Mix Mistakes
- Supersaws too wide (mono collapse, phase issues)
- Breakdown too quiet relative to drops (streaming normalization kills the build)
- Bass and kick fighting (need tight sidechain separation)
- Too much reverb on everything (loses punch and clarity)
- Hi-hats too harsh/bright (fatiguing over 7+ minutes)
- Breakdown piano too loud (should be intimate, not dominant)

### EQ Signature Moves
- High-pass bass at 30 Hz (let kick own the sub)
- Cut supersaw mud at 200–400 Hz (give bass space)
- Presence boost on lead melody at 2–4 kHz (cut-through in drops)
- De-ess crash cymbals above 10 kHz (prevent harshness)
- Low-cut pads at 150–200 Hz (prevent low-mid buildup)
- Gentle high shelf on mix bus at 8 kHz (+1–2 dB for air)

## Psytrance

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–60 Hz | Kick sub | Tight, precise |
| 60–200 Hz | Bass fundamental, kick body | DOMINANT — bass drives everything |
| 200–500 Hz | Bass upper harmonics, texture low-mids | Present |
| 500 Hz–2 kHz | FX textures, bass resonance peaks | Moderate density |
| 2–6 kHz | Hi-hat body, FX brightness, kick click | Clear |
| 6–12 kHz | Hi-hat air, texture brightness | Present but controlled |
| 12–20 kHz | Air, subtle texture | Less emphasis than uplifting |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: Kick, bass (ALWAYS mono below 200 Hz), core FX
- **Narrow stereo**: Hi-hats, main textures
- **Wide stereo**: Psychedelic FX, atmospheric layers, reverb/delay returns
- **Overall width**: Less wide than uplifting — more front-to-back depth
- **Mono compatibility**: CRITICAL — outdoor festivals, big systems

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (DJ tool)**: -7 to -9 LUFS integrated
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS
- **Dynamic range**: 5–8 dB (tighter than uplifting — sustained energy)
- **Bus compression**: Medium-heavy (3–5 dB GR), parallel compression common
- **Limiter**: More aggressive than uplifting (sustained loudness needed)
- **Philosophy**: Relentless energy — the mix should feel "pressurized"

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- Astrix — "He.Art" (bass clarity, kick-bass separation, texture layering)
- Vini Vici — "Great Spirit" (commercial psytrance mix, loud and clear)
- Ace Ventura — tracks with excellent bass-to-texture balance

### Common Mix Mistakes
- Kick and bass not separated (muddy low end — need surgical sidechain)
- Too many mid-frequency textures masking each other
- Psychedelic FX too loud (should be atmospheric, not dominant)
- Bass too distorted (loses sub-frequency content)
- Mix too dense — no space for the rolling bass to breathe
- Triplet hi-hats too loud (fatiguing at high tempo)

### EQ Signature Moves
- Surgical cut at kick frequency on bass (sidechain + static EQ)
- Bass: boost fundamental 60–100 Hz, cut 200–400 Hz mud
- Hi-hats: high-pass at 6 kHz minimum (keep out of bass territory)
- FX textures: band-pass 1–5 kHz (keep them in their lane)
- Kick: boost click at 3–5 kHz for cut-through, tight HPF at 30 Hz
- Overall: slightly darker balance than uplifting

## Goa Trance

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–60 Hz | Kick sub | Present but less dominant than modern psy |
| 60–200 Hz | 303 bass body, kick body | Warm, present |
| 200–800 Hz | 303 resonance, pad body, melody low-mids | RICH — dense midrange |
| 800 Hz–3 kHz | Melody body, 303 resonance peaks, vocal/lead | Active, layered |
| 3–8 kHz | Melody brightness, percussion attack | Present |
| 8–15 kHz | Hi-hat, shaker air | Moderate — warmer than modern psy |
| 15–20 kHz | Air (less emphasis — analog warmth) | Subtle |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: Kick, 303 bass, primary melody
- **Narrow stereo**: Secondary melodies, analog pads
- **Wide stereo**: Chorus-effected pads, reverb returns, nature sounds
- **Overall width**: Moderate — warm center, wide atmosphere
- **Mono compatibility**: Important for outdoor sound systems

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (modern master)**: -9 to -11 LUFS
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS
- **Dynamic range**: 8–12 dB (more dynamic than psytrance — analog era aesthetic)
- **Bus compression**: Light (2–3 dB GR) — preserve analog dynamics
- **Limiter**: Gentle — analog character lost with aggressive limiting
- **Philosophy**: Warmth and dynamics over loudness — honor the analog origins

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- Hallucinogen — "LSD" (acid bass balance, multiple melodic layers)
- Astral Projection — "Dancing Galaxy" (melodic layering, warmth)
- Filteria — neo-Goa (modern production of classic sound — good reference for clarity)

### Common Mix Mistakes
- 303 resonance peaks too harsh (painful at high volumes)
- Too many melodic layers fighting for the same frequency space
- Losing the warmth by over-processing (Goa should sound analog)
- Kick too aggressive/modern (should be rounder, warmer)
- Nature sounds too loud in intros (should be subtle, atmospheric)
- Over-compression removing the analog dynamic feel

### EQ Signature Moves
- Tame 303 resonance with dynamic EQ at 800 Hz – 2 kHz
- Roll off harsh highs above 12 kHz (warmer top end than modern)
- Each melody layer gets its own frequency "slot" (careful EQ carving)
- Boost warmth at 200–400 Hz on pads (analog character)
- High-pass nature sounds at 100–200 Hz (keep them airy)
- Gentle tape-style saturation on mix bus for cohesion

## Progressive Trance

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–60 Hz | Kick sub, bass sub | Deep, warm |
| 60–150 Hz | Kick body, bass body | Balanced, groove-focused |
| 150–500 Hz | Bass upper harmonics, groove plucks | Warm, present |
| 500 Hz–2 kHz | Plucks, melodic elements, pad body | Clean, moderate |
| 2–6 kHz | Percussion transients, melody brightness | Present but not aggressive |
| 6–12 kHz | Hi-hat air, reverb sparkle | Subtle, open |
| 12–20 kHz | Air, atmosphere | Gentle presence |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: Kick, bass, main groove pluck
- **Narrow stereo**: Percussion, secondary plucks
- **Wide stereo**: Pads, reverb/delay returns, atmospheric layers
- **Overall width**: Wide and spacious — room to breathe
- **Mono compatibility**: Important for DJ mixing (long blends)

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (DJ tool)**: -9 to -11 LUFS
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS
- **Dynamic range**: 8–12 dB (preserve the groove dynamics)
- **Bus compression**: Light (2–3 dB GR) — groove should breathe
- **Limiter**: Gentle — avoid flattening the groove character
- **Philosophy**: Space and groove over loudness — the mix should feel open

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- Grum — "Deep State" tracks (spacious, clean, groove-forward)
- Kyau & Albert — "Are You One?" (melodic progressive balance)
- Solarstone — "Seven Cities" (progressive-uplifting bridge, warm mix)

### Common Mix Mistakes
- Mix too dense (progressive needs SPACE — less is more)
- Bass too loud/prominent (should groove, not dominate)
- Delay tails cluttering the mix (need careful feedback management)
- Reverb too long (blurs the groove — keep reverbs shorter than uplifting)
- Not enough dynamic contrast between sections (everything at same level)
- Over-processing — progressive's charm is subtlety

### EQ Signature Moves
- Generous high-pass on atmospheric elements (200+ Hz — keep them out of groove territory)
- Warm low-mid boost on bass (100–200 Hz for depth)
- Subtle dip at 2–3 kHz (prevents fatigue over long progressive sets)
- High shelf on delays/reverbs (keep them airy, not muddy)
- Less aggressive presence boost than uplifting (smoother, less "in your face")
- Cut competing frequencies between groove pluck and melodic elements

## Tech Trance

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–60 Hz | Kick sub, bass sub | Heavy, powerful |
| 60–150 Hz | Kick body, bass body | DOMINANT — physical impact |
| 150–500 Hz | Acid resonance, stab body | Present, aggressive |
| 500 Hz–2 kHz | Stab brightness, acid resonance peaks | Forward |
| 2–6 kHz | Kick click, hi-hat body, transients | BRIGHT, aggressive |
| 6–12 kHz | Hi-hat air, crash | Present, harsh (intentional) |
| 12–20 kHz | Air, noise | Present |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: Kick, bass, stab (all energy focused center)
- **Narrow stereo**: Hi-hats, secondary percussion
- **Wide stereo**: FX, reverb returns (minimal width compared to uplifting)
- **Overall width**: Narrower than uplifting — focused, punchy, forward
- **Mono compatibility**: Very good — most content is center-focused

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (DJ tool)**: -7 to -9 LUFS (LOUD)
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS
- **Dynamic range**: 5–8 dB (tight — sustained high energy)
- **Bus compression**: Heavy (4–6 dB GR), aggressive character
- **Limiter**: Aggressive — loudness is part of the genre's impact
- **Philosophy**: PUNCH and LOUDNESS — the mix should hit hard physically

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- Simon Patterson — "Smack" (kick impact, bass aggression, stab clarity)
- Bryan Kearney — "Adrenaline" (sustained driving energy, loud master)
- Mark Sherry — "Chase" (acid bass clarity, aggressive but controlled)

### Common Mix Mistakes
- Kick lost under bass (needs MORE transient/click than uplifting kick)
- Acid resonance peaks causing harshness (need dynamic EQ taming)
- Mix too thin — tech trance needs physical weight
- Overdo distortion on bass (loses definition, becomes noise)
- Not loud enough for DJ context (tech trance must compete with techno volumes)
- Stabs masking kick transient (need frequency separation)

### EQ Signature Moves
- Boost kick click at 4–6 kHz (needs to punch through aggressive bass)
- Dynamic EQ on acid resonance peaks (tame but don't kill)
- Boost bass fundamental at 60–100 Hz (physical impact)
- Cut 200–400 Hz on stabs (prevent mud with bass)
- Aggressive high-pass on everything except kick/bass (150 Hz+)
- Hard limiter on mix bus (loudness is expected)
- Parallel compression on drum bus (50% wet blend for punch)
- Aggressive kick compression (fast attack, fast release)
- Bass: multi-band compression to control resonance peaks independently
- Sidechain: aggressive and obvious (part of the pumping aesthetic)

## Vocal Trance

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–60 Hz | Kick sub, bass sub | Present, controlled |
| 60–150 Hz | Kick body, bass body | Moderate — room for vocal low-end |
| 150–500 Hz | Bass harmonics, vocal warmth | Careful balance |
| 500 Hz–2 kHz | Vocal body, supersaw body | Vocal DOMINANT in this range |
| 2–6 kHz | Vocal presence, supersaw brightness | Bright — vocal clarity priority |
| 6–12 kHz | Vocal air, hi-hat, ride | Present, open |
| 12–20 kHz | Vocal breathiness, air | Subtle sparkle |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: Kick, bass, lead vocal (ALWAYS center)
- **Narrow stereo**: Vocal doubles, snare/clap
- **Wide stereo**: Supersaws, pads, reverb returns, vocal reverb
- **Overall width**: Wide but vocal anchored firmly in center
- **Mono compatibility**: Critical — vocal must remain clear in mono

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS integrated
- **Target LUFS (club/DJ)**: -8 to -10 LUFS
- **Dynamic range**: 8–12 dB (vocal dynamics preserved in verses)
- **Bus compression**: Light-medium (vocal dynamics need preservation)
- **Limiter**: Moderate — don't squash the vocal's emotional dynamics
- **Philosophy**: Vocal clarity above ALL else — everything serves the voice

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- OceanLab — "Satellite" (vocal clarity, emotional balance)
- Gareth Emery — "Concrete Angel" (modern vocal trance mix, wide)
- Delerium — "Silence" (Tiësto remix) (vocal-synth balance, clarity)

### Common Mix Mistakes
- Vocal buried under supersaws in drops (need sidechain/EQ management)
- Too much vocal reverb (loses intimacy and clarity)
- Synths competing with vocal frequencies (500 Hz – 5 kHz conflict)
- Vocal too compressed (loses emotional dynamics — verse should be intimate)
- Harsh de-essing creating lispy artifacts
- Not enough difference between verse (sparse) and chorus (full) density

### EQ Signature Moves
- Cut supersaws at 1–5 kHz (make room for vocal presence)
- Boost vocal at 3–5 kHz (presence and clarity)
- High-pass vocal at 80–100 Hz (remove room rumble)
- Cut vocal 200–300 Hz (remove boxiness/proximity effect)
- Sidechain supersaws to vocal (subtle 1–3 dB duck for clarity)
- De-ess at 6–8 kHz (control sibilance without killing brightness)
- Vocal chain: HPF 80 Hz → Compression (3–6 dB GR) → EQ (cut mud, boost presence) → De-esser → Reverb send → Delay send
- Vocal reverb: plate or hall, 1.5–2.5s, pre-delay 30–50ms
- Vocal delay: tempo-synced (dotted 8th for movement, quarter for effect)
- Vocal width: doubles panned 30–50% L/R in choruses

## Full-On

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–60 Hz | Kick sub | Tight, short |
| 60–200 Hz | Bass fundamental, kick body | Present, driving |
| 200–500 Hz | Bass harmonics, lead low-mids | Moderate |
| 500 Hz–2 kHz | Lead melody body, arpeggio | DOMINANT — melody focus |
| 2–6 kHz | Lead brightness, kick click | Bright, cutting |
| 6–12 kHz | Hi-hat, arpeggio brightness | Present, energetic |
| 12–20 kHz | Air | Moderate |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: Kick, bass, primary lead melody
- **Narrow stereo**: Secondary lead, arpeggios
- **Wide stereo**: FX, reverb/delay returns
- **Overall width**: Moderate — focused energy with some spatial depth
- **Mono compatibility**: Important for festival systems

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (DJ tool)**: -8 to -10 LUFS
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS
- **Dynamic range**: 6–9 dB (tighter than uplifting — sustained energy)
- **Bus compression**: Medium (3–4 dB GR)
- **Limiter**: Moderate-aggressive (festival loudness needed)
- **Philosophy**: Energetic and bright — the lead must cut through on big systems

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- Astrix — "Sahara" (lead clarity, bass drive, energy balance)
- Vini Vici — "Great Spirit" (commercial full-on, loud and clear)
- Neelix — tracks with excellent lead/bass separation at high tempo

### Common Mix Mistakes
- Lead melody lost under bass (lead MUST be the focus)
- Kick too boomy at 145+ BPM (needs shorter, tighter kick at high tempos)
- Bass and kick bleeding together (fast tempo = less time = tighter separation needed)
- Arpeggios masking the lead melody (keep in different frequency ranges)
- Masters not loud enough for festival context
- Too much reverb (high tempo + reverb = mud)

### EQ Signature Moves
- Boost lead at 2–4 kHz (cut-through on systems)
- Cut bass above 200 Hz (keep it out of melody range)
- Tight kick: HPF at 35 Hz, short body, bright click at 5 kHz
- Arpeggio high-passed at 1 kHz (sits above bass, below lead brightness)
- Shorter reverb times than uplifting (0.5–1.5s max at 145+ BPM)
- Side-chain lead delay return to kick (prevent mud)

## Dark Psy

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–60 Hz | Kick sub, bass sub | HEAVY, physical |
| 60–200 Hz | Distorted bass body, kick body | DOMINANT — aggressive |
| 200–800 Hz | Bass distortion harmonics, noise textures | Dense, aggressive |
| 800 Hz–3 kHz | Noise textures, industrial elements | Filled, abrasive |
| 3–8 kHz | Glitch, metallic percussion, kick click | Present, harsh |
| 8–15 kHz | Noise, industrial air | Filled with texture |
| 15–20 kHz | Noise (genre fills entire spectrum) | Present |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: Kick, bass (heavy mono content)
- **Narrow stereo**: Main noise textures
- **Wide stereo**: Secondary textures, industrial ambience
- **Overall width**: Variable — can be very mono (focused) or wide (immersive chaos)
- **Mono compatibility**: Important for dark outdoor stages

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (DJ tool)**: -6 to -8 LUFS (VERY LOUD)
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS
- **Dynamic range**: 3–6 dB (minimal — sustained assault)
- **Bus compression**: HEAVY (5–8 dB GR), aggressive character
- **Limiter**: Maximum aggression — clipping acceptable and stylistic
- **Philosophy**: Overwhelming sonic assault — loudness IS the aesthetic

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- Kindzadza — tracks (extreme bass, noise layering, aggression)
- Penta — Parvati Records releases (dark psy standard)
- Psykovsky — extreme productions (maximum density reference)

### Common Mix Mistakes
- Bass so distorted it loses all definition (some fundamental must survive)
- Kick completely lost under bass (even dark psy needs SOME kick presence)
- Mix SO dense that nothing is perceivable (some structure within chaos)
- Monitoring too loud during production (misjudging actual aggression levels)
- Not enough low end on final master (dark psy needs physical sub impact)

### EQ Signature Moves
- Boost sub at 40–60 Hz (physical system impact)
- Multi-band distortion: different amounts per frequency band
- Allow midrange harshness (1–4 kHz) — it's stylistic, not a mistake
- Kick needs extreme transient boost (8+ kHz click) to cut through distorted bass
- Accept that traditional EQ "rules" don't apply — the genre intentionally violates them
- Multi-band compression on master to prevent complete frequency chaos

## Forest

### Frequency Spectrum Profile
| Range | Content | Level |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 20–60 Hz | Kick sub, bass sub | Present, warm |
| 60–200 Hz | Bass body, deep drum tones | Warm, organic |
| 200–500 Hz | Percussion body, bass harmonics | Rich, natural |
| 500 Hz–2 kHz | Tribal percussion midrange, organic textures | Active, detailed |
| 2–6 kHz | Percussion transients, nature sounds, wood strikes | Present, natural |
| 6–12 kHz | Shaker air, nature detail (birds, insects) | Open, natural |
| 12–20 kHz | Nature ambience air, atmosphere | Subtle, organic |

### Stereo Field
- **Mono center**: Kick, bass
- **Narrow stereo**: Main tribal percussion (slightly off-center for each element)
- **Wide stereo**: Nature field recordings, atmospheric drones, reverb, secondary percussion
- **Overall width**: WIDE and IMMERSIVE — the listener should feel surrounded by forest
- **Mono compatibility**: Good — core rhythm elements are center-focused

### Dynamics & Loudness
- **Target LUFS (DJ tool)**: -9 to -11 LUFS
- **Target LUFS (streaming)**: -14 LUFS
- **Dynamic range**: 8–12 dB (more dynamic than dark psy — organic breathing)
- **Bus compression**: Light (2–4 dB GR) — preserve organic dynamics
- **Limiter**: Moderate — don't crush the natural dynamic feel
- **Philosophy**: Immersive and organic — the mix should feel like a living forest

### Reference Tracks for Mixing
- Derango — productions (organic texture balance, tribal rhythm clarity)
- Tengri — tracks (deep forest atmosphere, immersive stereo)
- Sangoma Records compilations (genre-standard forest sound)

### Common Mix Mistakes
- Nature sounds too loud (should be subliminal, not distracting)
- Tribal percussion masking each other (need careful panning and EQ separation)
- Bass too aggressive (should be warm, not harsh — this isn't dark psy)
- Too dry — forest needs reverb for spatial immersion
- Losing clarity in dense percussion sections (careful dynamic EQ)
- Mix too bright (forest is warm, earthy — not icy or clinical)

### EQ Signature Moves
- Warm high-shelf (gentle rolloff above 10 kHz for organic character)
- Each percussion element gets its own frequency band (djembe low, bongo mid, shells high)
- Bass: boost warmth at 100–200 Hz, gentle saturation for organic harmonics
- Nature sounds: band-pass filtering to sit in specific frequency pockets
- Kick: warmer character than dark psy (less click, more body)
- Room reverb on percussion bus for cohesive "space" (short, natural room)
- Convolution reverb with forest/outdoor impulse responses for spatial depth
- Pan automation on organic textures (subtle movement for immersion)
- Binaural techniques for headphone listeners (stereo widening with spatial cues)
- Nature sounds placed at different "depths" (close birds L/R, distant water center-back)
