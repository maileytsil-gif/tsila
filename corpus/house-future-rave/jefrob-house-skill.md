# Genre: House

## Purpose
Define structural rules, energy patterns, and arrangement conventions for house music and its subgenres.

## Subgenres Covered

- **Deep House** (115–124 BPM, warm, soulful, groove-focused, jazz-inflected chords)
- **Tech House** (122–128 BPM, driving, percussive, minimal melodic)
- **Progressive House** (124–128 BPM, evolving, layered, longer builds)
- **Funky House** (124–128 BPM, disco-influenced, funky basslines)
- **Afro House** (120–128 BPM, tribal percussion, polyrhythmic)
- **Jackin House** (124–128 BPM, raw, sample-heavy, Chicago influence)
- **Soulful House** (120–126 BPM, vocal-driven, warm production)
- **UK Garage** (130–135 BPM, 2-step rhythm, shuffled, skippy)
- **Electro House** (126–132 BPM, big synths, aggressive drops)
- **Bass House** (124–130 BPM, wobble bass, G-house influence)
- **Chicago House** (120–125 BPM, foundational jack-groove, raw Roland TR-707/808/909, continuous vocal chants)
- **Ghetto House** (125–135 BPM, fast, minimalist, explicit hyper-repetitive vocals, aggressive 808 patterns — precursor to Footwork)
- **UK Funky** (130 BPM, percussive tribal-infused club fusion, broken syncopated hooks, UK bass culture)
- **Gqom** (125–130 BPM, dark, minimalist, hypnotic Durban style, fractured percussion, low-freq drone bass, no melodic hooks)
- **French Touch** (120–126 BPM, phased disco samples, extreme sidechain, dramatic filter sweeps)
- **Lo-Fi House / Outsider House** (118–124 BPM, saturated, distorted production, tape warble, unquantized drums, filtered chords)
- **Future Garage** (130–140 BPM, cinematic, melancholic, unquantized percussion, vinyl crackle, spatial atmospheres)

## Tempo Ranges
- Deep/Soulful: 115–124 BPM
- Chicago House: 120–125 BPM
- Tech/Funky/Jackin/Progressive: 122–130 BPM
- French Touch: 120–126 BPM
- Ghetto House: 125–135 BPM
- Gqom: 125–130 BPM
- Afro: 120–128 BPM
- Electro/Bass House: 126–132 BPM
- UK Garage/UK Funky: 130–135 BPM
- Future Garage: 130–140 BPM
- Lo-Fi House: 118–124 BPM

## Energy Characteristics

### Deep House Energy Curve
- Warm, consistent groove with gentle peaks
- Energy varies within a narrow band (4–7)
- Breakdowns subtle — never fully strip the groove
- Peaks are felt more than heard (layering creates fullness, not loudness)

### Tech House Energy Curve
- Driving and repetitive with functional peaks
- Drops are punchy but not overwhelming
- Energy in a moderate-high band (5–8)
- Brief functional breaks, never emotional

### Bass House Energy Curve
- Closer to EDM structure: clear build → drop pattern
- Drops are heavy and bass-focused
- More dramatic contrast than other house styles
- Energy: 3–4 in breaks, 8–9 in drops

### Energy by Section (Deep House)
| Section | Energy Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 3–4 | Kick + basic groove |
| Groove A | 5–6 | Full rhythm section, bass present |
| Build | 6–7 | Additional elements, slight tension |
| Main | 6–7 | Peak groove, all elements, vocal hook |
| Breakdown | 4–5 | Reduce to pads/vocal, maintain feel |
| Main B | 6–7 | Return with variation |
| Outro | 3–5 | Strip back for mixing |

### Chicago House Energy Curve
- Steady jack-groove energy, minimal contrast
- Vocal chants provide rhythmic energy, not dynamic peaks
- Energy band: 5–7 (narrow, continuous groove)
- No builds, no drops — just groove evolution

### Ghetto House Energy Curve
- High and relentless once established
- Vocal repetition IS the energy (more vocal = more intensity)
- Fast and aggressive throughout
- Energy band: 6–9

### UK Funky Energy Curve
- Syncopated energy driven by percussion complexity
- Bass entry creates a defined energy step-up
- Abrupt section deletions create surprise drops
- Energy band: 5–8

### Gqom Energy Curve
- Dark, minimal, hypnotic — low but constant
- Energy comes from percussion weight, not melodic elements
- No builds, no releases — flat and heavy
- Energy band: 4–6 (narrow, heavy)

### French Touch Energy Curve
- High-contrast sections driven by filter position
- Filter closed = low energy / filter open = high energy
- Dramatic sweeps create the dynamic arc
- Energy band: 4–9 (wide range due to filter contrast)

### Lo-Fi House Energy Curve
- Warm, consistent, groove-locked
- Similar to deep house but more textured/distorted
- Imperfections add character, not drama
- Energy band: 4–7 (narrow, warm, never aggressive)

### Future Garage Energy Curve
- Melancholic, atmospheric, emotionally rich
- Grooves shuffle underneath ambient textures
- Breakdowns are atmospheric, not silent
- Energy band: 3–6 (generally understated, beauty over power)

## Transition Conventions

### Deep / Soulful House
- Element addition/subtraction (one per 4–8 bars)
- Filter opens/closes over 4–8 bars
- Vocal phrase leading into new section
- Drum fill (subtle, tasteful)
- Never harsh — always smooth

### Tech House
- Short filter sweeps (2–4 bars)
- Snare rolls (4–8 bars, moderate intensity)
- FX riser (short, functional)
- Vocal sample chops as transition markers
- Kick removal for 2–4 bars then re-entry

### Bass House
- Aggressive risers and sweeps
- Vocal build ("let me see your hands" style)
- Hard cut to silence before drop (1–2 bars)
- Impact hit on drop entry
- Approaches EDM-style buildups

### Chicago House
- Element layering on 16/32-bar boundaries (grid-locked)
- Roland drum machine fills (707/808/909 patterns)
- Vocal chant entry/exit as section marker
- Subtle filter opens
- Never dramatic — functional and groovy

### Ghetto House
- Hard cuts (genre standard — abrupt and fast)
- Vocal chant acceleration or repetition increase
- 808 roll filling transition
- No smooth builds — immediate section switches
- Vocal sample announcing next section

### UK Funky
- Abrupt section deletions (removing multiple elements simultaneously)
- Syncopated percussion fill
- Bass drop-out then punchy re-entry
- Tribal percussion accent patterns
- Broken swing shifting between sections

### Gqom
- Percussion pattern shift (subtle, no announcement)
- Drone bass frequency shift
- Layer subtraction to near-silence, then rebuild
- Eerie atmospheric FX as section marker
- Never melodic — always textural and percussive

### French Touch
- Filter sweeps (THE signature transition — long, dramatic)
- Extreme sidechain pump appearing/disappearing
- Disco sample phase changes
- Vocal chop stutter leading into open section
- High-contrast: filtered → unfiltered creates major energy shift

### Lo-Fi House
- Filter opens/closes (subtle, warm)
- Tape warble intensity changes
- Drum pattern subtraction → re-entry
- Chord loop filtering (like deep house but grittier)
- Intentionally imperfect — slight timing drift is aesthetic
- Never dramatic — everything is warm and hazy

### Future Garage
- Reverb/delay swells (spacious, cinematic)
- Pitched vocal chops entering/exiting
- Vinyl crackle intensity as section marker
- Drum pattern shuffle shift (2-step → broken → 2-step)
- Field recordings bleeding in/out
- Emotional, ambient transitions — never harsh

## Key Musical Elements

### Deep House
- Warm, round kick (not aggressive)
- Smooth bassline (often synth, sometimes sampled)
- Chord stabs: jazz-influenced, often 7ths and 9ths
- Vocal: soulful, often sampled or processed
- Keys: Rhodes, Wurlitzer, warm pads
- Swing/shuffle on hats (human feel)

### Tech House
- Punchy kick with click
- Driving bassline (often 16th note patterns)
- Percussive loops (congas, bongos, shakers)
- Minimal melodic content
- Vocal chops (short, processed, rhythmic)
- Hi-hats: 16ths, often open hat on off-beats

### Afro House
- Complex polyrhythmic percussion
- Djembe, talking drums, shakers
- Call-and-response vocal patterns
- Deep sub bass
- Organic instrumentation (kalimba, marimba)

### UK Garage
- 2-step kick pattern (not 4-on-the-floor)
- Shuffled hi-hats (skippy, syncopated)
- Chopped vocal samples (pitched up)
- Sub bass (prominent, wobbly)
- Reese bass stabs

### Chicago House
- Drums: Raw Roland TR-707, 808, 909 matrices (foundational, mechanical)
- Bass: Deep, simple, repetitive synth bass
- Vocals: Continuous rhythmic chants, raw, jacking
- Chords: Simple organ/synth stabs
- Structure: Grid-locked to 16/32-bar phrase shifts
- Feel: Raw, hypnotic, the "jack" groove

### Ghetto House
- Drums: Aggressive TR-808 patterns, fast tempo
- Vocals: Explicit, hyper-repetitive, rapid-fire (core element)
- Bass: Heavy 808 sub, short punchy hits
- Syncopation: Driving, pushing forward
- Minimal melodic content — vocals and rhythm only
- Direct precursor to Chicago Footwork

### UK Funky
- Drums: Heavy garage-style swing, broken syncopation
- Percussion: Tribal — congas, shakers, hand drums
- Bass: Prominent UK bass culture low-end
- Hooks: Syncopated rhythmic hooks (percussive, not melodic)
- Vocal: Choppy R&B vocal cuts
- Fusion: UK bass culture meets tribal percussion

### Gqom
- Drums: Heavy, fractured, thumping four-on-the-floor percussion
- Bass: Low-frequency drone bass (sustained, dark)
- Atmosphere: Eerie ambient design, dark pads
- Melodic content: NONE — no melodic hooks by design
- Percussion: Fractured, broken patterns over steady kick
- Feel: Dark, minimalist, deeply hypnotic

### French Touch
- Samples: Phased disco loop samples (main compositional element)
- Compression: Extreme sidechain pumping (signature sound)
- Filters: Dramatic sweeps driving section changes
- Bass: Disco-influenced, funky, prominent
- Production: High-gloss, compressed, saturated
- Structure: Filter position = song structure

### Lo-Fi House
- Drums: Unquantized drum machines, slightly off-grid (intentionally imperfect)
- Chords: Filtered, saturated, warm — often sampled from vinyl
- Bass: Simple, deep, distorted
- Texture: Tape warble, vinyl crackle, hiss, saturation
- Production: Intentionally lo-fi — heavy compression, bit reduction, tape emulation
- Aesthetic: Bedroom warmth, late-night, intimate
- Groove: Classical house grid underneath the distortion

### Future Garage
- Drums: 2-step influenced but unquantized, humanized, organic
- Bass: Warm filtered reese basses, deep and spatial
- Vocals: Pitched vocal chops (signature element), ethereal, fragmented
- Atmosphere: Field recordings, vinyl crackle, ambient textures, reverb
- Pads: Dark, minor-key, suspended chords
- Space: Cavernous reverbs, wide stereo field
- Tempo feel: Implies both half-time glide AND garage shuffle simultaneously

## Common Issues to Detect

- Deep house: energy jump too dramatic (> 2 points) — breaks the vibe
- Tech house: breakdown too long or emotional (> 16 bars)
- Groove not established in first 64 bars
- Missing bass in main sections
- UK Garage: using 4-on-the-floor when 2-step is expected
- Bass house: drop without sufficient bass presence
- Afro house: lacking percussive complexity (fewer than 3 percussion layers)
- Outro too short for DJ mixing (< 16 bars with kick)
- Chicago House: evaluating as if it needs melodic breakdowns (it doesn't — it's pure groove)
- Ghetto House: vocal triggers too infrequent (should be every 2–4 bars)
- Ghetto House: track too "clean" or polished (genre is raw, gritty, aggressive)
- UK Funky: using standard 4/4 without broken syncopation (misses the genre identity)
- Gqom: any melodic hooks or chord progressions present (genre explicitly rejects melody)
- Gqom: energy curve too varied (should be flat, dark, constant)
- French Touch: missing filter sweeps (they define the genre's structural logic)
- French Touch: no sidechain compression (extreme sidechain pump is signature)
- Lo-Fi House: too clean/polished (genre demands tape saturation, distortion, imperfection)
- Lo-Fi House: drums too quantized/perfect (should be slightly off-grid, humanized)
- Future Garage: too aggressive or energetic (genre is melancholic, understated)
- Future Garage: missing atmospheric elements (reverb, crackle, space are essential)
- Future Garage: straight quantized drums (should be unquantized, shuffled, organic)
