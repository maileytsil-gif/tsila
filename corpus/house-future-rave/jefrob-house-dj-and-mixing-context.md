# DJ Compatibility & Mixing Context

## Deep House

### Compatible Genres for Mixing
| Genre | Compatibility | Notes |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| Soulful House | High | Same tempo/warmth, natural companion |
| Lo-Fi House | High | Similar tempo, warm character, groove-focused |
| Afro House | High | Shared groove DNA, slightly different percussion |
| Tech House | Medium-High | Bridge via groovy tech house selections |
| Progressive House | Medium | Shared long-mix approach, slightly faster tempo |
| Amapiano | Medium | Same warmth/tempo range, jazz chord affinity |
| Chicago House | Medium | Ancestor genre, similar tempo |
| Minimal/Microhouse | Medium | Groove-focused, stripped back, similar BPM |
| Funky House | Medium | Same roots, more energetic |
| UK Garage | Low-Medium | Tempo gap (130+ vs 120), needs careful bridging |

### Set Progression Guidance
```
Warm-up → Ambient/Deep Intro (108–112 BPM, atmospheric, spacious)
        → Deep House (115–118 BPM, establishing groove)
        → Vocal Deep House (118–122 BPM, adding energy via vocals)
        → Peak Deep House (120–124 BPM, full groove with all elements)
        → Bridge to Afro/Soulful (122–124 BPM, percussion building)
        → Cool-down (back to 118–120 BPM, stripping layers)
```

### Mixing Techniques
- **Transition style**: Long blends (16–32 bars) — use bass swap technique
- **EQ mixing**: Low-frequency swap (cut bass of outgoing, bring in new bass)
- **Key matching**: Recommended — jazz chords clash audibly when keys conflict
- **Beatmatching**: Four-on-the-floor alignment is straightforward
- **Tempo adjustment**: ±3 BPM is safe; beyond ±5 BPM groove character changes
- **Looping**: Loop instrumental sections for extended transition time
- **FX**: Reverb throws, filter sweeps (subtle), echo for transitions

### Energy Management
- Deep house sets maintain a NARROW energy band (4–7)
- The groove IS the peak — no dramatic drops or builds
- Transitioning OUT: gradually increase tempo/energy toward tech or afro house
- Abrupt genre switches feel jarring (avoid tech house → deep house hard cuts)
- Build energy through layers, not tempo

### Harmonic Mixing Notes
- Most deep house is in minor keys (flat keys: Eb, Ab, Bb minor common)
- Jazz voicings mean tracks touch multiple tonal centers
- Safe key relationships: relative major/minor, perfect 4th/5th shifts
- Avoid tritone relationships (dissonant with jazz chords)
- Camelot wheel: stay within ±1 position

## Tech House

### Compatible Genres for Mixing
| Genre | Compatibility | Notes |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| Minimal Techno | High | Shared stripped-back groove, similar BPM |
| Jackin House | High | Raw, groove-focused, same tempo |
| Deep House | Medium-High | Warmer companion, bridge with groovy selections |
| Bass House | Medium-High | Shared energy, bass house is harder/heavier |
| Deep Tech | High | Groove-focused cousin |
| Techno (soft) | Medium | Bridge from tech house to darker territory |
| Progressive House | Medium | Longer arcs, more melodic |
| Funky House | Medium | Shared energy level, different character |
| Electro House | Low-Medium | Much harder/louder — needs careful selection |

### Set Progression Guidance
```
Opening → Deep/Groovy Tech House (122–124 BPM, subtle)
        → Standard Tech House (124–126 BPM, groove established)
        → Peak Tech House (126–128 BPM, vocal hooks, maximum groove)
        → Harder Tech House (127–128 BPM, more percussive)
        → Bridge to Techno (128–130 BPM, stripped back, darker)
        → Or Bridge to Bass House (126–128 BPM, heavier bass)
```

### Mixing Techniques
- **Transition style**: Short-medium blends (8–16 bars)
- **EQ mixing**: Bass swap (standard), hi-pass incoming for 8 bars
- **Key matching**: Less critical (minimal harmonic content)
- **Beatmatching**: Straightforward 4/4, watch percussion alignment
- **Tempo adjustment**: ±2 BPM safe; tech house is tight tempo range
- **Looping**: 8–16 bar loops on groove sections for mix time
- **FX**: Filter sweeps, echo out, reverb throws — quick and functional

### Energy Management
- Tech house energy is moderate-high and consistent (5–8)
- Build through percussion density and vocal hook addition
- Strip back to kick + hat for "reset" points
- Short breaks (2–4 bars) then immediate return
- Multiple peaks per set, not one big arc

### Harmonic Mixing Notes
- Many tracks have minimal harmonic content — key matching less critical
- Watch vocal chop pitches during blends
- Bass notes should be compatible (same root or harmonically related)
- Safe to blend during percussion-only sections (no harmonic conflict)

## Progressive House

### Compatible Genres for Mixing
| Genre | Compatibility | Notes |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| Melodic Techno | High | Same long-form structure, shared aesthetic (Anjunadeep/Innervisions) |
| Deep House | Medium-High | Warmer, slower — good for warming up |
| Afro Tech | Medium | Shared hypnotic quality, different percussion |
| Trance (progressive) | Medium | Shared build/release structure |
| Deep Tech | Medium | Groove-focused bridge |
| Minimal Techno | Medium | Shared long-form mixing approach |
| Ambient/Downtempo | Low-Medium | Opening/closing — tempo gap requires bridging |

### Set Progression Guidance
```
Opening → Deep Progressive (120–122 BPM, atmospheric, spacious)
        → Progressive House (122–124 BPM, arpeggios emerging)
        → Building Progressive (124–126 BPM, layers growing)
        → Peak Progressive (126–128 BPM, full emotional arc)
        → Bridge to Melodic Techno (128–130 BPM, darker)
        → Extended closure (126–124 BPM, atmospheric wind-down)
```

### Mixing Techniques
- **Transition style**: LONG blends (32–64 bars) — genre standard
- **EQ mixing**: Gradual bass swap over 16–32 bars
- **Key matching**: Important — melodic content is prominent
- **Beatmatching**: Simple 4/4, focus on phrase alignment
- **Tempo adjustment**: ±1–2 BPM ideal (long sets allow gradual tempo rides)
- **Looping**: Loop atmospheric sections for unlimited blend time
- **FX**: Reverb washes, filter sweeps (long, dramatic), delay throws

### Energy Management
- Energy builds over 2–4 hour sets (not per track)
- Tracks are chapters in a longer journey
- Never peak too early — slow build philosophy
- Breakdowns of adjacent tracks should NOT overlap (one at a time)
- Match breakdown intensity between outgoing and incoming

### Harmonic Mixing Notes
- Key matching is ESSENTIAL (melodic content is prominent)
- Camelot wheel: stay within ±1–2 positions
- Use breakdowns (less harmonic content) as blend points
- Long blends mean key clashes are more audible
- Plan key journeys across the set

- Build through bigger vocal hooks and fuller arrangements
- Disco breakdowns provide brief respite
- Track selection variety: different samples/hooks = variety without energy drop

### Harmonic Mixing Notes
- Vocal hooks make key matching important
- Disco samples often in major keys (Eb, Ab, Bb, F major)

### Compatible Genres for Mixing
| Genre | Compatibility | Notes |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| Speed Garage | High | Same scene, similar tempo, more aggressive bass |
| UK Funky | High | Shared UK bass DNA, similar tempo |
| 2-Step | High | Sub-style of UKG — same genre really |
| Future Garage | Medium-High | Shared DNA but different tempo feel |
| Bassline/4x4 Garage | High | Same roots, four-on-the-floor variant |
| Grime (instrumental) | Medium | Evolved from UKG, different energy |
| Deep House (UK) | Medium | Bridge via soulful garage selections |
| Dubstep (early) | Low-Medium | Shared roots but tempo gap |

### Set Progression Guidance
```
Opening → Deep 2-Step (128–130 BPM, sophisticated, Todd Edwards style)
        → Classic UKG (130–132 BPM, shuffled, vocal)
        → Peak UKG (132–134 BPM, chopped vocals, massive sub)
        → Darker garage (134 BPM, El-B style, stripped)
        → UK Funky bridge (130 BPM, tribal percussion entering)
        → Or speed garage (134–136 BPM, harder)
```

### Mixing Techniques
- **Transition style**: Quick blends (4–8 bars) or sharp cuts
- **EQ mixing**: SUB MANAGEMENT — swap sub bass frequencies carefully
- **Key matching**: Important (pitched vocals and sub melodies)
- **Beatmatching**: COMPLEX — 2-step makes alignment harder than 4/4
- **Tempo adjustment**: ±2 BPM (groove is sensitive to tempo)
- **Special technique**: Find moments where both tracks align rhythmically (not every beat)
- **FX**: Reverb throws, quick filters, sub drop cuts

### Energy Management
- Energy through bass weight and vocal intensity
- Sub bass entry = energy lift
- Stripped sections = brief respite before bass return
- Track selection variety: different vocal hooks, different bass character
- Crowd energy through rewinds and familiar vocal hooks

### Harmonic Mixing Notes
- Sub bass melodies make key matching important
- Pitched-up vocals have specific keys — check compatibility
- Safe zones: percussion-only sections for blending
- Reese bass stabs have tonal content — watch for clashes

- Anthemic hooks in specific keys — check Camelot compatibility
- Breakdowns (atmospheric) are easiest blend points

### Set Progression Guidance
```
Opening → G-House (122–124 BPM, dark grooves, attitude)
        → Bass House (124–126 BPM, wobble bass entering)
        → Peak Bass House (126–128 BPM, maximum wobble drops)
        → Heavier Bass House (128–130 BPM, aggressive)
        → Bridge to Tech House (126–128 BPM, cooling slightly)
        → Or bridge to UK Bass (128–130 BPM, different character)
```

### Mixing Techniques
- **Transition style**: Drop-to-drop mixing or short blends (4–8 bars)
- **EQ mixing**: BASS MANAGEMENT — swap sub carefully (two wobble basses = mud)
- **Key matching**: Less critical (bass is often atonal/noise-like)
- **Beatmatching**: Simple 4/4
- **Tempo adjustment**: ±2 BPM
- **Special technique**: Cut bass of outgoing completely before incoming drop hits
- **FX**: Filters (dramatic), reverb washes, stutter effects

### Energy Management
- High energy on drops, lower in breaks (contrast IS the genre)
- Build through longer breaks before bigger drops
- Variety through different bass sounds (not just louder/bigger)
- Peak moments: heaviest drops with most complex bass design
- Rest points: G-house grooves between heavy drops

### Harmonic Mixing Notes
- Bass is often atonal (wobble/noise) — key matching less critical
- Watch for tonal bass riffs that have clear pitches
- Vocal samples may have key — check compatibility
- Breaks/builds with melodic content need key awareness

### Compatible Genres for Mixing
| Genre | Compatibility | Notes |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| Chicago House | High | Parent genre |
| Juke/Footwork | High | Direct descendant |
| Jackin House (hard) | Medium-High | Shared rawness, slightly slower |
| Booty Bass/Miami Bass | Medium | Shared explicit vocal culture |
| Baltimore Club | Medium | Similar energy and vocal approach |
| Jersey Club | Medium | Short, looped, vocal-chop driven |
| Acid House (hard) | Medium | Same machines, harder territory |
| Tech House | Low-Medium | Very different aesthetic despite shared BPM |

### Set Progression Guidance
```
Opening → Jackin House (124–126 BPM, raw grooves)
        → Chicago House (124–125 BPM, classic jack)
        → Ghetto House (125–128 BPM, explicit vocals entering)
        → Peak Ghetto (128–132 BPM, fast, aggressive, maximum vocal density)
        → Faster Ghetto (132–135 BPM, approaching footwork territory)
        → Bridge to Footwork/Juke (140+ BPM) or wind down
```

### Mixing Techniques
- **Transition style**: HARD CUTS (genre standard)
- **Quick swap**: Kill outgoing, slam incoming on the 1
- **Echo out**: Delay/echo fills gap during cut
- **Loop and cut**: Loop 1 bar of incoming, cut outgoing, release
- **Tempo adjustment**: Tracks must match — no slow rides
- **Backspin**: Quick backspin on outgoing adds energy
- **Special**: Play partial tracks (30–60 seconds) then switch

### Energy Management
- HIGH ENERGY throughout (7–9) — no warm-up philosophy
- Track selection variety (different vocal hooks) provides movement
- Faster tempo = more intensity (speed up through the set)
- Brief drum-only breaks give crowd a breath
- Rewinds when crowd reacts to specific vocal hook

### Harmonic Mixing Notes
- IRRELEVANT — genre has no harmonic content
- Focus on BPM matching and energy matching
- Vocal pitch: brief enough that clashes don't matter during hard cuts
- 808 bass note: match roots when possible (same note or octave)

```
Opening → Deep Filtered House (118–120 BPM, subtle filter work)
        → French Touch (120–122 BPM, disco samples, filter sweeps)
        → Classic French Touch (122–124 BPM, pumping sidechain, open filters)
        → Peak French Touch (124–126 BPM, maximum pump, filter fully open)
        → French Electro bridge (126–128 BPM, Justice/Ed Banger territory)
        → Cool-down (122–120 BPM, filter closing back down)
```

### Mixing Techniques
- **Transition style**: FILTER-BASED — close filter on outgoing, open on incoming
- **EQ mixing**: Standard bass swap plus filter manipulation
- **Key matching**: Important (disco samples have clear harmonic content)
- **Beatmatching**: Simple 4/4, watch for pumping alignment
- **Tempo adjustment**: ±2–3 BPM
- **Special technique**: Close outgoing filter while opening incoming → smooth hand-off
- **FX**: Phaser, filter sweep (genre-native), reverb washes

### Energy Management
- Filter position = energy level (closed = low, open = high)
- The filter sweep IS the build/drop mechanism
- Maximum pump + open filter = peak energy
- Contrast between filtered (muted) and open (bright) sections
- Set structure mirrors individual track structure

### Harmonic Mixing Notes
- Disco samples have clear keys — matching is important
- Most French Touch in flat keys (disco heritage: Eb, Ab, Bb)
- Filter closing obscures key — safe zone for blending
- When filters are open, key clashes are very audible
- Plan key: blend during filtered sections

## Lo-Fi House / Outsider House

### Compatible Genres for Mixing
| Genre | Compatibility | Notes |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| Deep House | High | Same groove DNA, lo-fi is "degraded deep house" |
| Minimal House | Medium-High | Shared stripped-back approach |
| Chicago House (raw) | Medium-High | Shared rawness and warmth |
| Soulful House (deeper) | Medium | Warmer, fuller — bridge carefully |
| Ambient House | Medium | Textural overlap at low energies |
| Outsider Techno | Medium-High | Same L.I.E.S./DIY scene |
| Dub Techno | Medium | Shared spaciousness and texture |
| Amapiano (slower) | Medium | Similar warmth and tempo |

### Set Progression Guidance
```
Opening → Ambient/Textural (110–114 BPM, atmospheric lo-fi)
        → Lo-Fi House (116–118 BPM, establishing warm groove)
        → Deeper Lo-Fi (118–120 BPM, more groove-focused)
        → Peak Lo-Fi (120–122 BPM, full groove with texture)
        → Deeper/Outsider (120–124 BPM, experimental)
        → Cool-down (118–116 BPM, returning to ambient)
```

### Mixing Techniques
- **Transition style**: Long blends (16–32 bars) — let textures merge
- **EQ mixing**: Gentle bass swap (don't be surgical — genre is warm)
- **Key matching**: Less critical (filtered content, obscured harmony)
- **Beatmatching**: Approximate OK (unquantized nature means alignment is looser)
- **Tempo adjustment**: ±3 BPM acceptable (groove is flexible)
- **Special technique**: Let vinyl noise/crackle layers blend together
- **FX**: Minimal — genre's own texture is the effect

### Energy Management
- LOW energy throughout (3–6) — intimate, warm
- Build through groove density, not volume/intensity
- Never aggressive or "peak" in the traditional sense
- Warmth IS the experience — cozy, late-night listening
- DJ sets are background-level (not commanding attention)

### Harmonic Mixing Notes
- Filtered/degraded content obscures keys — less critical
- Warm blends are forgiving (everything sounds compatible when muffled)
- Bass notes should be in same key range (don't need perfect matching)
- Lo-fi processing masks minor key conflicts

### Compatible Genres for Mixing

| Lo-Fi House (deep) | Medium | Shared warmth and texture |
| Ambient (dark) | Medium | Opening/closing material |
| Trip-Hop | Medium | Shared melancholia, different era |

### Set Progression Guidance
```
Opening → Dark Ambient (no tempo, atmospheric)
        → Future Garage intro (128–130 BPM, sparse, atmospheric)
        → Future Garage (130–134 BPM, shuffled grooves emerging)
        → Peak Future Garage (134–136 BPM, full emotional groove)
        → Deeper/Darker (134–138 BPM, more abstract)
        → Ambient close (no tempo, atmospheric wind-down)
```

### Mixing Techniques
- **Transition style**: Long, atmospheric blends (32–64 bars)
- **EQ mixing**: Gradual, reverb-tail management
- **Key matching**: Important (melodic/emotional content is prominent)
- **Beatmatching**: DIFFICULT — unquantized drums resist sync
- **Tempo adjustment**: ±2 BPM (feel changes noticeably)
- **Special technique**: Blend during ambient/atmospheric sections (no rhythm = easier)
- **FX**: Reverb washes (long), filter sweeps (subtle), delay throws

### Energy Management
- VERY LOW energy throughout (3–6) — genre is melancholic, intimate
- Peak moments are emotional, not energetic
- Space and silence are expressive tools
- Sets are LISTENING experiences (not dance-focused)
- Headphone/speaker listening context (not club)

### Harmonic Mixing Notes
- Key matching ESSENTIAL (melodic content is emotional core)
- Pitched vocal chops have specific keys — must be compatible
- Reese bass has tonal content — check compatibility
- Long blends mean any clash is prolonged and painful
- Use ambient/atonal sections as blend points
- Camelot wheel: stay within ±1 position
