# Artists, Labels & Historical Context

## Deep House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: Chicago, USA, mid-1980s
- **Cultural roots**: Extension of Chicago house with jazz, soul, and funk influences; slower, warmer, more musical than the raw jack-tracks
- **Key moment**: Larry Heard's "Can You Feel It" (1986) established the blueprint — atmospheric, melodic, emotional
- **Scene evolution**: Spread to NYC (Kerri Chandler, Masters at Work), UK (90s garage-house crossover), then globally. 2010s saw deep house become mainstream pop-adjacent before returning underground
- **Modern era**: Post-2015, deep house split into commercial (Disclosure-influenced) and underground (Moda Black, Anjunadeep)

### Key Artists
| Artist | Role | Notable For |
|--------|------|-------------|
| Larry Heard (Mr. Fingers) | Producer | Invented deep house — "Can You Feel It," "Mystery of Love" |
| Kerri Chandler | DJ/Producer | NYC deep house royalty, Madhouse Records |
| Maya Jane Coles | Producer/DJ | Modern deep house, blending techno and house |
| Jimpster | Producer | Freerange Records, melodic deep house |
| Atjazz | Producer | UK soulful deep house, prolific remixer |

### Essential Tracks
1. **Larry Heard** — "Can You Feel It" (1986, the genesis of deep house)
2. **Kerri Chandler** — "Rain" (NYC deep house perfection)
3. **Maya Jane Coles** — "What They Say" (2011, modern crossover)
4. **Jimpster** — "Can't Stop Loving" (melodic deep house)
5. **Moodymann** — "Shades of Jae" (Detroit deep house, soulful and raw)

### Foundational Labels
- **Strictly Rhythm** — NYC, early deep house
- **Cajual Records** — Chicago, Green Velvet's label
- **Large Music** — Kerri Chandler's imprint
- **Freerange Records** — Jimpster's UK label
- **Madhouse Records** — Kerri Chandler's foundational label

## Tech House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: UK and Europe, early-to-mid 1990s
- **Cultural roots**: Fusion of minimal techno's stripped-back approach with house music's groove and accessibility
- **Key moment**: Mr. C and Eddie Richards coined the term; labels like Wiggle and Tortured brought the sound to London clubs
- **Scene evolution**: 2000s saw it become a dominant festival/Ibiza sound; 2010s–2020s it became the most commercially popular underground dance genre
- **Modern era**: Dominated by Fisher, Chris Lake, James Hype — heavily vocal-sample-driven, more "big room" than original minimal tech house

### Key Artists
| Artist | Role | Notable For |
|--------|------|-------------|
| Green Velvet | Producer/DJ | Chicago acid-tech house crossover, Cajual/Relief Records |
| Jamie Jones | DJ/Producer | Hot Creations, Ibiza tech house era |
| Patrick Topping | Producer/DJ | Melodic percussive tech house, Hot Creations |
| Fisher | Producer/DJ | Mainstream tech house crossover, "Losing It" |
| Solardo | Production duo | UK tech house, Sola Records |

### Essential Tracks
1. **Green Velvet** — "Flash" (acid-tech house classic)
2. **Fisher** — "Losing It" (2018, mainstream tech house anthem)
3. **Patrick Topping** — "Forget" (melodic tech house)
4. **Jamie Jones** — "My Paradise" (Ibiza-era defining track)
5. **CamelPhat** — "Cola" (2017, crossover smash)

### Foundational Labels
- **Hot Creations** — Jamie Jones, defined modern tech house
- **Toolroom Records** — Mark Knight, polished UK tech house
- **Sola Records** — Solardo's label
- **Cajual / Relief Records** — Green Velvet's Chicago imprints
- **elrow Music** — party-brand-turned-label

## Progressive House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: UK, early 1990s
- **Cultural roots**: Reaction to the intensity of hardcore rave — longer, more atmospheric, building over time rather than dropping immediately
- **Key moment**: Sasha & Digweed's Renaissance mixes (1994), Leftfield's "Leftism" album
- **Scene evolution**: Late 90s–2000s peak (Sasha, Digweed, Hernan Cattaneo); 2010s split into "big room progressive" (deadmau5/Eric Prydz) and underground (Anjunadeep, Sudbeat)
- **Modern era**: Hernan Cattaneo, Guy J, and Anjunadeep maintain the classic sound; Eric Prydz bridges to mainstream

### Key Artists
| Artist | Role | Notable For |
|--------|------|-------------|
| Sasha | DJ/Producer | Pioneered the genre, Renaissance compilations |
| John Digweed | DJ | Bedrock Records, marathon DJ sets |
| Hernan Cattaneo | DJ/Producer | South American progressive house institution |
| Eric Prydz | Producer | Bridged progressive and big room, "Opus" |
| Guy J | Producer | Modern underground progressive, Lost & Found label |

### Essential Tracks
1. **Sasha** — "Xpander" (1999, defining progressive house production)
2. **Eric Prydz** — "Opus" (2016, 9-minute progressive epic)
3. **Leftfield** — "Not Forgotten" (early UK progressive blueprint)
4. **Hernan Cattaneo & Soundexile** — "Telekinesis" (modern progressive)
5. **Sasha & Digweed** — "Northern Exposure" mix compilations (genre definition)

### Foundational Labels
- **Bedrock Records** — John Digweed
- **Anjunadeep** — Modern progressive house and melodic
- **Sudbeat** — Hernan Cattaneo's label
- **Last Night on Earth** — Sasha's label
- **Lost & Found** — Guy J's imprint

## Funky House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: UK and USA, mid-to-late 1990s
- **Cultural roots**: Disco, boogie, and funk filtered through house music production; heavily sample-based
- **Key moment**: Late 90s UK club scene (Ministry of Sound, Hed Kandi); DJs like Roger Sanchez and Armand Van Helden
- **Scene evolution**: Peaked in late 90s–mid 2000s as a club/commercial sound; declined as minimal and tech house rose
- **Modern era**: Elements live on in disco-house and nu-disco; Purple Disco Machine carries the torch

### Key Artists

## Afro House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: South Africa and West Africa, 2000s–2010s
- **Cultural roots**: Fusion of traditional African percussion, spirituality, and deep house structures; heavily connected to South African house scene
- **Key moment**: Black Coffee's rise in the 2010s bridged African house to global stages; Innervisions and Watergate bookings
- **Scene evolution**: From South African underground to international festival mainstages; Ibiza residencies for Black Coffee, Shimza

## Jackin House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: Chicago, USA, early 1990s (rooted in early Chicago house)
- **Cultural roots**: Direct descendant of the original Chicago "jacking" sound — raw, sample-heavy, dance-focused
- **Key moment**: DJ Sneak and Gene Farris kept the raw Chicago sound alive through the 90s while house evolved elsewhere
- **Scene evolution**: Always present in Chicago/US underground; resurgence in 2010s–2020s as DJs sought rawer alternatives to polished tech house
- **Modern era**: Labels like Snatch! It, Cajual, and Relief continue; artists like Gene Farris, DJ Sneak, and Todd Terry remain active

## Soulful House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: NYC and New Jersey, late 1980s
- **Cultural roots**: Gospel, R&B, soul music merged with house production; direct link to the Paradise Garage sound and Larry Levan
- **Key moment**: Masters at Work (Louie Vega & Kenny Dope) defined the NY soulful sound; Frankie Knuckles brought Chicago soul to house
- **Scene evolution**: Consistent underground presence since the late 80s; never fully "mainstream" but never disappeared
- **Modern era**: Louie Vega still active; labels like Nervous, King Street continue the tradition

## UK Garage

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: London, UK, mid-1990s
- **Cultural roots**: Evolved from US garage house (Todd Edwards, Masters at Work) sped up and given a distinctly British rhythmic sensibility; pirate radio culture
- **Key moment**: MJ Cole "Sincere" (1998), Artful Dodger "Re-Rewind" (1999) — broke UK Garage into the mainstream charts
- **Scene evolution**: Peaked commercially 1997–2001; spawned 2-step, speed garage, grime, and dubstep as it fragmented
- **Modern era**: Periodic revivals (Disclosure, Conducta, El-B); influenced bass music and UK dance culture permanently

### Key Artists

## Electro House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: Europe (France, Netherlands, Germany), early 2000s
- **Cultural roots**: Electro, tech house, and rave combined with aggressive synth sounds and bigger drops
- **Key moment**: Benny Benassi "Satisfaction" (2002) exploded the sound; Justice "Cross" album (2007) brought it to indie audiences
- **Scene evolution**: Dominated 2005–2013 festival scene (Deadmau5, Feed Me, Wolfgang Gartner); evolved into "big room" and "future house"
- **Modern era**: Less prominent as a standalone genre; elements absorbed into bass house, future house, and mainstream EDM

### Key Artists

| Wolfgang Gartner | Producer | Complextro style, intricate sound design |

### Foundational Labels

## Bass House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: UK and France (G-house from Lyon, France), early 2010s
- **Cultural roots**: Fusion of UK bass culture (dubstep, garage), tech house grooves, and heavy bass sound design
- **Key moment**: Tchami's "Promesses" (2013) defined "future house"; Jauz and AC Slater brought the heavier bass-house sound; Chris Lorenzo pioneered UK bass house
- **Scene evolution**: Grew from UK bass culture and French G-house; became a dominant festival genre by 2018–2020
- **Modern era**: Chris Lake, Fisher, Skrillex crossover; Night Bass and Confession labels drive the scene

### Key Artists

## Chicago House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: Chicago, USA, 1984–1986
- **Cultural roots**: Disco, Philly soul, Italo disco, electronic experimentation; born in Black and Latino LGBTQ+ clubs (The Warehouse, The Power Plant)
- **Key moment**: Frankie Knuckles at The Warehouse (house gets its name); Jesse Saunders "On and On" (1984, first house record); Trax Records and DJ International founded
- **Scene evolution**: Defined all of dance music; spawned acid house, deep house, and spread to UK/Europe by 1987–88
- **Modern era**: Historical influence remains enormous; artists like The Black Madonna (The Blessed Madonna), Honey Dijon carry the lineage

### Key Artists
| Artist | Role | Notable For |

| Frankie Knuckles | DJ | "Godfather of House," The Warehouse residency |
| Marshall Jefferson | Producer | "Move Your Body" (1986), first piano house |
| Larry Heard | Producer | "Can You Feel It," atmospheric depth |
| Ron Hardy | DJ | Muzic Box residency, raw jacking sets |
| Adonis | Producer | "No Way Back" (1986), dark raw Chicago house |

### Essential Tracks
1. **Marshall Jefferson** — "Move Your Body" (1986, the "house music anthem")
2. **Frankie Knuckles** — "Your Love" (foundational Chicago house)
3. **Adonis** — "No Way Back" (1986, dark jack-track)
4. **Mr. Fingers** — "Can You Feel It" (1986, atmospheric deep Chicago)
5. **Chip E.** — "Time to Jack" (1985, raw jacking groove)

### Foundational Labels

- **Dance Mania** — Chicago ghetto/jack-house
- **Cajual Records** — Green Velvet, Chicago lineage

## Ghetto House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: Chicago, USA, early 1990s
- **Cultural roots**: Direct acceleration of Chicago house; stripped down to raw 808 patterns and explicit, repetitive vocals; linked to juke/footwork evolution
- **Key moment**: DJ Funk, DJ Deeon, and Dance Mania Records defined the sound; Paul Johnson bridged ghetto house to mainstream with "Get Get Down" (1999)
- **Scene evolution**: Remained underground and Chicago-centric; evolved into footwork/juke by the mid-2000s (RP Boo, DJ Rashad)
- **Modern era**: Historical genre with ongoing influence; footwork is its direct descendant

### Key Artists
| Artist | Role | Notable For |
|--------|------|-------------|
| DJ Funk | Producer/DJ | Ghetto house pioneer, explicit vocals |
| DJ Deeon | Producer | "Let Me Bang," Dance Mania catalog |
| Paul Johnson | Producer | "Get Get Down," bridged ghetto house to mainstream |
| DJ Slugo | Producer | Raw 808 ghetto tracks |
| Traxman | Producer | Ghetto house to footwork bridge |

### Essential Tracks

### Foundational Labels

## Gqom

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: Durban, South Africa, early 2010s
- **Cultural roots**: Township party music; raw, dark, minimalist — from kwaito and local DJ culture in Durban's informal settlements
- **Key moment**: Nan (DJ Lag), Rudeboyz, and Citizen Boy uploaded tracks to file-sharing networks; Gqom Oh! label (Italy) gave it international visibility in 2016
- **Scene evolution**: From Durban taxi rank music to European festival stages; Beyoncé sampled DJ Lag on "The Gift" (2020)
- **Modern era**: DJ Lag is the global ambassador; genre remains rooted in Durban while gaining international interest

## French Touch

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: Paris, France, mid-1990s
- **Cultural roots**: Filtered disco, Italo house, hip-hop sampling culture; French producers applied hip-hop sampling techniques to disco records
- **Key moment**: Daft Punk "Homework" (1997), Cassius "1999" (1999), and Stardust "Music Sounds Better with You" (1998) defined the sound globally
- **Scene evolution**: Peaked 1996–2002 as a global phenomenon; Ed Banger Records (Justice, Breakbot) carried a harder second wave 2005–2010

## Lo-Fi House / Outsider House

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: Internet / global DIY scene, 2014–2017
- **Cultural roots**: Reaction against polished, commercial tech house; inspired by lo-fi hip-hop aesthetics, tape culture, and 90s deep house nostalgia
- **Key moment**: DJ Seinfeld "Time Spent Away from U" (2017) and Ross From Friends brought the sound to wider attention; L.I.E.S. Records pioneered the "outsider house" concept
- **Scene evolution**: Started on Bandcamp/SoundCloud; moved to vinyl-only releases; broke through to mainstream dance press by 2017
- **Modern era**: Post-hype, the aesthetic has been absorbed into broader house production; Mall Grab, DJ Seinfeld now make cleaner productions

### Key Artists
| Artist | Role | Notable For |
|--------|------|-------------|
| DJ Seinfeld | Producer | "Time Spent Away from U," emotional lo-fi house |
| Ross From Friends | Producer | "Talk to Me You'll Understand," warped textures |
| Mall Grab | Producer/DJ | Steel City Dance Discs, raw DJ tools |
| Ron Morelli | Label head/Producer | L.I.E.S. Records, outsider house |
| Palms Trax | Producer/DJ | Warm, sample-heavy lo-fi house |

### Essential Tracks
1. **DJ Seinfeld** — "U" (2016, emotional lo-fi house anthem)

## Future Garage

### Historical Context
- **Origin**: UK, 2010–2012
- **Cultural roots**: UKG/2-step rhythms slowed down, filtered through post-dubstep atmospherics and ambient production techniques
- **Key moment**: Burial's "Untrue" (2007) was the catalyst (though Burial himself predates the "future garage" label); Mount Kimbie and others followed
- **Scene evolution**: Brief but influential movement; artists moved toward broader experimental electronic music; Burial remains a singular influence
- **Modern era**: Elements absorbed into ambient, UK bass, and experimental electronic; Burial continues releasing sporadically

### Key Artists
| Artist | Role | Notable For |
|--------|------|-------------|
| Burial | Producer | "Untrue," defined the aesthetic — vinyl crackle, chopped vocals, rain |
| Mount Kimbie | Production duo | "Crooks & Lovers," textural future garage |
